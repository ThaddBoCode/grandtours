Stories

The big stories of the Tour and the story of every stage – updated continuously.

Headlines

★2026

Pogačar wins his fifth Tour, equalling the record

Tadej Pogačar won the Tour for the fifth time and the third year in a row, joining Jacques Anquetil, Eddy Merckx, Bernard Hinault and Miguel Induráin as the only five-time winners. He won five stages and finished 6:26 ahead of Remco Evenepoel.

🏆Stage 6

Pogačar's solo on the Tourmalet puts him in yellow

On stage 6 Pogačar went clear alone on the Col du Tourmalet, 43 km from the finish, and won at Gavarnie-Gèdre by 2:38 over Jonas Vingegaard – his largest stage-winning margin at the Tour. He kept the yellow jersey all the way to Paris, extending his lead with further wins at Le Lioran, Le Markstein and Alpe d'Huez.

⚠️Stage 15

Vingegaard crashes out with a broken collarbone

Jonas Vingegaard, the Giro d'Italia winner and second overall at the time, hit the kerb exiting a roundabout about 24 km from the finish of stage 15, broke his collarbone and abandoned. He had been woken around 2 am for a doping test authorised by a Paris prosecutor; the night-time tests on him and Pogačar sparked debate, and Pogačar suggested the lost sleep may have contributed to the crash.

✨Stage 20

Debutants del Toro and Seixas fight for the podium

Two Tour debutants fought over third place: Isaac del Toro finished third overall and won the white jersey, with Pogačar riding for him on stage 20. Paul Seixas, 19 and riding his first Grand Tour, finished fourth – with Lenny Martinez fifth, two French riders made the top five for the first time since 2014.

🌧️2026

Heatwave and wildfires: two stages shortened

A heatwave shaped the race: the UCI relaxed the rules on handing up water, stage 9 to Ussel was shortened by 30.9 km because of a red heat warning in Corrèze, and wildfires meant no spectators at the stage 3 finish. The final stage was cut by 43.8 km and started on the Champs-Élysées because security staff were redeployed to wildfires in Gironde.

⚡2026

Pedersen wins green with a single stage win

Mads Pedersen won the points classification with 559 points despite only one stage win (stage 4), thanks to consistent intermediate-sprint points – including from breakaways in the Alps after Jasper Philipsen had closed to seven points on stage 17. He has now won the points classification in all three Grand Tours.

•2026

Carapaz takes the polka-dot jersey and combativity prize

Richard Carapaz won two Alpine stages, at Orcières-Merlette and Alpe d'Huez, took the mountains lead from Pogačar on stage 19 after a duel with Valentin Paret-Peintre, and was named the most combative rider of the race.

🔥Stage 21

Van der Poel wins on the Champs-Élysées in a photo finish

On the shortened final stage, van der Poel and Pogačar attacked on the last climb of Montmartre; van der Poel held off the chasing sprinters to win a photo finish ahead of teammate Jasper Philipsen and Pedersen – his second stage win of the race after Ussel.

⚠️Stage 16

Lipowitz crashes out in the time trial

Florian Lipowitz crashed on a descent in the stage 16 time trial to Thonon-les-Bains, broke his collarbone and had to abandon. The stage went to Remco Evenepoel, a day after his first road-stage win at the Tour at Solaison; he went on to finish second overall.

•Stage 1

Barcelona start with a new-format team time trial

The Tour started in Barcelona with a team time trial – the first to open the race since 1971 – in which each rider received his own time for the general classification. Visma–Lease a Bike won and Vingegaard took the first yellow jersey; the first two stages both finished on Montjuïc.

Stage by stage

1 Sat 4 Jul · Barcelona ↻ · Review Visma win the team time trial, Vingegaard takes yellow For the first time since 1971 the Tour opened with a team time trial, under a new format in which each rider received his own time. Visma–Lease a Bike were 8 seconds faster than Netcompany INEOS and 12 faster than UAE Team Emirates XRG, with Jonas Vingegaard attacking the final climb to Montjuïc to take his first yellow jersey since 2023. Egan Bernal and Tadej Pogačar received the green and polka-dot jerseys for the fastest times on the opening and closing sections. 2 Sun 5 Jul · Tarragona → Barcelona · Review Del Toro wins on Montjuïc with Pogačar's help UAE Team Emirates XRG whittled down the peloton with a fast pace on three laps of the Montjuïc circuit, and in the uphill sprint Tadej Pogačar made sure his teammate Isaac del Toro won – the 22-year-old's first Tour stage victory. Remco Evenepoel was third ahead of Vingegaard, who kept yellow but saw his lead over Pogačar cut to 6 seconds. Del Toro also took the green jersey and kept the white one. 3 Mon 6 Jul · Granollers → Les Angles · Review Pogačar wins at Les Angles and takes yellow Because of wildfires in the Pyrénées-Orientales, spectators were discouraged from attending in France and there were none at the finish. On the final climb del Toro attacked with 300 metres to go to set up Pogačar, who won 2 seconds ahead of Vingegaard, Richard Carapaz and Paul Seixas. Pogačar took the yellow jersey from Vingegaard, level on time, while Alex Baudin took the polka-dot jersey. 4 Tue 7 Jul · Carcassonne → Foix · Review Pedersen wins from the break, Træen inherits yellow In temperatures of around 40 °C, which led the UCI to relax the rules on handing up water, a breakaway of around 35 riders was allowed to go clear. Lidl–Trek controlled the attacks in the finale and Mads Pedersen won the sprint ahead of teammate Quinn Simmons and Raúl García Pierna, taking the green jersey. The peloton finished almost 13 minutes down, so Torstein Træen became the first Uno-X Mobility rider to wear yellow, 28 seconds ahead of Sean Quinn, with Pogačar fourth at 7:53. 5 Wed 8 Jul · Lannemezan → Pau · Review Kooij beats the favourites in Pau Baptiste Veistroffer spent most of the day alone at the front before being caught about 14 km from the finish. After a crash 6 km from the line, a group of around 20 riders contested the sprint, and Olav Kooij beat Max Kanter and Tim Merlier for his first Tour stage win. Træen kept the yellow jersey, while Pedersen extended his points lead by finishing seventh. 6 Thu 9 Jul · Pau → Gavarnie-Gèdre · Review Pogačar goes solo on the Tourmalet and takes yellow On the first high-mountain stage, del Toro attacked 5 km from the top of the Tourmalet and Pogačar went clear alone 43 km from the finish, crossing the summit 31 seconds ahead of Vingegaard and extending his lead on the descent. He won at Gavarnie-Gèdre by 2:38 over Vingegaard – his largest winning margin at the Tour – with the other contenders a further 19 seconds back. Pogačar took the yellow and polka-dot jerseys and del Toro the white, while yellow jersey Torstein Træen crashed on the descent and left the race with concussion and rib fractures. 7 Fri 10 Jul · Hagetmau → Bordeaux · Review Merlier pounces in Bordeaux Veistroffer was in the breakaway again, this time with Jakub Otruba, but the pair were never given more than two minutes. Alpecin–Premier Tech's lead-out for Jasper Philipsen ran out too early and Tim Merlier took advantage to win ahead of Søren Wærenskjold and Biniam Girmay. The classifications were unchanged, with Pedersen leading the points by around 60. 8 Sat 11 Jul · Périgueux → Bergerac · Review Merlier makes it two in a row in Bergerac Liam Slock, the last survivor of a three-man breakaway, held off the peloton until around the final kilometre. Merlier, still several dozen metres back with 500 m to go, had the strongest acceleration and beat Girmay, Kooij and Philipsen for his second consecutive stage win. He moved up to second in the points classification, 15 points behind Pedersen. 9 Sun 12 Jul · Malemort → Ussel · Review Van der Poel wins the shortened heatwave stage to Ussel Because of a red heatwave warning for the Corrèze department, the stage was shortened by 30.9 km and raced in temperatures of up to 40 °C. Mathieu van der Poel attacked from an eight-man breakaway on Mont Bessou, with only Tobias Halland Johannessen and Tom Pidcock able to follow and Alex Baudin joining on the descent. Van der Poel won the sprint six seconds ahead of the peloton – in the Limousin, home region of his grandfather Raymond Poulidor – and the GC was unchanged. 10 Tue 14 Jul · Aurillac → Le Lioran · Review Pogačar strikes again on Bastille Day Richard Carapaz attacked on the Pas de Peyrol, but on the Col du Pertus Pogačar attacked about 15 km from the finish, passed Carapaz before the summit and rode alone to Le Lioran for his third stage win. His lead over Vingegaard grew to 3:36, and Evenepoel's sprint for the line, 32 seconds down, lifted him to third overall. Del Toro struggled on the Pertus, dropped from third to seventh and lost the white jersey to Juan Ayuso. 11 Wed 15 Jul · Vichy → Nevers · Review Wærenskjold wins the fastest road stage in Tour history A four-man breakaway including Mathis Le Berre was kept on a short leash and caught 6 km from the finish. In a disorganised sprint, Søren Wærenskjold jumped across to Kooij's lead-out man Cees Bol and won ahead of Kooij and Philipsen – his first Grand Tour stage win. Raced at an average of 50.9 km/h, it was the fastest road stage in Tour history. 12 Thu 16 Jul · Circuit de Nevers Magny-Cours → Chalon-sur-Saône · Review Merlier's third win as a crash strikes behind him A breakaway started by Veistroffer was caught 33 km from the finish, and Lidl–Trek tried in vain to prevent a bunch sprint. Tim Merlier beat Kooij and Philipsen for his third stage win of the race, while a heavy crash about 400 metres from the line brought down several riders, including Fernando Gaviria and Dorian Godon, but none of the favourites. Pedersen kept the green jersey and the GC was unchanged. 13 Fri 17 Jul · Dole → Belfort · Review Schmid wins in Belfort, Pidcock jumps to fourth The longest stage of the race, at 205.8 km, saw a 57-rider breakaway form early on. Mauro Schmid and Harold Tejada went clear 16 km from the finish and Schmid won the sprint in Belfort for his first Tour stage win. Tom Pidcock, also in the break, gained 7:33 on the favourites and jumped from tenth to fourth overall. 14 Sat 18 Jul · Mulhouse → Le Markstein · Review Pogačar attacks on the Col du Haag for a fourth win Pogačar attacked 1.5 km from the top of the Col du Haag, the last climb, and extended his lead on the plateau to the finish at Le Markstein for his fourth stage win. Del Toro and Seixas finished 38 seconds behind and Vingegaard, who had worked with Seixas to limit the losses, 44 seconds back, leaving Pogačar 4:30 ahead overall. Seixas took the white jersey from Ayuso and moved up to fourth. 15 Sun 19 Jul · Champagnole → Plateau de Solaison · Review Vingegaard crashes out, Evenepoel wins at Solaison After night-time doping tests on Vingegaard (around 2 am) and Pogačar (around 5 am), Vingegaard hit the kerb exiting a roundabout about 24 km from the finish, broke his collarbone and abandoned; Tim Merlier also left the race. On the first-ever finish at Plateau de Solaison, Pogačar and del Toro dropped Seixas and Lipowitz, but Evenepoel stayed with them and beat Pogačar in the sprint for his first road-stage win at the Tour. Evenepoel moved up to second overall, five minutes down, and del Toro regained the white jersey. 16 Tue 21 Jul · Évian-les-Bains → Thonon-les-Bains · Review Evenepoel wins the time trial, Lipowitz crashes out Evenepoel won the race's only individual time trial, over 26.1 km, 28 seconds ahead of Pogačar and over a minute ahead of Mattias Skjelmose – his third Tour time-trial win and his second stage win in a row. Florian Lipowitz crashed on a descent, broke his collarbone and abandoned. Seixas was fourth, five seconds faster than del Toro, cutting the gap in the fight for third overall to 20 seconds; Pogačar now led Evenepoel by 4:32. 17 Wed 22 Jul · Chambéry → Voiron · Review Philipsen wins in Voiron as the green battle tightens Pedersen bridged to a large breakaway and won the intermediate sprint, after which a series of attacks followed. In the sprint of a reduced group, Mathieu van der Poel led out Jasper Philipsen, who won ahead of Mauro Schmid and Olav Kooij. Pedersen finished only eighth and his lead in the points classification shrank to seven points. 18 Thu 23 Jul · Voiron → Orcières-Merlette · Review Carapaz solos to victory at Orcières-Merlette Pedersen joined a large breakaway and took maximum points at the intermediate sprint, extending his lead over Philipsen to 32 points. Valentin Paret-Peintre chased mountain points over the climbs, but Carapaz was strongest on the final ascent, going clear 3.5 km from the line to win ahead of Mauro Schmid and Matteo Jorgenson. The favourites finished together, while illness in the UAE team forced Pogačar's teammate Brandon McNulty out of the race. 19 Fri 24 Jul · Gap → Alpe d'Huez · Review Pogačar breaks Pantani's Alpe d'Huez record Pogačar went clear alone on the climb to Alpe d'Huez, about 12 km from the finish, picked off the breakaway riders one by one, caught Carapaz and Lenny Martinez 1.7 km from the line and won solo – his fifth stage win of the race. His time beat the record for the climb set by Marco Pantani in 1995. Carapaz took the polka-dot jersey from Pogačar by one point, and del Toro stayed third overall, 24 seconds ahead of Seixas. 20 Sat 25 Jul · Le Bourg-d'Oisans → Alpe d'Huez · Review Carapaz wins the queen stage after Kuss crashes twice On the queen stage over the Croix de Fer, Télégraphe, Galibier and Sarenne, Sepp Kuss led alone over the Col de Sarenne but crashed twice in the finale, and Carapaz passed him to win at Alpe d'Huez ahead of Evenepoel and Kuss, securing the polka-dot jersey. Pogačar rode for del Toro, who dropped Seixas to defend third overall and the white jersey; Seixas lost 1:50 but held on to fourth. Pedersen made the green jersey mathematically safe at the intermediate sprint. 21 Sun 26 Jul · Paris → Paris (Champs-Élysées) · Review Van der Poel holds off the sprinters on the Champs-Élysées Shortened by 43.8 km because security staff were redeployed to wildfires in Gironde, the stage started on the Champs-Élysées and kept three climbs of Montmartre, with GC times frozen early on. Pogačar and van der Poel attacked on the last ascent and carried a slim lead into the final straight, where van der Poel left Pogačar behind and won a photo finish ahead of Philipsen and Pedersen. Pogačar finished with the bunch to seal his fifth Tour victory.
