Stories

The big stories of the Tour and the story of every stage – updated continuously.

🕐 As of 22 October 2026

The storylines before the start

★2027

Pogačar chases a sixth win – it would be a record

With five wins he is level with Anquetil, Merckx, Hinault and Induráin. A sixth would put him alone at the top of Tour history.

🏆Stage 7

71 km of time trials – the most since 2012

Two individual time trials, at Chambord and Toulouse, and no team time trial. The route is seen as a chance for Remco Evenepoel to gain time before the mountains.

🏆Stage 18

Tourmalet and Portet on the same day

The queen stage to Saint-Lary-Soulan packs 5,400 m of climbing and finishes on the Col du Portet, at 2,215 m the highest point of the race. Pogačar won there in 2021.

•Stage 1

Grand Départ in Edinburgh – the northernmost ever

The 28th start abroad and the third in Great Britain after London 2007 and Yorkshire 2014. Three stages run through Scotland, England and Wales.

🏆Stage 9

Only three summit finishes

Grand Colombier, Les Saisies and the Col du Portet – plus six mountain stages across the Jura, Alps, Massif Central and Pyrenees.

•Stage 10

Domancy: a dress rehearsal for the Worlds

The Côte de Domancy is climbed twice – the same hill will decide the World Championships in Sallanches a few weeks later.

•2027

Nine stage towns making their debut

Edinburgh, Carlisle, Keswick, Liverpool, Welshpool, Cardiff, Pontorson, Les Saisies and Hasparren host the Tour for the first time.

🏆2027

Vingegaard aims to return after his crash

The 2022 and 2023 winner broke his collarbone on stage 15 in 2026. His team still plans the Tour as his main goal.

Stage by stage

1 Fri 2 Jul · Edinburgh → Carlisle · Preview The sprinters get a golden chance at the first yellow jersey. The Scottish Borders bring the first modest climbs – the polka-dot jersey is first contested on the Côte de Melrose. Then easy terrain across the border into England and Carlisle. 2 Sat 3 Jul · Keswick → Liverpool · Preview Starts in the Lake District National Park, followed by five categorised climbs for attackers and KOM hunters. Coastal wind can split the bunch, and the run-in to Liverpool is unpredictable. 3 Sun 4 Jul · Welshpool → Cardiff · Preview Seven Welsh climbs – no day for the sprinters. The puncheurs fight for the win and possibly yellow. The favourites will have to show their hand on the steep walls before Cardiff. 4 Tue 6 Jul · Cherbourg-en-Cotentin ↻ · Preview A loop with long coastal stretches west and east of Cherbourg – exposed to the wind. The Côte du Becquet, with double-digit gradients just before the final 10 km, thins the field. Uphill finish: strong puncheur or resilient sprinter? 5 Wed 7 Jul · Pontorson → Plumelec · Preview Starts near Mont-Saint-Michel, then long straight roads through Brittany past Saint-Méen-le-Grand. Decided on the Plumelec circuit with two ascents of the Côte de Cadoudal – roughly three minutes flat out, made for puncheurs. 6 Thu 8 Jul · Vannes → Angers · Preview A long, mostly flat and sheltered stage from the Gulf of Morbihan to Anjou – finally another chance for the sprinters. A long straight 50 km out invites tactics; the finish is on a rising false flat. 7 Fri 9 Jul · Blois → Chambord · Preview First individual time trial: 38 km, more rolling than technical, through the woods, vineyards and châteaux of the Loire, past Cheverny. The last 3 km run through the grounds of Chambord. The first clear GC hierarchy before the mountains. 8 Sat 10 Jul · Bourges → Le Creusot · Preview The longest stage of the Tour: long and gruelling through the Morvan with hidden traps, including the Signal d'Uchon. In 2021 this terrain sparked fireworks – a day for breakaways and surprises. 9 Sun 11 Jul · Villars-les-Dombes Parc des Oiseaux → Grand Colombier · Preview First big mountain stage and first summit finish to close week one. After a flat start, five categorised climbs including the Col du Berthiand and Col de Richemond. The final climb starts in Culoz up to the Grand Colombier. 10 Tue 13 Jul · Annecy → Sallanches · Preview On the trail of Bernard Hinault's 1980 world title in Sallanches. After the Plan du Salève and Brison, a first pass through the finish, then a series of steep climbs. The second ascent of the Côte de Domancy is the final launchpad. 11 Wed 14 Jul · Cluses → Les Saisies · Preview Bastille Day and the last Alpine stage – with the most climbing in the Alps. The Col du Pré and Montée de Bisanne add up to 25 km at around 8% in the final 50 km. A technical descent, then the finish at Les Saisies, 4 km beyond the last summit. 12 Thu 15 Jul · Chambéry → Valence · Preview A breather after the Alps. A few rises around Voiron, then into the Rhône Valley. Watch the wind: the final 25 km are exposed, and a change of direction 10 km out could be decisive. 13 Fri 16 Jul · Aubenas → Montpellier · Preview A short stage with little climbing – the sprinters' teams will want control. The danger comes from the Mistral and Tramontane winds. New finish right in Montpellier's city centre. 14 Sat 17 Jul · Montpellier → Mende · Preview 4,300 m of climbing into the Massif Central: the Col du Vent, a first visit to the spectacular Cirque de Navacelles, the Col de la Lusette near Mont Aigoual. Finale on the steep Côte de la Croix Neuve in Mende – attacks possible anywhere. 15 Sun 18 Jul · Rodez → Albi · Preview Eight categorised climbs, constant ups and downs – a day for attackers with good legs and bike handling. The descent towards Albi ends 5 km from the line, which sits on a rising false flat. 16 Tue 20 Jul · Toulouse ↻ · Preview Second individual time trial to open the final week. About 20 km out and back along the Garonne – pure power. Then the Côte de Pech-David with sections of up to 20%, followed by an urban finale. 17 Wed 21 Jul · Muret → Pau · Preview Largely flat before the Pyrenees – a day for the sprinters. Breakaway riders may try their luck at the intermediate sprint in Castelnau-Magnoac or on the Côte de Baleix. 18 Thu 22 Jul · Pau → Saint-Lary-Soulan Col du Portet · Preview The queen stage: 5,400 m of climbing over the Soulor, Tourmalet (from Luz-Saint-Sauveur) and Hourquette d'Ancizan, then up to the Col du Portet – at 2,215 m the highest point of the Tour. Three days from Paris, yellow is on the line. 19 Fri 23 Jul · Hasparren → Laruns · Preview The last big mountain battle through the Basque Country: Burdincurutcheta (8.7 km at 9.3%, max 15%) and Bagargui, then the Soudet and Marie-Blanque before the drop into Laruns. The Tour can still turn here. 20 Sat 24 Jul · Maubourguet → Libourne · Preview Out of the Pyrenees on rolling, winding roads. The sprinters are counting on the roughly 6 km finishing straight – but after three weeks they first have to reel in the breakaway. 21 Sun 25 Jul · Saint-Quentin-en-Yvelines → Paris Champs-Élysées · Preview The ceremonial final stage through the Yvelines into Paris. Again with a detour via Rue Lepic up Montmartre – which keeps the Champs-Élysées finale open between Classics specialists and sprinters.
