Legendary places on the route

Climb · Hautes-Pyrénées

Col du Portet

2,215 m 17.8 km · 7.9 %
Stage 18

The Col de Portet above Saint-Lary-Soulan is, at about 2,215 m, one of the highest paved passes in the French Pyrenees. The Tour first used it as a summit finish in 2018.

 Climb · Hautes-Pyrénées

Col du Tourmalet

2,115 m 18.3 km · 7.7 %
Stage 18

The Tourmalet (2,115 m) is the most frequently crossed pass in the Tour de France, first climbed in 1910 with Octave Lapize leading. The Souvenir Jacques Goddet is awarded at its summit.

 Climb · Savoie

Montée de Bisanne

1,723 m 12.4 km · 8.2 %
Stage 11

The Montée de Bisanne climbs the D123 from Villard-sur-Doron to the junction below the Signal de Bisanne (1941 m), the high point of the Les Saisies resort. At 12.4 km and 8.2% it is rated hors catégorie; in 2027 it precedes the finish at Les Saisies (stage 11).

 Climb · Savoie

Col du Pré

1,703 m 12.2 km · 7.9 %
Stage 11

The Col du Pré in the Beaufortain separates Arêches-Beaufort from the Lac de Roselend; the narrow road from Beaufort is steep and irregular. The Tour has crossed it three times, each time rated hors catégorie.

 Climb · Savoie

Les Saisies

1,650 m 14.8 km · 5 %
Stage 11

The Col des Saisies links the Val d'Arly with the Beaufortain, and the ski resort of the same name, known for cross-country skiing, sits on the pass. The Tour has crossed it 14 times as a classified climb; a stage finish at Les Saisies is announced for the first time in 2027 (stage 11).

 Climb · Haute-Savoie

Col de la Colombière

1,607 m 16.3 km · 6.8 %
Stage 11

The Col de la Colombière in the Bornes massif links the Arve valley at Scionzier with Le Grand-Bornand. It has featured 23 times in the Tour since 1960, rated first category since 1978.

 Climb · Hautes-Pyrénées

Hourquette d'Ancizan

1,564 m 10.3 km · 7.8 %
Stage 18

The Hourquette d’Ancizan (1,564 m) links the Payolle valley with the Vallée d’Aure. The Tour first crossed it in 2011.

 Climb · Pyrénées-Atlantiques

Col de Soudet

1,533 m 22 km · 5.5 %
Stage 19

The Col du Soudet lies near the Spanish border below the La Pierre-Saint-Martin ski station. It links the Barétous valley with the Soule.

 Climb · Ain

Grand Colombier

1,498 m 18.3 km · 6.9 %
Stage 9

The Grand Colombier at the southern end of the Jura is considered one of the hardest climbs in France, with four ascents and ramps above 20% on the western side. The Tour first came in 2012, with summit finishes in 2020 and 2023; another is planned for 2027 (stage 9).

 Climb · Hautes-Pyrénées

Col du Soulor

1,474 m 19.5 km · 5.2 %
Stage 18

The Col du Soulor lies on the road from Argelès-Gazost to the Col d’Aubisque and is usually crossed together with it. Only some of its Tour crossings counted for the mountains classification.

 Climb · Haute-Savoie

Col de la Croix Fry

1,467 m 12.8 km · 6.6 %
Stage 11

The Col de la Croix Fry in the Aravis range links the Manigod valley with the Nom valley near Saint-Jean-de-Sixt. The Tour has crossed it six times, always rated first category.

 Climb · Pyrénées-Atlantiques

Col de Bagargui

1,330 m 13 km · 5.4 %
Stage 19

The Col Bagargui (Basque Bagargi) lies in the Iraty forest in the Basque part of the Pyrénées-Atlantiques. The Tour crossed it three times: 1986, 1987 and 2003.

 Climb · Pyrénées-Atlantiques

Col de Burdincurutcheta

1,141 m 13 km · 0 %
Stage 19

The Col de Burdincurutcheta (Basque Burdinkurutxeta, "iron cross") lies in Lower Navarre and leads into the Iraty forest. The Tour crossed it in 1986, 1987 and 2003.

 Finish · Lozère

Côte de la Croix Neuve

1,045 m 3.1 km · 10.1 %
Stage 14

The Côte de la Croix Neuve climbs steeply from Mende onto the causse to the Mende-Brenoux aerodrome, where Tour stages finish. Since 2005 it has also been called "Montée Laurent Jalabert" after his 1995 win.

 Climb · Ain

Col de Richemond

1,036 m 8.5 km · 3.9 %
Stage 9

The Col de Richemond in the Jura links the Valromey with the Genevois near Bellegarde-sur-Valserine; a memorial at the top honours resistance fighters killed in 1944. According to French Wikipedia the Tour first crossed it in 2012 on the way to Bellegarde.

 Climb · Pyrénées-Atlantiques

Col de Marie Blanque

1,035 m 9.3 km · 7.7 %
Stage 19

The Col de Marie-Blanque (1,035 m) links the Aspe and Ossau valleys. The western side from Escot is short but very steep over its final four kilometres.

 Climb · Haute-Savoie

Côte de Domancy

810 m 2.5 km · 9.4 %
Stage 10

The short, steep Côte de Domancy above Sallanches is famous from the 1980 World Road Championships, where Bernard Hinault built his winning move here; the road is now called 'Route Bernard Hinault'. In the Tour it featured in two mountain time trials (2016, 2023) and as a classified climb on an Alpine stage in 2021.

 Finish · Morbihan

Côte de Cadoudal

156 m 1.7 km · 6.2 %
Stage 5

The Côte de Cadoudal is the 1.7 km, 6.2% rise from the Manoir de Cadoudal up into Plumelec in the Landes de Lanvaux. The Tour has finished here five times (including the 1985 prologue and two team time trials), with another finish planned for 2027.

 Climb · Paris

Côte de la Butte Montmartre

130 m 1.1 km · 5.9 %
Stage 21

The Côte de la Butte Montmartre, via the cobbled Rue Lepic, was added to the Tour’s final stage after the 2024 Olympics. In 2025 and 2026 it was climbed three times before the Champs-Élysées finish.

 Finish · Paris

Paris Champs-Élysées

Stage 21

Since 1975 the Tour de France has finished on the Champs-Élysées, usually with a bunch sprint after several laps. The only exception was 2024, when the race ended in Nice because of the Olympics.

 City · Saône-et-Loire

Le Creusot

Stage 8

Le Creusot is an old industrial town in Saône-et-Loire; to its west rises the Signal d'Uchon (681 m), one of Burgundy's steepest climbs (3.2 km at 9.2%). The Tour visited in 1998, 2006 and 2021, and the longest stage of 2027 (Bourges–Le Creusot) finishes here again.

 Climb · Hérault

Cirque de Navacelles

Stage 14

The Cirque de Navacelles is a cut-off meander of the Vis river between the Causse du Larzac and the Causse de Blandas, on the Hérault–Gard border, labelled 'Grand Site de France' since 2016. No Tour de France passage is documented in the sources so far, so the history is empty.
