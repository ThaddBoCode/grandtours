Stories

The big stories of the Tour and the story of every stage – updated continuously.

Headlines

🏆2025

Pogačar wins his fourth Tour and the polka-dot jersey

Tadej Pogačar won the Tour for the fourth time, 4:24 ahead of Jonas Vingegaard and 11:00 ahead of Florian Lipowitz, and also took the mountains classification. He won four stages – Rouen, Mûr-de-Bretagne, Hautacam and the Peyragudes mountain time trial – and answered every attack Vingegaard launched in the final week.

🏆Stage 12

Pogačar's Pyrenean double at Hautacam and Peyragudes

On stage 12, the first high-mountain stage, Pogačar attacked 12 km from the top of Hautacam, beat Vingegaard by 2:10 and took the yellow jersey back from Ben Healy. The next day he won the 10.9 km mountain time trial to Peyragudes by 36 seconds – his 21st Tour stage win – and led by 4:07.

🔥Stage 14

Evenepoel abandons on the Tourmalet while third overall

Remco Evenepoel won the Caen time trial on stage 5 and was second overall after it, but struggled in the Pyrenees, losing time at Hautacam and Peyragudes. On stage 14 he was dropped early on the Tourmalet and climbed off while third overall; his team said he left to preserve the rest of his season.

🏆2025

Lipowitz becomes the first German on the podium since 2006

Florian Lipowitz finished third, 11:00 behind Pogačar, and won the white jersey – the first German on the Tour podium since Andreas Klöden in 2006. He moved up to third when Evenepoel abandoned on stage 14, saw Oscar Onley close to within 22 seconds at the Col de la Loze, then pulled clear again at La Plagne.

⚠️Stage 3

Crashes knock out Philipsen and Almeida

Jasper Philipsen, winner of stage 1 and the first yellow jersey, crashed in the intermediate sprint on stage 3 and abandoned with a broken collarbone and broken ribs. Pogačar's teammate João Almeida broke a rib in a crash on stage 7 and abandoned two days later, while Filippo Ganna and Stefan Bissegger had already left on stage 1 with concussions.

•Stage 10

Healy: a stage win, two days in yellow and the combativity prize

Ben Healy won stage 6 to Vire Normandie with a solo of more than 40 km, then took the yellow jersey at Puy de Sancy on stage 10 from a 29-rider breakaway and wore it for two days. He finished ninth overall and was named the race's most combative rider.

⚡2025

Milan wins green and ends Italy's wait for a stage win

Jonathan Milan won the points classification with 372 points, ahead of Pogačar and Biniam Girmay, after winning in Laval and Valence and scoring consistently in the intermediate sprints. His stage 8 win was the first Tour stage won by an Italian since Vincenzo Nibali in 2019.

🔥2025

Van der Poel: yellow twice, an all-day break, then out ill

Mathieu van der Poel won stage 2 to take the yellow jersey and won it back by a single second from the stage 6 breakaway. On stage 9 he spent almost the whole day out front with teammate Jonas Rickaert before being caught inside the final kilometre, and pneumonia forced him out before stage 16.

•Stage 21

Montmartre in the finale: van Aert wins in Paris

For the final stage the organisers added three climbs of the Butte Montmartre, inspired by the Paris 2024 Olympic road race – a change some riders had criticised beforehand. In the rain, with GC times neutralised, Wout van Aert dropped Pogačar on the last ascent and won alone on the Champs-Élysées; the organisers later said the stage had exceeded their expectations.

•Stage 19

Cattle disease outbreak cuts the last Alpine stage to 93.1 km

On the eve of stage 19, an outbreak of lumpy skin disease among cattle on the Col des Saisies forced the organisers to reroute the stage, removing two categorised climbs and shortening it to 93.1 km. Thymen Arensman won at La Plagne, his second stage win of the race after Superbagnères.

Stage by stage

1 Sat 5 Jul · Lille ↻ · Review Philipsen takes first yellow after Visma split the race in Lille Visma–Lease a Bike split the peloton in the crosswinds 17 km from the finish, and Jasper Philipsen won the sprint of the reduced front group ahead of Biniam Girmay to take the first yellow jersey. Pogačar and Vingegaard made the split, while Remco Evenepoel and Primož Roglič lost 39 seconds. Filippo Ganna and Stefan Bissegger abandoned after crashes with concussions. 2 Sun 6 Jul · Lauwin-Planque → Boulogne-sur-Mer · Review Van der Poel beats Pogačar in Boulogne and takes yellow After a nervous finale over three late climbs, Mathieu van der Poel opened the sprint early in Boulogne-sur-Mer and held off Tadej Pogačar, with Jonas Vingegaard third. Van der Poel took the yellow jersey from his teammate Jasper Philipsen, with Pogačar 4 seconds and Vingegaard 6 seconds behind. 3 Mon 7 Jul · Valenciennes → Dunkirk · Review Philipsen crashes out, Merlier edges Milan in Dunkirk Green jersey Jasper Philipsen crashed heavily in the intermediate sprint and abandoned with a broken collarbone and broken ribs. After several crashes in the final 5 km, Tim Merlier beat Jonathan Milan in a photo finish, and Milan took over the green jersey. For lack of attackers, no combativity award was given. 4 Tue 8 Jul · Amiens → Rouen · Review Pogačar's 100th career win in Rouen, van der Poel stays in yellow Tadej Pogačar attacked on the steep Rampe Saint-Hilaire, with only Jonas Vingegaard able to follow, before a small group regrouped on the descent. Pogačar came around Mathieu van der Poel in the sprint for the 100th win of his career. Van der Poel kept the yellow jersey on the same time as Pogačar, ahead on the sum of stage placings. 5 Wed 9 Jul · Caen ↻ · Review Evenepoel wins the Caen time trial, Pogačar takes yellow World and Olympic time trial champion Remco Evenepoel won the flat 33 km time trial in Caen, 16 seconds ahead of Tadej Pogačar. Pogačar took the yellow jersey, 42 seconds ahead of Evenepoel, while Jonas Vingegaard lost 1:21 and dropped to fourth at 1:13. 6 Thu 10 Jul · Bayeux → Vire Normandie · Review Healy solos to Vire, van der Poel back in yellow by one second Ben Healy attacked from an eight-rider breakaway about 42 km from the finish and soloed to his first Tour stage win in Vire Normandie, 2:44 ahead of Quinn Simmons. Mathieu van der Poel, also in the break, gained 1:29 on the favourites and took the yellow jersey back from Tadej Pogačar by a single second. 7 Fri 11 Jul · Saint-Malo → Guerlédan (Mûr-de-Bretagne) · Review Pogačar wins on the Mûr-de-Bretagne and retakes yellow A high-speed crash in the finale cost Pogačar's teammate João Almeida more than ten minutes and a broken rib. On the second ascent of the Mûr-de-Bretagne Tadej Pogačar won the uphill sprint ahead of Jonas Vingegaard and took the yellow jersey back from the dropped Mathieu van der Poel. He now led Remco Evenepoel by 54 seconds and Vingegaard by 1:17. 8 Sat 12 Jul · Saint-Méen-le-Grand → Laval · Review Milan wins in Laval – first Italian Tour stage win since 2019 On a quiet day, Jonathan Milan came around Mathieu van der Poel, who was leading out Kaden Groves, and beat Wout van Aert to win his first Tour stage. He was the first Italian to win a Tour stage since Vincenzo Nibali in 2019, and he regained the green jersey. Tim Merlier was out of contention after a mechanical 12.5 km from the finish. 9 Sun 13 Jul · Chinon → Châteauroux · Review Merlier wins after van der Poel's all-day raid with Rickaert Mathieu van der Poel and his teammate Jonas Rickaert attacked from the start and led for almost the whole day; van der Poel went solo 6 km from the finish but was caught inside the final kilometre. Tim Merlier then beat Jonathan Milan for his second stage win. At 50.013 km/h it was the second-fastest stage in Tour history, and João Almeida abandoned with injuries from his stage 7 crash. 10 Mon 14 Jul · Ennezat → Mont-Dore (Puy de Sancy) · Review Yates wins on Bastille Day, Healy takes the yellow jersey On the first mountain stage, through the Massif Central, Simon Yates attacked twice on the Puy de Sancy and won from a 29-rider breakaway, 9 seconds ahead of Thymen Arensman. Ben Healy, third from the same break, took the yellow jersey with a 29-second lead over Tadej Pogačar, while Lenny Martinez took the polka-dot jersey. 11 Wed 16 Jul · Toulouse ↻ · Review Abrahamsen outsprints Schmid in Toulouse, Pogačar crashes unhurt Jonas Abrahamsen and Mauro Schmid held off the chasing Mathieu van der Poel by 7 seconds, and Abrahamsen won the two-up sprint for his and Uno-X Mobility's first Grand Tour stage. Tadej Pogačar crashed in the finale after contact with Tobias Halland Johannessen, but the GC group waited and Ben Healy kept the yellow jersey. 12 Thu 17 Jul · Auch → Hautacam · Review Pogačar dominates on Hautacam and retakes yellow After a minute's silence for the under-23 rider Samuele Privitera, who had died after a crash the previous day, UAE set a hard pace on Hautacam and Tadej Pogačar attacked 12 km from the top. He won 2:10 ahead of Jonas Vingegaard and 2:23 ahead of Florian Lipowitz; Ben Healy lost 13 minutes, and Pogačar took back the yellow jersey with a 3:31 lead over Vingegaard. 13 Fri 18 Jul · Loudenvielle → Peyragudes · Review Pogačar wins the Peyragudes mountain time trial Tadej Pogačar was fastest at both time checks and climbed the 10.9 km to the Peyragudes altiport in exactly 23 minutes, 36 seconds faster than Jonas Vingegaard, with Primož Roglič third. It was his 21st Tour stage win, and his lead grew to 4:07. Remco Evenepoel, caught by Vingegaard near the finish, kept third overall by only 6 seconds over Florian Lipowitz. 14 Sat 19 Jul · Pau → Superbagnères · Review Evenepoel quits on the Tourmalet, Arensman wins at Superbagnères Third-placed Remco Evenepoel was dropped early on the Tourmalet and abandoned, while Mattias Skjelmose also left after a crash. Thymen Arensman attacked from the break on the Peyresourde, 37 km from the finish, and won his first Tour stage at Superbagnères, 1:08 ahead of Tadej Pogačar, who gained 4 seconds on Vingegaard in the final metres. Florian Lipowitz moved up to third overall and took the white jersey. 15 Sun 20 Jul · Muret → Carcassonne · Review Wellens solos to Carcassonne for his first Tour stage A crash early in the stage briefly caught out Jonas Vingegaard and Florian Lipowitz before the peloton regrouped. Tim Wellens attacked from the lead group 43.5 km from the finish and won alone in Carcassonne, 1:28 ahead of Victor Campenaerts, completing stage wins in all three Grand Tours. The peloton finished more than six minutes down, with no change at the top of the overall standings. 16 Tue 22 Jul · Montpellier → Mont Ventoux · Review Paret-Peintre beats Healy on Mont Ventoux Without Mathieu van der Poel, who did not start because of pneumonia, the race headed to the first summit finish on Mont Ventoux since 2013. Valentin Paret-Peintre came around Ben Healy in the sprint of the breakaway to become the first French stage winner of this Tour. Behind, Tadej Pogačar answered every attack from Jonas Vingegaard and gained 2 seconds, extending his lead to 4:15. 17 Wed 23 Jul · Bollène → Valence · Review Milan takes his second stage in Valence after a late crash A crash just before the flamme rouge brought down several riders, including Biniam Girmay, and held up Tim Merlier, leaving a small group to contest the sprint. Jonathan Milan, who had been dropped on the first climb before his team brought him back, held off Jordi Meeus for his second stage win and stretched his points lead to 72 over Tadej Pogačar. Carlos Rodríguez, tenth overall, fractured his pelvis in a crash in the closing stages and did not start the next day. 18 Thu 24 Jul · Vif → Courchevel (Col de la Loze) · Review O'Connor wins solo on the Col de la Loze, Pogačar drops Vingegaard On the queen stage over the Glandon and the Madeleine, Ben O'Connor attacked in the valley before the Col de la Loze and won alone, 1:45 ahead of Tadej Pogačar, who dropped Jonas Vingegaard inside the final kilometre. Pogačar's lead grew to 4:26, while Oscar Onley gained more than a minute and a half on Florian Lipowitz to close within 22 seconds of third place. Lenny Martinez was docked 8 mountains points for taking sticky bottles. 19 Fri 25 Jul · Albertville → La Plagne · Review Arensman holds off Vingegaard and Pogačar at La Plagne An outbreak of lumpy skin disease among cattle forced a late reroute that cut the stage to 93.1 km, still finishing at La Plagne. Thymen Arensman went clear 14 km from the finish and held off Jonas Vingegaard and Tadej Pogačar by 2 seconds for his second stage win. Florian Lipowitz gained 41 seconds on Oscar Onley to strengthen his hold on third place overall. 20 Sat 26 Jul · Nantua → Pontarlier · Review Groves solos to Pontarlier to complete his Grand Tour stage set Kaden Groves went clear of what remained of a 13-rider breakaway 16.5 km from the finish and won alone in Pontarlier, 54 seconds ahead of Frank van den Broek. The win completed his set of stage victories in all three Grand Tours. Jordan Jegat, also in the break, moved into tenth overall at Ben O'Connor's expense. 21 Sun 27 Jul · Mantes-la-Ville → Paris (Champs-Élysées) · Review Van Aert drops Pogačar on Montmartre and wins in Paris The final stage featured three ascents of the Butte Montmartre, inspired by the Paris 2024 Olympic road race, and GC times were neutralised early because of rain. Wout van Aert followed Tadej Pogačar's attack on the last climb of Montmartre, dropped him near the top and soloed to victory on the Champs-Élysées. Pogačar finished fourth to seal his fourth Tour win, with Jonathan Milan in green and Florian Lipowitz in white.
