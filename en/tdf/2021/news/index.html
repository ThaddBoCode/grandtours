Stories

The big stories of the Tour and the story of every stage – updated continuously.

Headlines

🏆2021

Pogačar wins his second Tour in a row

Tadej Pogačar won the Tour for the second year running, 5:20 ahead of Jonas Vingegaard and 7:03 ahead of Richard Carapaz, becoming the youngest rider to win the Tour twice. He won three stages – the Laval time trial and the summit finishes at the Col de Portet and Luz Ardiden – and, as in 2020, also took the mountains and young rider classifications. Carapaz became the first Ecuadorian to finish on the Tour podium.

🏆Stage 8

Pogačar's attack on the Col de Romme decides the Tour

On the first Alpine stage, partly in the rain, Pogačar attacked 4 km from the top of the Col de Romme, dropped Richard Carapaz and gained almost three and a half minutes on his rivals. He took the yellow jersey with a lead of 1:48 over Wout van Aert and kept it all the way to Paris, extending his advantage at Tignes and on both Pyrenean summit finishes.

★Stage 13

Cavendish equals Merckx's record of 34 stage wins

Mark Cavendish, a late replacement for the injured Sam Bennett at Deceuninck–Quick-Step, won in Fougères for his first Tour stage win since 2016 and added further wins in Châteauroux, Valence and Carcassonne. His fourth victory was his 34th Tour stage win, equalling Eddy Merckx's record. He wore the green jersey from stage 4 to Paris and won the points classification for the second time.

⚠️Stage 1

A spectator's sign causes a mass crash on stage 1

45 km from the end of the opening stage, a spectator holding up a sign for the TV cameras brought down Tony Martin and caused a crash that took down most of the peloton. Jasha Sütterlin had to abandon, and a second big crash 7.5 km from the finish ended the race for Cyril Lemoine and Ignatas Konovalovas. In December 2021 the spectator was ordered to pay a fine of €1,201.

⚠️Stage 3

Crashes end Roglič's Tour

Pre-race favourite Primož Roglič crashed about 10 km from the finish of stage 3, a day of crashes on which Jack Haig and Robert Gesink abandoned, Geraint Thomas dislocated his shoulder and Caleb Ewan broke his collarbone. The peloton stopped for a minute at the start of stage 4 in protest at the dangerous run-in. Roglič lost more than 30 minutes on stage 8 and did not start stage 9.

🏆Stage 11

Domestique Vingegaard becomes the main challenger

Jonas Vingegaard, originally meant to be a domestique for Primož Roglič, became Jumbo–Visma's leader after Roglič's crash and abandon. On Mont Ventoux he dropped Pogačar near the summit before being caught on the descent. He finished second on both Pyrenean summit finishes and third in the final time trial to take second overall, 5:20 behind Pogačar.

•2021

Van Aert wins on the Ventoux, in the time trial and in Paris

Wout van Aert won three very different stages: alone in Malaucène after the double ascent of Mont Ventoux, the time trial to Saint-Émilion and the sprint on the Champs-Élysées. He had been second overall after stage 7 before losing more than 31 minutes on the road to Tignes.

🔥Stage 2

Van der Poel takes yellow for his grandfather Poulidor

Mathieu van der Poel won on the Mûr-de-Bretagne on stage 2 to take the yellow jersey, which his grandfather Raymond Poulidor never wore. He defended it by 8 seconds in the Laval time trial and in a long breakaway on stage 7, lost it to Pogačar after six days and left the race before stage 9 to prepare for the Olympic mountain bike race.

•Stage 9

O'Connor wins at Tignes and finishes fourth overall

Ben O'Connor of AG2R Citroën, who started stage 9 8:13 behind Pogačar, went clear from a 41-rider breakaway, won at Tignes by more than five minutes and moved up to second overall. He lost about four minutes on Mont Ventoux but defended fourth place in the final time trial, finishing 10:02 behind Pogačar.

•Stage 19

Mohorič double as Bahrain Victorious win the team prize

Matej Mohorič soloed to wins in Le Creusot and Libourne, the first of which completed his set of stage wins in all three Grand Tours. His teammate Dylan Teuns won in Le Grand-Bornand, and Bahrain Victorious won the team classification.

Stage by stage

1 Sat 26 Jun · Brest → Landerneau · Review Alaphilippe attacks into yellow after the mass crash 45 km from the finish a spectator's sign brought down Tony Martin and most of the peloton with him, and a second mass crash 7.5 km out ended the race for Cyril Lemoine and Ignatas Konovalovas. World champion Julian Alaphilippe attacked 2.3 km from the line on the Côte de la Fosse aux Loups and won in Landerneau, 8 seconds ahead of Michael Matthews and Primož Roglič. He took the yellow and green jerseys, Ide Schelling the polka-dot jersey. 2 Sun 27 Jun · Perros-Guirec → Guerlédan (Mûr-de-Bretagne) · Review Van der Poel takes yellow on the Mûr-de-Bretagne Mathieu van der Poel attacked on the first ascent of the Mûr-de-Bretagne to collect the 8 bonus seconds, then went clear again on the second and won 6 seconds ahead of Tadej Pogačar, Primož Roglič and Wilco Kelderman. That earned him the yellow jersey, 8 seconds ahead of Julian Alaphilippe, as well as the polka-dot jersey. Geraint Thomas lost 17 seconds to the two Slovenians. 3 Mon 28 Jun · Lorient → Pontivy · Review Merlier wins in Pontivy as crashes hit Roglič and Ewan On a crash-strewn stage Geraint Thomas dislocated his shoulder, Robert Gesink and Jack Haig abandoned, and Primož Roglič crashed about 10 km from the finish and lost more than a minute. Caleb Ewan went down in the sprint, taking Peter Sagan with him, as Tim Merlier won ahead of his teammate Jasper Philipsen. Mathieu van der Poel stayed in yellow and Richard Carapaz moved up to third. 4 Tue 29 Jun · Redon → Fougères · Review Cavendish takes his first Tour stage win since 2016 The peloton stopped for a minute at kilometre zero in protest at the previous day's crashes. Brent Van Moer went clear from the breakaway and was caught only in the final 200 metres, where Mark Cavendish beat Nacer Bouhanni and Jasper Philipsen for his 31st Tour stage win and his first since 2016. Cavendish took the green jersey and van der Poel stayed in yellow. 5 Wed 30 Jun · Changé → Laval · Review Pogačar wins the time trial, van der Poel hangs on to yellow Tadej Pogačar won the 27.2 km time trial from Changé to Laval in 32:00, 19 seconds ahead of European champion Stefan Küng and 27 ahead of Jonas Vingegaard. Mathieu van der Poel was fifth, 31 seconds down, and kept the yellow jersey by 8 seconds over Pogačar. Primož Roglič, Richard Carapaz and Geraint Thomas were now almost two minutes behind Pogačar. 6 Thu 1 Jul · Tours → Châteauroux · Review Cavendish wins in Châteauroux for the third time Greg Van Avermaet and Roger Kluge were the last breakaway survivors and were caught 2.5 km from the finish. Mark Cavendish came past Jasper Philipsen and Nacer Bouhanni in the final 150 metres for his 32nd Tour stage win, his third in Châteauroux after 2008 and 2011. Van der Poel stayed in yellow and the top ten was unchanged. 7 Fri 2 Jul · Vierzon → Le Creusot · Review Mohorič solos to Le Creusot as Roglič loses time On the 249.1 km stage, the longest of the race, a 29-rider group including Mathieu van der Poel and Wout van Aert went clear and Matej Mohorič attacked alone on the Signal d'Uchon. He won by 1:20 over Jasper Stuyven, took the polka-dot jersey and completed his set of stage wins in all three Grand Tours. Van der Poel extended his lead to 30 seconds over van Aert, while Primož Roglič lost more than three minutes to the other favourites. 8 Sat 3 Jul · Oyonnax → Le Grand-Bornand · Review Teuns wins in Le Grand-Bornand, Pogačar storms into yellow On the first Alpine stage, partly in the rain, Geraint Thomas and Primož Roglič lost more than 30 minutes and Mathieu van der Poel was dropped on the Col de Romme. Tadej Pogačar attacked 4 km from the top of the Romme, dropped Richard Carapaz and gained almost three and a half minutes on his rivals, while Dylan Teuns soloed to victory, 44 seconds ahead of Ion Izagirre. Pogačar took yellow with a lead of 1:48 over Wout van Aert, and Wout Poels the polka-dot jersey. 9 Sun 4 Jul · Cluses → Tignes · Review O'Connor triumphs at Tignes as Roglič and van der Poel quit Primož Roglič and Mathieu van der Poel did not start. Ben O'Connor went clear from a 41-rider breakaway on the final climb to Tignes and won 5:07 ahead of Mattia Cattaneo, while Tadej Pogačar dropped his rivals near the top and gained 32 seconds. O'Connor moved up to second overall, 2:01 behind Pogačar, Nairo Quintana took the polka-dot jersey, and seven riders, including Arnaud Démare and Bryan Coquard, missed the time cut. 10 Tue 6 Jul · Albertville → Valence · Review Cavendish wins in Valence for number 33 Hugo Houle and Tosh Van der Sande formed the day's breakaway and were caught before the finale; crosswinds split the peloton several times, but no favourite lost time. Deceuninck–Quick-Step led out Mark Cavendish perfectly for his third stage win of the race and his 33rd in the Tour, ahead of Wout van Aert and Jasper Philipsen. The overall standings were unchanged. 11 Wed 7 Jul · Sorgues → Malaucène · Review Van Aert conquers the double Ventoux On the first Tour stage to climb Mont Ventoux twice, Wout van Aert went clear from the breakaway on the second ascent and soloed to Malaucène, 1:14 ahead of Kenny Elissonde and Bauke Mollema. Behind, Jonas Vingegaard dropped Tadej Pogačar near the summit, but Pogačar, Richard Carapaz and Rigoberto Urán caught him near the flamme rouge. Urán moved up to second, 5:18 down, while Ben O'Connor lost about four minutes and David Gaudu more than 25. 12 Thu 8 Jul · Saint-Paul-Trois-Châteaux → Nîmes · Review Politt solos to victory in Nîmes After crosswinds split the peloton into three groups early on, a 13-rider breakaway went clear and was never chased. Nils Politt made the decisive attack 12 km from the finish and won his first Tour stage, 31 seconds ahead of Imanol Erviti and Harry Sweeny. The peloton finished around 16 minutes down; Peter Sagan had not started because of a knee injury. 13 Fri 9 Jul · Nîmes → Carcassonne · Review Cavendish equals Merckx with his 34th stage win About 60 km from the finish a crash on a descent brought down a group of riders, some of them into a small ravine, and forced Simon Yates, Lucas Hamilton and Roger Kluge out of the race. In a chaotic sprint in Carcassonne Michael Mørkøv led out Mark Cavendish, who won ahead of Mørkøv and Jasper Philipsen for his fourth stage of the race and his 34th in the Tour, equalling Eddy Merckx's record. Cavendish extended his lead in the points classification to 101 points. 14 Sat 10 Jul · Carcassonne → Quillan · Review Mollema solos to Quillan, Martin jumps to second It took almost 100 km for a 14-rider breakaway including Guillaume Martin to form, and Bauke Mollema attacked from it on a descent 42 km from the finish. He soloed to victory in Quillan, 1:04 ahead of Patrick Konrad and Sergio Higuita. Martin rose from ninth to second overall, 4:04 behind Tadej Pogačar, and Michael Woods took the polka-dot jersey from Nairo Quintana. 15 Sun 11 Jul · Céret → Andorra la Vella · Review Kuss wins in Andorra, Poels back in polka dots From a 32-rider breakaway, Sepp Kuss attacked 5 km from the top of the Col de Beixalis and won his first Tour stage in Andorra la Vella, 23 seconds ahead of Alejandro Valverde. Nairo Quintana was first over the Port d'Envalira, the highest point of the race, and Wout Poels took back the polka-dot jersey. Pogačar stayed in yellow 5:18 ahead of Rigoberto Urán, while Guillaume Martin lost almost four minutes and dropped to ninth. 16 Tue 13 Jul · El Pas de la Casa → Saint-Gaudens · Review Konrad solos to Saint-Gaudens Patrick Konrad bridged to the leaders on the Col de la Core and went clear alone on the Col de Portet-d'Aspet. He won in Saint-Gaudens 42 seconds ahead of Sonny Colbrelli and Michael Matthews, Bora–Hansgrohe's second stage win of the race. The yellow jersey group finished almost 14 minutes down and the top ten was unchanged. 17 Wed 14 Jul · Muret → Saint-Lary-Soulan (Col de Portet) · Review Pogačar wins atop the Col de Portet on Bastille Day On Bastille Day Anthony Perez was the last survivor of a breakaway featuring four French riders before Tadej Pogačar attacked about 8.5 km from the summit of the Col de Portet. Only Jonas Vingegaard and Richard Carapaz could follow, and Pogačar won the three-man sprint at 2,215 m, 3 seconds ahead of Vingegaard and 4 ahead of Carapaz. Vingegaard moved up to second overall at 5:39 and Carapaz to third at 5:43, while Rigoberto Urán dropped to fourth. 18 Thu 15 Jul · Pau → Luz Ardiden · Review Pogačar doubles up at Luz Ardiden and takes polka dots Pierre Latour was first over the Col du Tourmalet, and David Gaudu was the last of the breakaway to be caught on the final climb. Tadej Pogačar attacked 3.3 km from the top of Luz Ardiden, closed down two moves by Enric Mas in the final kilometre and won ahead of Jonas Vingegaard and Richard Carapaz, both 2 seconds back. With 80 points from his two summit wins Pogačar also took the polka-dot jersey, and his lead grew to 5:45 over Vingegaard; Rigoberto Urán lost almost nine minutes. 19 Fri 16 Jul · Mourenx → Libourne · Review Mohorič wins again, this time in Libourne A 20-rider breakaway went clear and the peloton gave up the chase with 81 km to go. Matej Mohorič attacked 25 km from the finish and soloed to his second stage win of the race, 58 seconds ahead of Christophe Laporte and Casper Pedersen. The peloton finished about 21 minutes down and the overall standings did not change. 20 Sat 17 Jul · Libourne → Saint-Émilion · Review Van Aert wins the time trial as Pogačar seals the Tour Wout van Aert set the only time under 36 minutes, 35:53, to win the 30.8 km time trial from Libourne to Saint-Émilion, 21 seconds ahead of Kasper Asgreen and 32 ahead of Jonas Vingegaard. Vingegaard secured second overall, Ben O'Connor held fourth ahead of Wilco Kelderman, and Tadej Pogačar rode cautiously to eighth. Going into Paris he led Vingegaard by 5:20 and Richard Carapaz by 7:03. 21 Sun 18 Jul · Chatou → Paris (Champs-Élysées) · Review Van Aert wins on the Champs-Élysées, Pogačar wins the Tour After the traditional procession the stage came down to a sprint on the Champs-Élysées, where Mike Teunissen led out Wout van Aert to beat Jasper Philipsen and Mark Cavendish – his third stage win after Mont Ventoux and the time trial. Tadej Pogačar finished safely in the bunch to win his second consecutive Tour along with the mountains and young rider classifications, Cavendish took the green jersey and Franck Bonnamour the overall combativity award. 141 of the 184 starters reached Paris.
