Stories

The big stories of the Tour and the story of every stage – updated continuously.

Headlines

🏆2022

Vingegaard dethrones Pogačar to win his first Tour

Jonas Vingegaard won the Tour for the first time, 2:43 ahead of two-time defending champion Tadej Pogačar and 7:22 ahead of Geraint Thomas. He became the first Dane to win the race since Bjarne Riis in 1996 and also took the polka-dot jersey.

🏆Stage 11

Pogačar cracks on the Col du Granon as Vingegaard takes yellow

On stage 11 Jumbo–Visma's Primož Roglič and Jonas Vingegaard attacked Pogačar again and again on the Télégraphe and the Galibier. On the final climb to the Col du Granon, included for the first time since 1986, Vingegaard went clear 4.5 km from the top and won, while Pogačar lost 2:51 and the yellow jersey.

🏆Stage 18

Pogačar crashes, Vingegaard waits – then wins at Hautacam

On the last mountain stage Pogačar attacked repeatedly on the Col de Spandelles but could not drop Vingegaard. On the descent Pogačar crashed and the yellow jersey waited for him; on Hautacam, Sepp Kuss and Wout van Aert set the pace before Vingegaard rode away about 4 km from the top. He won by 1:04, extended his lead to 3:26 and took the polka-dot jersey.

★2022

Van Aert's record green jersey crowns a Jumbo–Visma sweep

Wout van Aert won the points classification with 480 points, breaking Peter Sagan's modern record, won three stages and was named the Tour's most combative rider. With Vingegaard in yellow and polka dots, it was the first time since Eddy Merckx's Faema team in 1969 that one team won the yellow and green jerseys and the mountains classification.

⚠️Stage 5

Chaos on the cobbles: Roglič dislocates his shoulder

Stage 5 brought cobbles back to the Tour for the first time since 2018. Vingegaard needed three bike changes after a mechanical and Roglič crashed, dislocated his shoulder and lost more than two minutes, while Pogačar attacked with Jasper Stuyven and gained time on all his rivals. Roglič did not start stage 15, the day Steven Kruijswijk also crashed out.

🔥2022

COVID-19 and abandons: the fewest finishers since 2000

In total, 17 riders had to leave the race because of COVID-19, among them stage winners Magnus Cort and Simon Clarke and former winner Chris Froome. Only 135 of the 176 starters reached Paris, the fewest finishers since 2000, and both Vingegaard's and Pogačar's teams were badly depleted by the end.

•Stage 1

Copenhagen's Grand Départ and Cort's mountain record

The Tour started in Copenhagen for the first time, with three stages in Denmark, and Yves Lampaert surprised the favourites to win the opening time trial and the first yellow jersey. Home rider Magnus Cort took all the mountain points on offer in Denmark and on stage 4 broke Federico Bahamontes' record for the most consecutive summits won.

★Stage 12

Pidcock becomes the youngest winner on Alpe d'Huez

On the Bastille Day queen stage, Tom Pidcock bridged across to the breakaway, attacked 10.6 km from the finish and won alone on Alpe d'Huez, 48 seconds ahead of Louis Meintjes and more than two minutes ahead of Chris Froome. It was his first WorldTour win and made him the youngest rider to win on Alpe d'Huez.

🌧️2022

Heatwave and climate protests

The second week was raced in an intense heatwave, with temperatures of around 40 °C on several stages, and some riders suffered heatstroke. Climate protesters sitting on the road brought the race to a halt on stage 10 and again in the final week; on stage 10 the riders were restarted with the gaps they had before.

Stage by stage

1 Fri 1 Jul · Copenhagen ↻ · Review Lampaert stuns the favourites in the Copenhagen time trial Yves Lampaert won the 13.2 km time trial through Copenhagen in 15:17, 5 seconds ahead of Wout van Aert and 7 ahead of Tadej Pogačar, taking his first Tour stage and the first yellow and green jerseys. Pogačar was the fastest of the GC contenders, 8 seconds up on Jonas Vingegaard, and took the white jersey. 2 Sat 2 Jul · Roskilde → Nyborg · Review Jakobsen wins in Nyborg, van Aert takes yellow on bonus seconds After the crossing of the Great Belt Bridge, where race leader Lampaert was caught up in a crash, Fabio Jakobsen won a reduced bunch sprint ahead of van Aert and Mads Pedersen for his first Tour stage win. Van Aert took the yellow and green jerseys thanks to six bonus seconds, while Magnus Cort led over all three climbs to take the polka-dot jersey. 3 Sun 3 Jul · Vejle → Sønderborg · Review Groenewegen pips van Aert, second for the third day in a row Magnus Cort spent most of the day alone in the breakaway and again led over all three climbs. Van Aert opened the sprint in Sønderborg, but Dylan Groenewegen came past him on the line for his first WorldTour win in over two years, with Jasper Philipsen third. Van Aert kept yellow, now 7 seconds ahead of Lampaert, before the transfer to France. 4 Tue 5 Jul · Dunkirk → Calais · Review Van Aert attacks in yellow and wins alone in Calais Jumbo–Visma blew the race apart on the Côte du Cap Blanc-Nez, and van Aert went clear near the top to solo to victory, 8 seconds ahead of the peloton led home by Jasper Philipsen, who celebrated thinking he had won. Earlier Magnus Cort led over the first five climbs, breaking Federico Bahamontes' record for consecutive summits won. Van Aert's lead grew to 25 seconds over Lampaert. 5 Wed 6 Jul · Lille → Arenberg · Review Clarke wins on the cobbles, Pogačar profits as Roglič crashes Simon Clarke narrowly beat Taco van der Hoorn in the sprint of the breakaway at Arenberg. Behind, Vingegaard was slowed by a mechanical, Roglič crashed and dislocated his shoulder and Jack Haig abandoned, while Pogačar attacked with Jasper Stuyven and gained time on all his rivals. Van Aert kept yellow, 13 seconds ahead of Neilson Powless. 6 Thu 7 Jul · Binche → Longwy · Review Pogačar wins the uphill sprint in Longwy and takes yellow On the longest stage of the race, van Aert spent much of the day in the breakaway wearing yellow but was caught 11.1 km from the finish and dropped. Pogačar won the uphill sprint in Longwy ahead of Michael Matthews and David Gaudu and took the yellow jersey, 4 seconds ahead of Neilson Powless and 31 ahead of Vingegaard. 7 Fri 8 Jul · Tomblaine → La Super Planche des Belles Filles · Review Pogačar edges Vingegaard on La Super Planche des Belles Filles On the first summit finish, Vingegaard attacked on the gravel in the final 200 m and only Pogačar could follow; the race leader came back past him to win, after both had overtaken the last breakaway rider, Lennard Kämna, close to the line. Roglič was third at 12 seconds, and Pogačar's lead grew to 35 seconds over Vingegaard, with Geraint Thomas third overall at 1:10. 8 Sat 9 Jul · Dole → Lausanne · Review Van Aert takes his second stage win in Lausanne Before the start, Pogačar's teammate Vegard Stake Laengen left the race with COVID-19. Fred Wright, the last of the breakaway, was caught 3.5 km from the finish on the climb to Lausanne, where van Aert won the uphill sprint ahead of Michael Matthews and Pogačar. Pogačar's 4 bonus seconds increased his lead over Vingegaard to 39 seconds. 9 Sun 10 Jul · Aigle → Châtel · Review Jungels solos to victory in Châtel Bob Jungels attacked from the big breakaway on the Col de la Croix with 64 km to go and held on alone for his first Tour stage win, 22 seconds ahead of Jonathan Castroviejo and 26 ahead of Carlos Verona; Thibaut Pinot, who had chased hard, was passed in the final kilometre. Simon Geschke took the polka-dot jersey, and Pogačar kept yellow before the rest day. 10 Tue 12 Jul · Morzine → Megève · Review Cort wins a photo finish in Megève, Kämna comes close to yellow Climate protesters on the road forced the race to stop, after which it was restarted with the previous gaps. From a 25-rider breakaway, Magnus Cort beat Nick Schultz in a photo finish at the Megève altiport. Lennard Kämna, who was also in the move, rose to second overall, just 11 seconds behind Pogačar. 11 Wed 13 Jul · Albertville → Col du Granon · Review Vingegaard breaks Pogačar on the Granon and takes yellow Jumbo–Visma attacked Pogačar repeatedly on the Télégraphe and the Galibier, where Warren Barguil was first over the top. On the Col du Granon Vingegaard went clear 4.5 km from the finish and won alone, 59 seconds ahead of Nairo Quintana, while Pogačar lost 2:51. Vingegaard took yellow, 2:16 ahead of Romain Bardet and 2:22 ahead of Pogačar; Mathieu van der Poel abandoned. 12 Thu 14 Jul · Briançon → Alpe d'Huez · Review Pidcock wins alone on Alpe d'Huez, Vingegaard resists Tom Pidcock attacked from the breakaway 10.6 km from the finish and won alone on Alpe d'Huez, 48 seconds ahead of Louis Meintjes, with Chris Froome third. Behind, Pogačar attacked twice near the top but Vingegaard followed each time. Vingegaard kept yellow, with Pogačar moving up to second at 2:22 and Geraint Thomas third at 2:26. 13 Fri 15 Jul · Le Bourg-d'Oisans → Saint-Étienne · Review Pedersen wins from a three-man group in Saint-Étienne Warren Barguil did not start after a positive COVID-19 test. Mads Pedersen attacked his breakaway companions 10.6 km from the finish, only Fred Wright and Hugo Houle could go with him, and he won the sprint for his first Grand Tour stage. The peloton finished almost six minutes down, with no changes at the top of the general classification. 14 Sat 16 Jul · Saint-Étienne → Mende · Review Matthews wins in Mende as Vingegaard stays on Pogačar's wheel A 23-rider breakaway was allowed more than 14 minutes. Michael Matthews, briefly dropped by Alberto Bettiol on the steep Côte de la Croix Neuve, fought his way back, attacked near the top and won alone, 15 seconds ahead of Bettiol. Pogačar attacked on the same climb, but Vingegaard followed and the gap between them stayed at 2:22. 15 Sun 17 Jul · Rodez → Carcassonne · Review Philipsen wins in Carcassonne on a bad day for Jumbo–Visma Magnus Cort and Simon Clarke left the race with COVID-19 before the start and Roglič did not start; Steven Kruijswijk then abandoned with a dislocated shoulder after a crash, and Vingegaard also went down but continued. Benjamin Thomas was caught inside the final kilometre, and Jasper Philipsen came past van Aert to win his first Tour stage. 16 Tue 19 Jul · Carcassonne → Foix · Review Houle solos to his first Grand Tour stage win in Foix Hugo Houle attacked from the breakaway on the descent of the Port de Lers, 38 km from the finish, and won alone, 1:10 ahead of Valentin Madouas and his teammate Michael Woods. Pogačar attacked three times around the top of the Port de Lers but could not shake off Vingegaard, who kept his lead of 2:22. Lennard Kämna and Jakob Fuglsang did not start. 17 Wed 20 Jul · Saint-Gaudens → Peyragudes · Review Pogačar beats Vingegaard again, this time at Peyragudes Without Rafał Majka, who did not start after being injured near the end of stage 16, UAE Team Emirates relied on Brandon McNulty, who paced Pogačar and Vingegaard clear of all the other contenders. On the steep final ramp Pogačar came past Vingegaard to win his third stage, with McNulty third at 32 seconds. Bonus seconds cut Vingegaard's lead to 2:18, with Geraint Thomas third at 4:56. 18 Thu 21 Jul · Lourdes → Hautacam · Review Vingegaard wins at Hautacam and all but seals the Tour Pogačar attacked repeatedly on the Col de Spandelles, crashed on the descent and was waited for by Vingegaard. On Hautacam, Sepp Kuss and Wout van Aert set a fierce pace before Vingegaard went clear about 4 km from the top and won, 1:04 ahead of Pogačar, with van Aert third. Vingegaard's lead grew to 3:26 and he also took the polka-dot jersey from Simon Geschke; Chris Froome had not started after testing positive for COVID-19. 19 Fri 22 Jul · Castelnau-Magnoac → Cahors · Review Laporte takes the first French stage win of this Tour Protesters on the road briefly neutralised the race early on, and Enric Mas had left with COVID-19 before the start. After Alexis Gougeard, Fred Wright and Jasper Stuyven had been reeled in, Christophe Laporte jumped from the peloton in the final two kilometres and held off the sprinters in Cahors by one second, with Jasper Philipsen second. It was the first stage win by a French rider in this Tour. 20 Sat 23 Jul · Lacapelle-Marival → Rocamadour · Review Van Aert wins the Rocamadour time trial, Vingegaard safe in yellow Wout van Aert won the 40.7 km time trial in 47:59, beating Filippo Ganna's long-standing best time by 42 seconds, for his third stage win. Vingegaard was second at 19 seconds, ahead of Pogačar at 27 and Geraint Thomas at 32, and went into the final stage 3:34 ahead of Pogačar. 21 Sun 24 Jul · Paris La Défense Arena → Paris (Champs-Élysées) · Review Philipsen wins on the Champs-Élysées, Vingegaard crowned in Paris Jasper Philipsen won the sprint on the Champs-Élysées ahead of Dylan Groenewegen and Alexander Kristoff for his second stage win of the Tour. Vingegaard crossed the line with his teammates, losing 51 seconds, and won the Tour by 2:43 over Pogačar, who took the white jersey for the third time, equalling Jan Ullrich and Andy Schleck. Van Aert won the green jersey, Vingegaard the polka-dot jersey and Ineos Grenadiers the team classification.
