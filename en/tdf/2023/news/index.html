Stories

The big stories of the Tour and the story of every stage – updated continuously.

Headlines

🏆2023

Vingegaard defends his title by 7:29 over Pogačar

Jonas Vingegaard won the Tour for the second year in a row, 7:29 ahead of Tadej Pogačar and 10:56 ahead of Adam Yates – the largest winning margin since 2014. After two weeks in which only seconds separated the two rivals, he settled the race in the Alps, and his Jumbo–Visma team also won the team classification.

🏆Stage 16

Vingegaard crushes the Combloux time trial

Going into the only individual time trial, 22.4 km from Passy to Combloux, Vingegaard led Pogačar by just 10 seconds. He won by 1:38 over the Slovenian, with teammate Wout van Aert third at 2:51, and stretched his lead to 1:48. Adam Yates moved up to third overall, five seconds ahead of Carlos Rodríguez.

🏆Stage 17

Pogačar cracks on the Col de la Loze

A day after the time trial, on the queen stage to Courchevel with more than 5,000 m of climbing, Pogačar was dropped on the Col de la Loze and needed help from his teammate Marc Soler. Vingegaard's lead grew to 7:35, while Felix Gall won the stage alone.

🏆2023

Just 10 seconds apart after two weeks

The first two weeks were a duel at close quarters: Vingegaard gained 1:04 on Pogačar on the Col de Marie-Blanque and took yellow the next day at Cauterets, where Pogačar won the stage. The Slovenian clawed back more seconds on the Puy de Dôme and the Grand Colombier, and at the second rest day he was only 10 seconds behind.

⚠️Stage 8

Crash ends Cavendish's chase of the stage-win record

Mark Cavendish started his last Tour level with Eddy Merckx on 34 stage wins. In Bordeaux he finished second to Jasper Philipsen after his gears slipped in the final metres; the next day he crashed on the road to Limoges and had to abandon injured.

⚡2023

Philipsen wins four stages and the green jersey

Jasper Philipsen won the bunch sprints in Bayonne, Nogaro, Bordeaux and Moulins and wore green from stage 4 all the way to Paris. He won the points classification for the first time, with 377 points, 119 more than Mads Pedersen, and was beaten by a tyre's width by Jordi Meeus on the Champs-Élysées.

•Stage 20

Ciccone takes the polka-dot jersey, Italy's first since 1992

After Neilson Powless, Felix Gall and Vingegaard had all led the mountains classification, Giulio Ciccone took over on stage 15 and sealed it in the Vosges on stage 20, finishing with 106 points to Gall's 92. He was the first Italian to win the classification since Claudio Chiappucci in 1992.

🔥Stage 20

Pinot's farewell on the Petit Ballon

In his last Tour before retirement, Thibaut Pinot attacked from the breakaway on stage 20 and crossed the Petit Ballon alone, on a climb lined by thousands of cheering fans. He was caught on the Col du Platzerwasel and Pogačar won in Le Markstein ahead of Felix Gall and Vingegaard, while Pinot received the day's combativity award.

•Stage 9

The Tour returns to the Puy de Dôme after 35 years

For the first time since 1988 a stage finished on top of the Puy de Dôme. Matteo Jorgenson attacked from the breakaway 47 km from the finish and led alone until Michael Woods passed him in the final 500 m to win; behind, Pogačar took 8 seconds out of Vingegaard.

•Stage 1

Basque Grand Départ: Yates twins one-two, Mas and Carapaz out

The Tour started in Bilbao, the second Grand Départ in the Basque Country after 1992. Twins Adam and Simon Yates broke away in the finale of stage 1 and finished first and second, giving Adam the first yellow jersey. A crash ended the race for pre-race favourites Enric Mas and Richard Carapaz, who did not start stage 2 with a fractured kneecap.

Stage by stage

1 Sat 1 Jul · Bilbao ↻ · Review Yates twins one-two in Bilbao, Adam takes yellow Adam Yates won the opening stage around Bilbao four seconds ahead of his twin brother Simon after the pair broke away from the favourites in the finale. Pogačar led the chasers home 12 seconds down; Adam Yates took the yellow and green jerseys and Neilson Powless the polka-dot jersey. Enric Mas abandoned after a crash. 2 Sun 2 Jul · Vitoria-Gasteiz → San Sebastián · Review Lafay strikes under the flamme rouge in San Sebastián Richard Carapaz did not start after his crash. On the Jaizkibel Pogačar took the 8 bonus seconds ahead of Vingegaard, and in the finale Victor Lafay attacked under the flamme rouge and held off the group led by Wout van Aert – Cofidis' first Tour stage win since 2008. Adam Yates stayed in yellow, six seconds ahead of Pogačar, and Lafay took green. 3 Mon 3 Jul · Amorebieta-Etxano → Bayonne · Review Philipsen opens his account in Bayonne After a two-man breakaway by Neilson Powless and Laurent Pichon, Jasper Philipsen won the sprint into Bayonne ahead of Phil Bauhaus and Caleb Ewan following a strong lead-out by Mathieu van der Poel. The commissaires reviewed his sprint duel with Wout van Aert but let the result stand. Adam Yates kept yellow and Powless added to his mountains lead. 4 Tue 4 Jul · Dax → Nogaro · Review Philipsen doubles up on the Nogaro motor circuit On a slow stage with no breakaway until around 100 km in, Philipsen won at the Circuit Paul Armagnac by a quarter of a wheel from Caleb Ewan. Crashes in the finale brought down Fabio Jakobsen among others, and Mathieu van der Poel was relegated for impeding Biniam Girmay. Philipsen took the green jersey from Victor Lafay. 5 Wed 5 Jul · Pau → Laruns · Review Hindley wins in Laruns and takes yellow Jai Hindley got into a big breakaway, dropped Felix Gall on the Col de Marie-Blanque and won alone in Laruns, 32 seconds ahead of Giulio Ciccone and Gall. Behind, Vingegaard attacked on the Marie-Blanque, finished 34 seconds down on Hindley and gained 1:04 on Pogačar. Hindley took yellow by 47 seconds over Vingegaard, and Gall the polka-dot jersey. 6 Thu 6 Jul · Tarbes → Cauterets (Cambasque) · Review Pogačar wins at Cauterets, Vingegaard takes yellow Jumbo–Visma's pace on the Col du Tourmalet left only Pogačar with Vingegaard, and Wout van Aert dropped back from the breakaway to help his leader. On the final climb to Cambasque Pogačar attacked less than 3 km from the finish and won by 24 seconds. Vingegaard took yellow with a 25-second lead, Hindley dropped to third at 1:34 and Powless regained the polka-dot jersey. 7 Fri 7 Jul · Mont-de-Marsan → Bordeaux · Review Philipsen again in Bordeaux as Cavendish's gears slip Simon Guglielmi spent most of the day alone in front. In the sprint along the quays of Bordeaux Philipsen narrowly beat Mark Cavendish, whose gears slipped in the final metres, with Biniam Girmay third. It was Philipsen's third stage win, and Vingegaard kept yellow. 8 Sat 8 Jul · Libourne → Limoges · Review Pedersen powers to victory in Limoges, Cavendish out Mark Cavendish crashed and abandoned with a shoulder injury, ending his chase of the stage-win record. A crash 6 km from the line, caused by a spectator, cost Simon Yates and Mikel Landa time and forced Steff Cras out. Mads Pedersen won the uphill sprint ahead of Jasper Philipsen and Wout van Aert. 9 Sun 9 Jul · Saint-Léonard-de-Noblat → Puy de Dôme · Review Woods snatches the Puy de Dôme The Tour finished on the Puy de Dôme for the first time since 1988. Matteo Jorgenson attacked from a 14-man breakaway 47 km out and led alone until Michael Woods passed him in the last 500 m; Pierre Latour was second and Jorgenson fourth. Pogačar gained 8 seconds on Vingegaard, cutting the gap to 17 seconds. 10 Tue 11 Jul · Vulcania → Issoire · Review Pello Bilbao wins in Issoire for Gino Mäder After a chaotic start in great heat, with Vingegaard and Pogačar themselves joining early attacks, a stable breakaway only formed around halfway. Krists Neilands rode alone in front for a long time but was caught near the finish, and Pello Bilbao won the six-man sprint ahead of Georg Zimmermann and Ben O'Connor. Bilbao dedicated the win to his late teammate Gino Mäder. 11 Wed 12 Jul · Clermont-Ferrand → Moulins · Review Philipsen's fourth stage win in Moulins Daniel Oss was the last survivor of a three-man breakaway but was caught in the finale. Without a teammate to lead him out, Jasper Philipsen won the bunch sprint in Moulins by a length from Dylan Groenewegen – his fourth stage win of the Tour. The overall standings were unchanged. 12 Thu 13 Jul · Roanne → Belleville-en-Beaujolais · Review Ion Izagirre solos to Belleville-en-Beaujolais Fabio Jakobsen did not start and David de la Cruz crashed out. After a fierce fight for the breakaway, Ion Izagirre went clear on the Col de la Croix Rosier and rode the last 30 km or so alone, winning by 58 seconds from Mathieu Burgaudeau and Matteo Jorgenson. It was Cofidis' second stage win of the Tour after Lafay's. 13 Fri 14 Jul · Châtillon-sur-Chalaronne → Grand Colombier · Review Kwiatkowski conquers the Grand Colombier on Bastille Day On the Bastille Day summit finish Michał Kwiatkowski went clear from the breakaway 11 km from the finish and won 47 seconds ahead of Maxim Van Gils. Pogačar was third, four seconds ahead of Vingegaard, and with the bonus seconds cut the gap to 9 seconds. Caleb Ewan abandoned during the stage. 14 Sat 15 Jul · Annemasse → Morzine · Review Rodríguez wins in Morzine after the Joux Plane duel A mass crash shortly after the start led to the race being neutralised and several riders abandoned; Romain Bardet later crashed out too. On the Col de Joux Plane Pogačar could not shake off Vingegaard, and his last attack was obstructed by motorbikes crowding the road. Carlos Rodríguez caught the pair on the descent and won in Morzine, moving into third overall one second ahead of Hindley; Vingegaard now led by 10 seconds and also took the polka-dot jersey. 15 Sun 16 Jul · Les Gets → Saint-Gervais-les-Bains · Review Poels takes his first Tour stage win at Saint-Gervais On a stage where a crash caused by a spectator brought down many riders, a big group got away. Wout Poels attacked on the Côte des Amerands 11 km from the finish and won alone at Saint-Gervais, 2:08 ahead of Wout van Aert – his first Tour stage win after years as a super-domestique. Pogačar and Vingegaard finished together, and Giulio Ciccone took the polka-dot jersey. 16 Tue 18 Jul · Passy → Combloux · Review Vingegaard wins the time trial by 1:38 In the only individual time trial, 22.4 km from Passy to Combloux, Vingegaard was fastest at every time check and won by 1:38 over Pogačar, who had switched bikes for the Côte de Domancy; Wout van Aert was third at 2:51. Vingegaard's lead grew from 10 seconds to 1:48, and Adam Yates moved up to third overall. 17 Wed 19 Jul · Saint-Gervais-les-Bains → Courchevel · Review Gall wins at Courchevel as Pogačar cracks On the queen stage Felix Gall attacked 6.5 km from the top of the Col de la Loze and won alone at Courchevel on his Tour debut, 34 seconds ahead of Simon Yates. Pogačar was dropped on the Loze and lost heavily; Vingegaard, fourth on the stage, now led by 7:35. Phil Bauhaus abandoned. 18 Thu 20 Jul · Moûtiers → Bourg-en-Bresse · Review Asgreen's breakaway beats the sprinters in Bourg-en-Bresse Wout van Aert did not start, leaving the race to be with his wife, who was about to give birth. Kasper Asgreen, Jonas Abrahamsen and Victor Campenaerts, later joined by Pascal Eenkhoorn, were out front all day with barely more than a minute's lead. Three of them held off the charging bunch by a whisker, Asgreen winning ahead of Eenkhoorn and Abrahamsen, with Philipsen fourth. 19 Fri 21 Jul · Moirans-en-Montagne → Poligny · Review Mohorič wins the photo finish in Poligny After a fast, nervous stage, Kasper Asgreen, Matej Mohorič and Ben O'Connor went clear on the Côte d'Ivory. O'Connor opened the sprint early and Mohorič beat Asgreen in a photo finish by the width of a rim – his third Tour stage win. In an emotional interview he paid tribute to his late teammate Gino Mäder. 20 Sat 22 Jul · Belfort → Le Markstein · Review Pogačar wins in Le Markstein as Pinot bids farewell In his last Tour, Thibaut Pinot attacked from the breakaway and crossed the Petit Ballon alone to the cheers of thousands of fans, but was caught on the Col du Platzerwasel. Pogačar won the sprint of a small group ahead of Felix Gall and Vingegaard, cutting his deficit to 7:29, and Giulio Ciccone secured the polka-dot jersey. After crashing early in the stage, Carlos Rodríguez lost fourth place overall to Simon Yates. 21 Sun 23 Jul · Saint-Quentin-en-Yvelines → Paris (Champs-Élysées) · Review Meeus pips Philipsen on the Champs-Élysées Pogačar made the first attack on the Paris circuit, but the stage ended in a sprint that Jordi Meeus won by a tyre's width from Jasper Philipsen, denying the green jersey a fifth stage win. Vingegaard crossed the line arm in arm with his teammates to seal his second Tour victory, 7:29 ahead of Pogačar and 10:56 ahead of Adam Yates.
