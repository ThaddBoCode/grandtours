Stories

The big stories of the Tour and the story of every stage – updated continuously.

Headlines

🏆Stage 20

Pogačar snatches yellow on the penultimate day

Tadej Pogačar started the time trial up La Planche des Belles Filles 57 seconds behind Primož Roglič and finished it as race leader, 59 seconds ahead – the first time since 2011 that the yellow jersey changed hands on the eve of Paris. On his Tour debut, aged 21, he became the first Slovenian to win the race, the youngest winner since the Second World War and the second-youngest ever.

🏆Stage 9

Roglič and Jumbo-Visma rule the race – until the time trial

Primož Roglič took the yellow jersey in Laruns on stage 9, the first Slovenian to wear it, and kept it for eleven stages. Jumbo-Visma controlled the mountains with Wout van Aert, Sepp Kuss, George Bennett and Tom Dumoulin setting the pace, and after the Col de la Loze Roglič led Pogačar by 57 seconds – before losing everything in the final time trial.

★2020

Three jerseys and three stage wins for Pogačar

Besides yellow, Pogačar won the mountains classification – taking the polka-dot jersey from Richard Carapaz in the final time trial – and the young rider classification, making him the first rider since Eddy Merckx to win three jerseys in one Tour. He won stages at Laruns, the Grand Colombier and La Planche des Belles Filles.

🔥Stage 15

Defending champion Bernal cracks and leaves the race

Egan Bernal was second overall after stage 9, but on the Grand Colombier he was dropped early and lost 7:20, ending INEOS Grenadiers' hopes of a sixth consecutive Tour win; he did not start stage 17. INEOS responded on stage 18 with a one-two for Michał Kwiatkowski and Richard Carapaz at La Roche-sur-Foron.

•2020

A Tour in September, raced inside a health bubble

Because of the COVID-19 pandemic the Tour was moved from its planned 27 June start to 29 August–20 September – for the first time since 1945 it was not held in July. Riders and staff lived in a health bubble with tests before the start and on rest days, and any team with two positive cases faced exclusion; race director Christian Prudhomme tested positive on the first rest day and had to isolate.

⚡Stage 21

Bennett beats Sagan to the green jersey

Sam Bennett won his first Tour stage on Île de Ré, took the green jersey that day and kept it to Paris, where he also won on the Champs-Élysées. Peter Sagan, chasing a record eighth points title, was relegated in Poitiers for shouldering Wout van Aert and could not close the gap – it was the first Tour he finished without winning the points classification.

🔥Stage 5

Alaphilippe loses yellow to a feeding penalty

Julian Alaphilippe won stage 2 in Nice to take the yellow jersey for the second time in his career, but after stage 5 he was given a 20-second penalty for taking an illegal feed inside the last 20 km. Adam Yates inherited the lead, saying nobody wanted to take the jersey like that, and held it for four stages until Laruns.

⚠️Stage 1

Rain and crashes mar the Grand Départ in Nice

The opening stage around Nice was raced on rain-soaked roads that brought crash after crash; the peloton agreed a truce, and a big pile-up still struck 3 km from the finish. Philippe Gilbert (broken kneecap) and Rafael Valls (broken leg) did not start the next day and John Degenkolb finished outside the time limit, while Alexander Kristoff won the sprint for the first yellow jersey.

•Stage 12

Hirschi lights up the race and is named most combative

Team Sunweb's Swiss rider Marc Hirschi was second in Nice, was caught 1.6 km from the line after a long solo to Laruns, and finally won alone at Sarran on stage 12. He was voted the Tour's most combative rider, and Sunweb took three stage wins in all, with Søren Kragh Andersen winning in Lyon and Champagnole.

✨Stage 17

New climbs: López conquers the Col de la Loze

The route featured four climbs used by the Tour for the first time – the Col de la Lusette, the Col de la Hourcère, the Suc au May and the Col de la Loze, at 2,304 m the highest point of the race – while the Grand Colombier hosted its first stage finish. Miguel Ángel López won alone on the Loze on the queen stage and also took the Souvenir Henri Desgrange, before dropping from third to sixth overall in the time trial.

Stage by stage

1 Sat 29 Aug · Nice ↻ · Review Kristoff wins a crash-marred opener in Nice Rain made the roads around Nice treacherous and caused numerous crashes, so the peloton called a truce; riders caught in a mass pile-up 3 km from the line were given the winner's time. Alexander Kristoff narrowly held off world champion Mads Pedersen and Cees Bol in the sprint to take the first yellow and green jerseys. Fabien Grellier took the polka-dot jersey and Pedersen the white one. 2 Sun 30 Aug · Nice ↻ · Review Alaphilippe attacks, wins in Nice and takes yellow On the last climb, the Col des Quatre Chemins, Julian Alaphilippe attacked and only Marc Hirschi and Adam Yates could join him. The trio stayed two seconds clear of the peloton and Alaphilippe beat Hirschi in the sprint to take the yellow jersey, 4 seconds ahead of Yates; Hirschi took white. Alexander Kristoff was dropped early and lost more than 28 minutes. 3 Mon 31 Aug · Nice → Sisteron · Review Ewan weaves through to win in Sisteron Jérôme Cousin rode alone at the front for over 100 km and was caught 16 km from the finish, while Anthony Perez, on course for the polka-dot jersey, crashed into his team car on a descent and had to abandon. Caleb Ewan came from far back and passed Sam Bennett on the line. Peter Sagan took the green jersey; Alaphilippe stayed in yellow. 4 Tue 1 Sept · Sisteron → Orcières-Merlette · Review Roglič wins the first summit finish at Orcières-Merlette Jumbo-Visma controlled the race's first summit finish, with Sepp Kuss setting a fierce tempo in the closing kilometres. Primož Roglič chased down Guillaume Martin's late attack and won ahead of Tadej Pogačar and Martin. Julian Alaphilippe finished fifth to keep yellow, and Pogačar took the white jersey. 5 Wed 2 Sept · Gap → Privas · Review Van Aert wins in Privas, Alaphilippe penalised out of yellow With no breakaway on a mostly downhill stage, Wout van Aert came off Cees Bol's wheel to win the slightly uphill sprint – his first Grand Tour stage win – ahead of Bol and Sam Bennett, who took the green jersey. After the stage Julian Alaphilippe was given a 20-second penalty for an illegal feed inside the last 20 km, and Adam Yates took the yellow jersey, 3 seconds ahead of Primož Roglič. 6 Thu 3 Sept · Le Teil → Mont Aigoual · Review Lutsenko solos to victory on Mont Aigoual An eight-man breakaway was allowed to fight out the stage, and Kazakh champion Alexey Lutsenko dropped Neilson Powless on the Col de la Lusette, a climb new to the Tour, to win alone. Jesús Herrada was second at 55 seconds and Greg Van Avermaet third at 2:15. The favourites finished together almost three minutes down, and Adam Yates kept yellow by 3 seconds over Primož Roglič. 7 Fri 4 Sept · Millau → Lavaur · Review Van Aert wins again as crosswinds catch out Pogačar Bora–Hansgrohe set a fierce pace from the start to drop Sam Bennett and other sprinters, and later INEOS Grenadiers used the crosswinds to split the peloton. Wout van Aert won his second stage in three days ahead of Edvald Boasson Hagen and Bryan Coquard. Tadej Pogačar, Mikel Landa, Richie Porte and Bauke Mollema were caught behind and lost 1:21; Peter Sagan took back green and Egan Bernal the white jersey. 8 Sat 5 Sept · Cazères → Loudenvielle · Review Peters wins in Loudenvielle as Pinot's hopes fade Nans Peters dropped Ilnur Zakarin on the descent of the Port de Balès and held on over the Peyresourde to win from a 13-man break, 47 seconds ahead of Toms Skujiņš and Carlos Verona. Behind, Tadej Pogačar attacked on the Peyresourde and gained around 40 seconds on the yellow-jersey group. Thibaut Pinot, suffering from back pain, lost almost 19 minutes, while Adam Yates kept yellow by 3 seconds. 9 Sun 6 Sept · Pau → Laruns · Review Pogačar wins in Laruns, Roglič takes yellow Marc Hirschi attacked about 90 km from the finish and rode alone over the Col de Marie Blanque, only to be caught 1.6 km from the line by Tadej Pogačar, Primož Roglič, Egan Bernal and Mikel Landa. Pogačar won the five-man sprint ahead of Roglič and Hirschi, becoming the youngest Tour stage winner in 27 years. Roglič took the yellow jersey, the first Slovenian to wear it, 21 seconds ahead of Bernal, while Adam Yates dropped to eighth. 10 Tue 8 Sept · Île d'Oléron → Île de Ré · Review Bennett wins his first Tour stage and takes green On the island-to-island stage from Oléron to Ré, crashes hit the peloton: Sam Bewley abandoned with a broken wrist, and Pogačar's teammate Davide Formolo fractured his collarbone and left the race after the stage. Led out by Michael Mørkøv, Sam Bennett held off Caleb Ewan and Peter Sagan for his first Tour de France stage win and took the green jersey. Primož Roglič stayed in yellow. 11 Wed 9 Sept · Châtelaillon-Plage → Poitiers · Review Ewan wins a photo finish in Poitiers, Sagan relegated After Mathieu Ladagnous's solo breakaway was caught, four riders threw their bikes at the line and Caleb Ewan won his second stage of the Tour. Peter Sagan, initially second, was relegated for shouldering Wout van Aert in the sprint, so Sam Bennett moved up to second and extended his green-jersey lead to 68 points. Ion Izagirre abandoned after a crash 30 km from the finish. 12 Thu 10 Sept · Chauvigny → Sarran · Review Hirschi finally gets his reward with a solo win in Sarran On the longest stage of the race, 218 km, Marc Hirschi attacked on the Suc au May, a climb new to the Tour, about 26 km from the finish. After coming close on stages 2 and 9, the Team Sunweb rider won alone, 47 seconds ahead of Pierre Rolland, while his teammate Søren Kragh Andersen led in the next group at 52 seconds. There were no changes at the top of the general classification. 13 Fri 11 Sept · Châtel-Guyon → Puy Mary · Review Martínez wins on Puy Mary as the Slovenians break clear From a 17-rider breakaway, Daniel Martínez beat Lennard Kämna by 4 seconds on the steep climb to Puy Mary, with Maximilian Schachmann third. Behind, Tadej Pogačar and Primož Roglič broke clear of the favourites on the Pas de Peyrol; Pogačar moved up to second overall, 44 seconds down, and retook the white jersey, while Egan Bernal lost 38 seconds and fell to third. Bauke Mollema abandoned after a crash, and Romain Bardet, concussed in a fall, did not start the next day. 14 Sat 12 Sept · Clermont-Ferrand → Lyon · Review Kragh Andersen escapes in the finale in Lyon Bora–Hansgrohe drove the pace on the Col du Béal to drop Sam Bennett and other sprinters. In a finale full of attacks, Søren Kragh Andersen went clear 3 km from the finish and won alone by 15 seconds; Luka Mezgec won the sprint for second ahead of Simone Consonni and Peter Sagan. Roglič kept yellow and Bennett the green jersey. 15 Sun 13 Sept · Lyon → Grand Colombier · Review Pogačar wins on the Grand Colombier as Bernal cracks The Grand Colombier hosted a stage finish for the first time, and Jumbo-Visma's relentless pace on the final climb dropped Egan Bernal, Nairo Quintana and Guillaume Martin. In the last kilometre Richie Porte and Primož Roglič accelerated, but Tadej Pogačar came past Roglič to win, with Porte third at 5 seconds. Defending champion Bernal lost 7:20, and Roglič led Pogačar by 40 seconds going into the second rest day. 16 Tue 15 Sept · La Tour-du-Pin → Villard-de-Lans · Review Kämna makes up for Puy Mary with a solo win at Villard-de-Lans A breakaway of more than 20 riders built a lead of over 12 minutes, and Richard Carapaz's repeated accelerations on the climb to Saint-Nizier-du-Moucherotte broke it apart. Lennard Kämna attacked just before the summit and won alone, 1:27 ahead of Carapaz and 1:56 ahead of Sébastien Reichenbach. Apart from Tom Dumoulin and Nairo Quintana, the top ten finished together and the first eight places overall were unchanged. 17 Wed 16 Sept · Grenoble → Méribel (Col de la Loze) · Review López conquers the Col de la Loze on the queen stage Defending champion Egan Bernal did not start. On the Col de la Loze, climbed for the first time and at 2,304 m the highest point of the race, Miguel Ángel López attacked 2.5 km from the line and won 15 seconds ahead of Primož Roglič and 30 ahead of Tadej Pogačar. López moved up to third overall, Roglič extended his lead to 57 seconds, and Pogačar took the polka-dot jersey from Benoît Cosnefroy. 18 Thu 17 Sept · Méribel → La Roche-sur-Foron · Review Kwiatkowski and Carapaz cross the line together for INEOS Michał Kwiatkowski and Richard Carapaz went clear from a large breakaway and rode in together, Carapaz leaving the stage win to his teammate – Kwiatkowski's first individual Grand Tour stage win – while taking the polka-dot jersey himself. Marc Hirschi crashed on a descent but was named the day's most combative rider. Mikel Landa attacked on the Plateau des Glières and Richie Porte punctured on the gravel section that followed, but the top four overall were unchanged. 19 Fri 18 Sept · Bourg-en-Bresse → Champagnole · Review Kragh Andersen doubles up in Champagnole After Rémi Cavagna's early solo attack was neutralised, a 12-rider group including Sam Bennett, Peter Sagan and Matteo Trentin went clear in the finale. Søren Kragh Andersen attacked 16 km from the finish and won alone by 53 seconds ahead of Luka Mezgec and Jasper Stuyven – his second stage win of this Tour. The general classification remained unchanged before the time trial. 20 Sat 19 Sept · Lure → La Planche des Belles Filles · Review Pogačar overturns Roglič on La Planche des Belles Filles Starting 57 seconds behind his compatriot, Tadej Pogačar won the 36.2 km time trial by 1:21 over Tom Dumoulin and Richie Porte, while Primož Roglič finished fifth, 1:56 down. Pogačar took the yellow jersey with a 59-second lead, also claimed the polka-dot jersey from Richard Carapaz and kept the white one; Porte moved up to third overall. It was the first time since 2011 that the yellow jersey changed hands on the eve of the finish in Paris. 21 Sun 20 Sept · Mantes-la-Jolie → Paris (Champs-Élysées) · Review Bennett wins on the Champs-Élysées, Pogačar wins the Tour Sam Bennett won the sprint on the Champs-Élysées ahead of Mads Pedersen and Peter Sagan to seal the green jersey. Tadej Pogačar finished safely in the bunch to become the first Slovenian winner of the Tour, 59 seconds ahead of Primož Roglič and 3:30 ahead of Richie Porte, and also won the mountains and young rider classifications. Movistar won the team classification.
