Legendary places on the route

Climb · Savoie

Les Saisies

1,650 m 14.8 km · 5 %
Stage 18 · Marc Hirschi

The Col des Saisies links the Val d'Arly with the Beaufortain, and the ski resort of the same name, known for cross-country skiing, sits on the pass. The Tour has crossed it 14 times as a classified climb; a stage finish at Les Saisies is announced for the first time in 2027 (stage 11).

 Climb · Pyrénées-Atlantiques

Col de Soudet

1,533 m 22 km · 5.5 %
Stage 9 · Marc Hirschi

The Col du Soudet lies near the Spanish border below the La Pierre-Saint-Martin ski station. It links the Barétous valley with the Soule.

 Climb · Ain

Grand Colombier

1,498 m 18.3 km · 6.9 %
Stage 15

The Grand Colombier at the southern end of the Jura is considered one of the hardest climbs in France, with four ascents and ramps above 20% on the western side. The Tour first came in 2012, with summit finishes in 2020 and 2023; another is planned for 2027 (stage 9).

 Climb · Pyrénées-Atlantiques

Col de Marie Blanque

1,035 m 9.3 km · 7.7 %
Stage 9 · Marc Hirschi

The Col de Marie-Blanque (1,035 m) links the Aspe and Ossau valleys. The western side from Escot is short but very steep over its final four kilometres.

 Finish · Paris

Paris Champs-Élysées

Stage 21

Since 1975 the Tour de France has finished on the Champs-Élysées, usually with a bunch sprint after several laps. The only exception was 2024, when the race ended in Nice because of the Olympics.
