Stories

The big stories of the Tour and the story of every stage – updated continuously.

Headlines

🏆2024

Pogačar wins his third Tour and the Giro–Tour double

Tadej Pogačar won the Tour for the third time after 2020 and 2021, following two second places behind Jonas Vingegaard. Having already won the Giro d'Italia, he became the first rider since Marco Pantani in 1998 to win both races in the same year. He won six stages, including the last three, and finished 6:17 ahead of Vingegaard and 9:18 ahead of Remco Evenepoel.

🏆Stage 15

Pyrenean double: Pogačar beats Pantani's Plateau de Beille record

In yellow since stage 4, Pogačar tightened his grip on the race in the Pyrenees, winning at Pla d'Adet and Plateau de Beille with Vingegaard second and Evenepoel third both times. His time on the climb to Plateau de Beille beat the record Marco Pantani had set in 1998, and his lead grew to 3:09 over Vingegaard and 5:19 over Evenepoel.

★Stage 5

Cavendish breaks Merckx's record with his 35th stage win

Mark Cavendish won stage 5 in Saint-Vulbas for his 35th Tour de France stage win, breaking the record of 34 he had shared with Eddy Merckx. It was his last Tour. On the final podium in Nice he received a special presentation: a framed black jersey bearing the letters CAXXXV.

🔥Stage 11

Vingegaard returns from his crash to beat Pogačar at Le Lioran

Two-time defending champion Jonas Vingegaard had not raced since a heavy crash at the Tour of the Basque Country in April, in which he suffered a punctured lung among other injuries. On stage 11 he caught Pogačar after his attack on the Puy Mary and beat him in the sprint at Le Lioran. He finished second overall, 6:17 behind.

✨Stage 7

Debutant Evenepoel wins the time trial and finishes third

In his first Tour de France, world time trial champion Remco Evenepoel won the stage 7 time trial in Burgundy, the only rider to take time out of Pogačar that day. He finished third overall, 9:18 behind, and won the young rider classification.

⚡2024

Girmay makes history and wins the green jersey

Biniam Girmay won stage 3 in Turin to become the first Black African to win a Tour stage, and on stage 5 he became the first African to wear the green jersey. He added wins in Colombey-les-Deux-Églises and Villeneuve-sur-Lot and kept green all the way to Nice with 387 points, ahead of Jasper Philipsen (354), who also won three stages.

⚠️Stage 12

Crashes end the Tour for Roglič and Pedersen

Primož Roglič, one of the pre-race 'Big Four', crashed on stage 11 and again about 12 km from the finish of stage 12, losing almost two and a half minutes; he did not start the next day. Mads Pedersen, who crashed in the stage 5 sprint, did not start stage 8. In all, 141 of the 176 starters reached Nice.

•2024

Carapaz: first Ecuadorian in yellow, then king of the mountains

Olympic road race champion Richard Carapaz took the yellow jersey in Turin on stage 3, the first Ecuadorian to wear it. He later won stage 17 at SuperDévoluy, took the polka-dot jersey on stage 19 and kept it to Nice, and was named the most combative rider of the race.

•Stage 21

From Florence to Nice: an Italian start and no finish in Paris

Italy hosted the Grand Départ for the first time, 100 years after Ottavio Bottecchia's first Italian Tour win, and San Marino became the 14th country visited by a Tour stage. Because of the Paris Olympics, the Tour did not finish in Paris for the first time; it ended instead with a time trial from Monaco to Nice, the first final-stage time trial since 1989.

🔥Stage 1

Bardet takes his first yellow jersey in his final Tour

In what he had announced as his last Tour, Romain Bardet attacked about 50 km from the finish of the opening stage, joined his teammate Frank van den Broek and won in Rimini 5 seconds ahead of the peloton. It was the first time he had worn the yellow jersey; he lost it to Pogačar the next day in Bologna.

Stage by stage

1 Sat 29 Jun · Florence → Rimini · Review Bardet and van den Broek hold off the bunch, Bardet in yellow Romain Bardet attacked on the climb of San Leo about 50 km from the finish and joined his breakaway teammate Frank van den Broek. The dsm–firmenich PostNL duo held off the favourites by 5 seconds in Rimini, and Bardet took the first yellow jersey of his career in his last Tour. Van den Broek led the points and young rider classifications, Jonas Abrahamsen the mountains classification. 2 Sun 30 Jun · Cesenatico → Bologna · Review Vauquelin wins solo on San Luca, Pogačar takes yellow Kévin Vauquelin went clear of the breakaway on the second ascent of San Luca and won alone in Bologna, 36 seconds ahead of Jonas Abrahamsen. Behind, Tadej Pogačar attacked on the climb and only Jonas Vingegaard could follow; Remco Evenepoel and Richard Carapaz rejoined them just before the line. Pogačar took yellow from the dropped Bardet on the same time as those three, and Abrahamsen also moved into the lead of the points classification. 3 Mon 1 Jul · Piacenza → Turin · Review Girmay makes history in Turin, Carapaz first Ecuadorian in yellow A crash 2.3 km from the finish disrupted the sprint in Turin at the end of the longest stage of the race, and Biniam Girmay beat Fernando Gaviria and Arnaud De Lie. He became the first Black African to win a Tour stage and the first Eritrean stage winner. Richard Carapaz, level on time with Pogačar, took yellow thanks to his better finishing positions, the first Ecuadorian to wear it. 4 Tue 2 Jul · Pinerolo → Valloire · Review Pogačar attacks on the Galibier and retakes yellow in Valloire UAE Team Emirates set a fierce pace on the Col du Galibier, where Carapaz was dropped, and Tadej Pogačar attacked less than a kilometre from the summit; only Jonas Vingegaard could follow at first. Pogačar descended alone to Valloire and won 35 seconds ahead of Remco Evenepoel, Juan Ayuso and Primož Roglič, with Vingegaard two seconds further back. With the time bonuses he retook yellow, 45 seconds ahead of Evenepoel and 50 ahead of Vingegaard. 5 Wed 3 Jul · Saint-Jean-de-Maurienne → Saint-Vulbas · Review Cavendish takes his 35th Tour stage win and the record On the flat run to Saint-Vulbas, Mark Cavendish won the sprint ahead of Jasper Philipsen and Alexander Kristoff for his 35th Tour de France stage win, breaking the record he had shared with Eddy Merckx. Mads Pedersen crashed in the final metres, and Phil Bauhaus was relegated for a dangerous change of line. Biniam Girmay took the green jersey, the first African rider to wear it. 6 Thu 4 Jul · Mâcon → Dijon · Review Groenewegen wins by a tyre's width in windy Dijon Echelons in the crosswind split the peloton for a while, catching out Carapaz, Ayuso and Philipsen among others, but the groups came back together. In Dijon Dylan Groenewegen won the sprint by a tyre's width; Jasper Philipsen crossed the line second but was relegated for impeding Wout van Aert, leaving second place to Biniam Girmay. Pogačar kept yellow. 7 Fri 5 Jul · Nuits-Saint-Georges → Gevrey-Chambertin · Review Evenepoel wins the time trial, Pogačar limits the damage World time trial champion Remco Evenepoel won the 25.3 km test through the Burgundy vineyards for his first Tour de France stage win, 12 seconds ahead of Tadej Pogačar. Primož Roglič was third at 34 seconds and Jonas Vingegaard fourth at 37. Pogačar kept yellow with 33 seconds over Evenepoel and 1:15 over Vingegaard. 8 Sat 6 Jul · Semur-en-Auxois → Colombey-les-Deux-Églises · Review Girmay doubles up in green at Colombey-les-Deux-Églises Jonas Abrahamsen spent much of the day alone at the front but was caught in the finale. In the slightly uphill sprint, green jersey Biniam Girmay took his second stage win ahead of Jasper Philipsen and Arnaud De Lie. Mads Pedersen did not start, not having recovered from his stage 5 crash. 9 Sun 7 Jul · Troyes ↻ · Review Turgis wins the gravel stage around Troyes On a stage with 14 gravel sectors around Troyes, the breakaway stayed away and Anthony Turgis won the sprint ahead of Tom Pidcock and Derek Gee. Tadej Pogačar and Remco Evenepoel attacked repeatedly, but Jonas Vingegaard and the other favourites always came back. The top of the general classification was unchanged going into the first rest day. 10 Tue 9 Jul · Orléans → Saint-Amand-Montrond · Review Philipsen finally gets his win in Saint-Amand-Montrond After a quiet stage from Orléans, Jasper Philipsen, led out by Mathieu van der Poel, won the bunch sprint for his first stage win of this Tour. Green jersey Biniam Girmay was second and Pascal Ackermann third. There were no changes at the top of the classifications. 11 Wed 10 Jul · Évaux-les-Bains → Le Lioran · Review Vingegaard catches Pogačar and wins in Le Lioran On the toughest stage so far, in the Massif Central, Tadej Pogačar attacked just below the top of the Puy Mary and built a lead, but Jonas Vingegaard counter-attacked and caught him before the summit of the Col de Pertus. Vingegaard won the two-up sprint in Le Lioran by half a wheel – his first win since his life-threatening crash in April – while Remco Evenepoel and Primož Roglič finished 25 seconds back. Evenepoel moved to 1:06 behind Pogačar and Vingegaard to 1:14, and Pogačar also took the lead of the mountains classification. 12 Thu 11 Jul · Aurillac → Villeneuve-sur-Lot · Review Girmay's third win as a crash ends Roglič's Tour Green jersey Biniam Girmay won his third stage in Villeneuve-sur-Lot ahead of Wout van Aert and Pascal Ackermann, while Arnaud Démare and Mark Cavendish were relegated for irregular sprinting. A crash about 12 km from the finish brought down Primož Roglič, who lost almost two and a half minutes and dropped to sixth overall, 4:42 behind. Roglič did not start the next day. 13 Fri 12 Jul · Agen → Pau · Review Philipsen wins a contested sprint in Pau With Primož Roglič a non-starter and Juan Ayuso abandoning during the stage, crosswinds again briefly split the peloton before the race came back together for a sprint in Pau. Jasper Philipsen won ahead of Wout van Aert, Pascal Ackermann and Biniam Girmay, after a heavy crash 700 m from the line had held up many riders. Rivals criticised Philipsen's sprint, and Ackermann refused to shake his hand. 14 Sat 13 Jul · Pau → Saint-Lary-Soulan (Pla d'Adet) · Review Pogačar strikes at Pla d'Adet, Vingegaard moves up to second After the Tourmalet and the Hourquette d'Ancizan, Tadej Pogačar attacked just under 5 km from the summit finish at Pla d'Adet, passing Ben Healy and his teammate Adam Yates. He won 39 seconds ahead of Jonas Vingegaard, with Remco Evenepoel a further 31 seconds back. Vingegaard moved up to second overall at 1:57, while Evenepoel dropped to third at 2:22. 15 Sun 14 Jul · Loudenvielle → Plateau de Beille · Review Pogačar breaks Pantani's Plateau de Beille record On Bastille Day, Jonas Vingegaard attacked on the final climb to Plateau de Beille, but Tadej Pogačar followed and went clear himself about 5 km from the top. He won 1:08 ahead of Vingegaard, with Remco Evenepoel third at 2:51, and his time for the ascent beat the record set by Marco Pantani in 1998. Pogačar now led Vingegaard by 3:09 and Evenepoel by 5:19. 16 Tue 16 Jul · Gruissan → Nîmes · Review Philipsen's third win in Nîmes as Girmay crashes Thomas Gachignard rode alone at the front for much of the second half but was caught 25 km from Nîmes. Green jersey Biniam Girmay crashed 1.5 km from the finish, and Jasper Philipsen, led out by Robbe Ghys and Mathieu van der Poel, won ahead of Phil Bauhaus and Alexander Kristoff. It was Philipsen's third stage win, matching Girmay's tally. 17 Wed 17 Jul · Saint-Paul-Trois-Châteaux → SuperDévoluy · Review Carapaz wins solo at SuperDévoluy Richard Carapaz went clear of the chasing group on the Col du Noyer, caught and dropped Simon Yates and won alone at SuperDévoluy, 37 seconds ahead of Yates and 57 ahead of Enric Mas. It was his first Tour stage win, making him a stage winner in all three Grand Tours. Among the favourites, Remco Evenepoel gained a few seconds on Pogačar and Vingegaard. 18 Thu 18 Jul · Gap → Barcelonnette · Review Campenaerts outsprints his breakaway rivals in Barcelonnette A breakaway of more than 30 riders went clear early, and on the last climb Michał Kwiatkowski attacked, later joined by Mattéo Vercher and Victor Campenaerts. Campenaerts won the three-man sprint in Barcelonnette ahead of Vercher and Kwiatkowski for his first Tour stage win. The yellow jersey group finished more than 13 minutes down, with no change in the overall standings. 19 Fri 19 Jul · Embrun → Isola 2000 · Review Pogačar reels in the breakaway at Isola 2000 After the Col de Vars and the Cime de la Bonette, Tadej Pogačar attacked 10 km from the summit finish at Isola 2000 and caught the last escapee, Matteo Jorgenson, 1.9 km from the line. He won 21 seconds ahead of Jorgenson, with Simon Yates third, and extended his lead over Vingegaard to 5:03. Remco Evenepoel could not drop Jonas Vingegaard, and Richard Carapaz took the polka-dot jersey from the breakaway. 20 Sat 20 Jul · Nice → Col de la Couillole · Review Pogačar wins on the Couillole, Vingegaard secures second On the last mountain stage, Remco Evenepoel attacked twice on the final climb to the Col de la Couillole, but Jonas Vingegaard countered and only Tadej Pogačar could follow. The pair caught Enric Mas and Richard Carapaz 2.5 km from the top, and Pogačar launched his sprint 150 m from the line to win 7 seconds ahead of Vingegaard. Evenepoel lost 53 seconds, which secured second overall for Vingegaard. 21 Sun 21 Jul · Monaco → Nice · Review Pogačar wins the Monaco–Nice time trial to seal his third Tour For the first time since 1989 the Tour ended with a time trial, and for the first time ever it did not finish in Paris. Tadej Pogačar won the 33.7 km from Monaco to Nice 1:03 ahead of Jonas Vingegaard and 1:14 ahead of Remco Evenepoel, his sixth stage win of the race. He took the overall victory by 6:17 over Vingegaard and 9:18 over Evenepoel, with Biniam Girmay in green, Richard Carapaz in polka dots and Evenepoel in white.
