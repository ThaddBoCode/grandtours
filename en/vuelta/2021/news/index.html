Stories

The big stories of the Vuelta and the story of every stage – updated continuously.

Headlines

★2021

Roglič wins a third straight Vuelta

Primož Roglič won the Vuelta by 4' 42" over Enric Mas, with Jack Haig third at 7' 40". He is only the third rider to win the race in three successive years and took stages 1, 11, 17 and 21. His margin was the largest since Alex Zülle won by 5' 07" in 1997.

🔥Stage 10

Eiking holds the red jersey for seven days

On stage 10 Odd Christian Eiking finished in the breakaway and took the red jersey, the day Roglič crashed on a descent. He kept it through stage 16 and lost it only on stage 17, when he was dropped on the way to the Lagos de Covadonga and Roglič took over.

⚠️Stage 7

Crashes and abandons: only 142 of 184 reach Santiago

142 riders finished the race, 42 fewer than started. On stage 7 Alejandro Valverde (fractured collarbone after a crash) and Hugh Carthy abandoned; after the stage 16 crash Giulio Ciccone, Rudy Molard and Sep Vanmarcke left the race, Louis Meintjes followed on stage 19 while tenth overall, and Miguel Ángel López on stage 20.

🔥Stage 20

Haig steps up after Landa's exit to take third; Mäder wins white

Jack Haig started as a domestique for Mikel Landa, who struggled and abandoned on stage 17; Haig then became team leader. On stage 20 he moved past López into third overall, Gino Mäder won the white jersey and finished fifth, and Bahrain Victorious took the team classification.

⚡Stage 16

Jakobsen wins three sprints and the green jersey

Fabio Jakobsen won stages 4, 8 and 16 and the points classification with 250 points, ahead of Roglič (199) and Magnus Cort (161). Jasper Philipsen had won stages 2 and 5 but withdrew before stage 11 with a mild fever.

•Stage 20

Storer takes two stages and the mountains jersey

Michael Storer won stages 7 and 10 from the breakaway. He took the mountains jersey on stage 18 and finished the mountains classification with 80 points, ahead of Romain Bardet (61) and Roglič (51).

•Stage 19

Cort wins three stages and the combativity award

Magnus Cort won stages 6, 12 and 19 and was second in the final-day time trial, 14 seconds behind Roglič. He was also given the overall award for the most combative rider of the Vuelta.

🔥Stage 14

Carapaz abandons, Bernal only sixth: Ineos fall short

Richard Carapaz, the recently crowned Olympic road race champion, withdrew on stage 14 suffering from fatigue in the high temperatures. Giro winner Egan Bernal wore the white jersey from stage 3 to 19 and attacked on stage 17, but lost almost seven minutes on stage 20 and finished sixth overall.

✨Stage 18

New climb Altu d'El Gamoniteiru makes its Vuelta debut

Because of the COVID-19 pandemic the Vuelta stayed entirely within Spain, from Burgos to a time trial in Santiago de Compostela. On stage 18 the Altu d'El Gamoniteiru featured for the first time in Vuelta history, and Miguel Ángel López won there solo.

Stage by stage

1 Sat 14 Aug · Burgos ↻ · Review Roglič opens in red with the Burgos time trial Defending champion Primož Roglič won the 7.1 km time trial in Burgos in 8' 32" as the last rider off the ramp and took the first red jersey. Alex Aranburu, who sat in the hot seat for most of the day, was second at 6", Jan Tratnik third at 8". 2 Sun 15 Aug · Caleruega → Burgos · Review Philipsen beats Jakobsen in the Burgos sprint Jasper Philipsen came around Juan Sebastián Molano to win the sprint ahead of Fabio Jakobsen and Michael Matthews, and took the green jersey. The breakaway of Diego Rubio was caught 20 km out, and Roglič kept the red jersey. 3 Mon 16 Aug · Santo Domingo de Silos → Espinosa de los Monteros – Picón Blanco · Review Taaramäe wins at Picón Blanco and takes red Rein Taaramäe dropped his companions from the breakaway on the first summit finish and won 21 seconds ahead of Joe Dombrowski. He took both the red jersey and the mountains lead, with Kenny Elissonde third at 36". 4 Tue 17 Aug · El Burgo de Osma → Molina de Aragón · Review Jakobsen wins and takes green; Taaramäe survives crash Fabio Jakobsen outsprinted Arnaud Démare for the win and took the green jersey from Philipsen. Red jersey Taaramäe crashed 2.2 km from the line but, inside the final 3 km, was credited with the peloton's time and kept the lead. 5 Wed 18 Aug · Tarancón → Albacete · Review Philipsen wins in Albacete; Elissonde takes red Jasper Philipsen won the sprint ahead of Jakobsen and Alberto Dainese, after a big crash 11 km from the finish had caught Romain Bardet and Taaramäe among others. Kenny Elissonde took the red jersey, five seconds ahead of Roglič. 6 Thu 19 Aug · Requena → Alto de Cullera · Review Cort holds off Roglič; Roglič retakes red Magnus Cort dropped his breakaway companions inside the final kilometre and held on up the ramp to Cullera, with Roglič only bridging up at the line for the same time in second. Elissonde lost four and a half minutes, so Roglič took the red jersey back. 7 Fri 20 Aug · Gandía → Balcón de Alicante · Review Storer solos at Alicante; Valverde crashes out Michael Storer made his decisive move 3.3 km out from a 29-rider breakaway and won 21 seconds ahead of Carlos Verona, while Pavel Sivakov took the mountains lead. Alejandro Valverde crashed heavily on a descent (fractured collarbone) and abandoned, as did Hugh Carthy; Roglič stayed in red. 8 Sat 21 Aug · Santa Pola → La Manga del Mar Menor · Review Jakobsen wins again and takes back green Despite Astana pushing the pace for echelons 38 km out, which briefly split the bunch into three groups, the stage ended in a mass sprint: Fabio Jakobsen won ahead of Alberto Dainese and Philipsen. He took the green jersey back from Philipsen, while Roglič kept the red jersey. 9 Sun 22 Aug · Puerto Lumbreras → Alto de Velefique · Review Caruso wins at Velefique; Roglič extends his lead Damiano Caruso dropped his breakaway companions 71 km out and held off the GC group to win solo, taking the mountains jersey. Roglič sprinted to second at 1' 05", one second ahead of Enric Mas; Mikel Landa lost five minutes, and Mas was the only rider within a minute of Roglič going into the rest day. 10 Tue 24 Aug · Roquetas de Mar → Rincón de la Victoria · Review Storer solos again; Eiking takes the red jersey Michael Storer dropped his breakaway companions on the Puerto de Almáchar and soloed to a second stage win. Roglič attacked on the climb but crashed on the descent and was caught; Odd Christian Eiking, part of the break, took the red jersey, with Roglič 2' 17" down. 11 Wed 25 Aug · Antequera → Valdepeñas de Jaén · Review Roglič beats Mas in Valdepeñas; Eiking stays in red Magnus Cort was caught with 200 m to go and Roglič powered away from Enric Mas to win his second stage of the race (Mas at 3"). Eiking finished 11 seconds down and kept the red jersey; Philipsen (fever) and Alex Aranburu (crash) had both withdrawn before the start. 12 Thu 26 Aug · Jaén → Córdoba · Review Cort wins in Córdoba by half a wheel Magnus Cort, led out by Jens Keukeleire, held off Andrea Bagioli in the sprint by half a wheel for his second stage win, with Michael Matthews third. The top ten was unchanged and Eiking kept the red jersey. 13 Fri 27 Aug · Belmez → Villanueva de la Serena · Review Sénéchal wins his first Grand Tour stage Deceuninck–Quick-Step set such a fast pace in the technical finale that sprinter Jakobsen was dropped, so lead-out man Florian Sénéchal sprinted himself and held off Matteo Trentin for his first Grand Tour stage win. Eiking stayed in the red jersey. 14 Sat 28 Aug · Don Benito → Pico Villuercas · Review Bardet wins at Pico Villuercas; Carapaz abandons Romain Bardet emerged as the strongest of an 18-man break, dropped everyone on the final climb and won 44 seconds ahead of Jesús Herrada and Jay Vine, taking the mountains lead. Richard Carapaz abandoned, suffering in the high temperatures, and Eiking lost 20 seconds but stayed in red. 15 Sun 29 Aug · Navalmoral de la Mata → El Barraco · Review Majka wins solo; Eiking keeps red before the rest day Rafał Majka dropped Fabio Aru with 87 km to go and soloed to the finish in El Barraco, with Steven Kruijswijk second at 1' 27". The GC group stayed quiet apart from Adam Yates, who gained 15 seconds on the last climb, and Eiking kept the red jersey into the second rest day. 16 Tue 31 Aug · Laredo → Santa Cruz de Bezana · Review Jakobsen wins a third stage; crash victims abandon Fabio Jakobsen won his third stage of the race in the sprint, ahead of Jordi Meeus and Matteo Trentin. After a crash a few kilometres after the start, Giulio Ciccone, Rudy Molard and Sep Vanmarcke abandoned the race; Eiking kept the red jersey. 17 Wed 1 Sept · Unquera → Lagos de Covadonga · Review Roglič wins solo at Covadonga and retakes red Egan Bernal attacked 61 km out and Roglič followed immediately, then dropped him with 7.5 km to go and soloed to his third stage win. Eiking was dropped, so Roglič took the red jersey with a 2' 22" lead over Mas; teammate Sepp Kuss led home the chase group at 1' 35", denying the contenders the second-place bonus. 18 Thu 2 Sept · Salas → Altu d'El Gamoniteiru · Review López wins on the new Gamoniteiru; Roglič second Michael Storer rode 72 km solo off the front before David de la Cruz caught him, and Miguel Ángel López then attacked from the GC group to drop de la Cruz and win solo. Roglič outsprinted Mas and Bernal for second at 14", extending his lead to 2' 30", and Storer took the mountains jersey. 19 Fri 3 Sept · Tapia → Monforte de Lemos · Review Cort wins a third stage; Meintjes crashes out of the top 10 Magnus Cort won the sprint of a seven-man breakaway that stayed about half a minute clear of the peloton, ahead of Rui Oliveira and Quinn Simmons, for his third stage win. With 43 km to go a crash forced Louis Meintjes, tenth on GC, to abandon, and David de la Cruz moved into the top ten. 20 Sat 4 Sept · Sanxenxo → Mos (Castro de Herville) · Review Champoussin wins; López quits and Haig takes third Clément Champoussin accelerated from behind inside the final 2 km while the favourites looked at each other, and held on at Castro de Herville for his first Grand Tour stage win, six seconds ahead of Roglič. López abandoned mid-stage, Jack Haig moved into third overall, Egan Bernal lost almost seven minutes and Gino Mäder took the white jersey. 21 Sun 5 Sept · Padrón → Santiago de Compostela · Review Roglič wins the final time trial and the Vuelta As the last starter Roglič won the 33.8 km time trial from Padrón to Santiago de Compostela in 44' 02", 14 seconds ahead of Magnus Cort, taking his fourth stage and confirming a third straight overall win. Jakobsen (green), Storer (mountains) and Mäder (white) secured their jerseys, and Bahrain Victorious won the team classification.
