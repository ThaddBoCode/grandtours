Stories

The big stories of the Vuelta and the story of every stage – updated continuously.

Headlines

🏆2020

Roglič wins a second straight Vuelta by 24 seconds

Defending champion Primož Roglič (Jumbo–Visma) won the 2020 Vuelta in 72:46:12, 24 seconds ahead of Richard Carapaz, with Hugh Carthy third at 1:15. Roglič took four stages (1, 8, 10 and 13) and the green points jersey as well.

🏆2020

The red jersey changes hands four times between Roglič and Carapaz

Roglič wore the red jersey after stages 1–5; Carapaz took it on stage 6 and held it through stage 9. Roglič regained it on stage 10, it went back to Carapaz on the Angliru (stage 12), and Roglič took it for good in the stage 13 time trial and kept it to Madrid.

🏆Stage 13

Roglič turns the Vuelta around in the Ézaro mountain time trial

Before the 33.7 km mountain time trial Roglič sat 10 seconds behind race leader Carapaz. He won in 46:39 with Will Barta second (+0:01) and Carthy fourth (+0:25), and moved back into the lead 39 seconds ahead of Carapaz and 47 ahead of Carthy.

🏆Stage 17

Roglič survives Carapaz's attack on La Covatilla

David Gaudu won the final mountain stage, but the race was decided behind him: Carapaz cut Roglič's lead from 45 to 24 seconds and could do no more.

🔥2020

COVID-19 cuts the Vuelta to 18 stages, starting in Irún

The Vuelta was moved from August to the autumn and ran from 20 October to 8 November. The planned start in Utrecht fell away; the three Dutch stages were cancelled, so the race began in Irún. It was not 21 stages long for the first time since 1985, and stage 6 had to be changed because of French COVID-19 restrictions (finishing at Aramón Formigal instead of the Col du Tourmalet).

🔥Stage 12

Carthy wins on the Angliru, Carapaz back in red

On the 109.4 km stage finishing atop the Alto de L'Angliru, Hugh Carthy beat Aleksandr Vlasov, Enric Mas and Richard Carapaz (all +0:16). Roglič finished 26 seconds behind Carthy, Carapaz took the red jersey with a 10-second lead, and Carthy rose to third overall.

🔥Stage 9

Bennett relegated, Ackermann promoted to the stage 9 win

Sam Bennett (Deceuninck–Quick-Step) first won the sprint in Aguilar de Campoo but was relegated by the race jury for aggressively shoulder-barging Emīls Liepiņš in the run-in. Pascal Ackermann (Bora–Hansgrohe) was promoted to the winner of the stage.

•Stage 11

David Gaudu wins mountain stages 11 and 17

Frenchman David Gaudu (Groupama–FDJ) won stage 11 at the Alto de la Farrapona ahead of Marc Soler (+0:04) and stage 17 at La Covatilla ahead of Gino Mäder (+0:28). He finished eighth overall (+7:45) and second in the young rider classification behind Enric Mas.

•2020

G. Martin wins the mountains, Mas the youth, Movistar the teams

Guillaume Martin (Cofidis) won the mountains classification with 99 points. Enric Mas led the young rider classification from first to last stage and finished fifth overall (+3:36); Movistar won the team classification ahead of Jumbo–Visma. Rémi Cavagna took the combativity award.

Stage by stage

1 Tue 20 Oct · Irun → Arrate · Review Roglič wins on Arrate and takes the lead Primož Roglič won the opening stage from Irún to Arrate one second ahead of Richard Carapaz and Dan Martin. The defending champion took the red jersey with a five-second lead over Carapaz; Enric Mas led the young riders and Sepp Kuss the mountains. 2 Wed 21 Oct · Pamplona → Lekunberri · Review Soler wins in Lekunberri, Roglič stays in red Marc Soler (Movistar) won the stage from Pamplona to Lekunberri, 19 seconds ahead of Roglič and Dan Martin. Roglič stayed in the red jersey, now nine seconds ahead of Martin and eleven ahead of Carapaz, who took over the mountains lead. 3 Thu 22 Oct · Lodosa → Laguna Negra de Urbión (Vinuesa) · Review Dan Martin wins at Laguna Negra de Urbión Dan Martin (Israel Start-Up Nation) won the summit finish at Laguna Negra de Urbión, with Roglič and Carapaz following on the same time. Roglič stayed in front, five seconds ahead of Martin and 13 ahead of Carapaz. 4 Fri 23 Oct · Garray → Ejea de los Caballeros · Review Bennett wins the first sprint in Ejea de los Caballeros Sam Bennett (Deceuninck–Quick-Step) won the bunch sprint on the first flat stage in Ejea de los Caballeros ahead of Jasper Philipsen and Jakub Mareczko. The general classification stayed unchanged: Roglič led Dan Martin (+0:05) and Carapaz (+0:13). 5 Sat 24 Oct · Huesca → Sabiñánigo · Review Wellens wins in Sabiñánigo and takes the mountains lead Tim Wellens (Lotto–Soudal) won in Sabiñánigo ahead of Dan Martin (+0:04) and Thymen Arensman (+0:12) and took the mountains lead from Carapaz. Roglič stayed in the red jersey, ahead of Martin (+0:05) and Carapaz (+0:13). 6 Sun 25 Oct · Biescas → Aramón Formigal · Review Izagirre wins at Formigal, Carapaz takes red Ion Izagirre (Astana) won the mountain stage to Aramón Formigal ahead of Michael Woods and Rui Costa (both +0:25). Because of French COVID-19 restrictions, the originally planned 136.6 km stage to the Col du Tourmalet had been replaced by a 146.4 km stage to Formigal. Carapaz took the red jersey, 18 seconds ahead of Carthy and 20 ahead of Dan Martin; Roglič was 30 seconds back. 7 Tue 27 Oct · Vitoria-Gasteiz → Villanueva de Valdegovia · Review Woods wins in Villanueva de Valdegovía After the first rest day, Michael Woods (EF Pro Cycling) won the stage from Vitoria-Gasteiz to Villanueva de Valdegovía ahead of Omar Fraile and Alejandro Valverde (both +0:04). Carapaz kept the red jersey, and Guillaume Martin took the lead of the mountains classification. 8 Wed 28 Oct · Logroño → Alto de Moncalvillo · Review Roglič wins on Moncalvillo and closes in on Carapaz Roglič won the summit finish at the Alto de Moncalvillo ahead of Carapaz (+0:13) and Dan Martin (+0:19). Carapaz kept the red jersey, but Roglič cut his deficit from 30 to 13 seconds; Martin was now 28 seconds back. 9 Thu 29 Oct · Castrillo del Val → Aguilar de Campoo · Review Ackermann gets the win in Aguilar de Campoo after Bennett is relegated Pascal Ackermann (Bora–Hansgrohe) was declared the winner of the flat stage to Aguilar de Campoo after Sam Bennett was relegated for shoulder-barging Emīls Liepiņš. Gerben Thijssen and Max Kanter followed on the same time; Carapaz stayed in red, 13 seconds ahead of Roglič. 10 Fri 30 Oct · Castro Urdiales → Suances · Review Roglič wins in Suances and regains the red jersey Roglič won the flat stage from Castro Urdiales to Suances ahead of Felix Großschartner and Andrea Bagioli (both on the same time) and took the red jersey back from Carapaz. Carapaz is listed on the same time in the general classification, with Dan Martin 25 seconds back. 11 Sat 31 Oct · Villaviciosa → Alto de la Farrapona · Review Gaudu wins at La Farrapona ahead of Soler Frenchman David Gaudu (Groupama–FDJ) won the summit finish at the Alto de la Farrapona ahead of Marc Soler (+0:04) and Michael Storer (+0:52). In the general classification Roglič stayed ahead of Carapaz (listed on the same time) and Dan Martin (+0:25). 12 Sun 1 Nov · La Pola Llaviana → Alto de L'Angliru · Review Carthy wins on the Angliru, Carapaz back in red On the short mountain stage (109.4 km) to the Alto de L'Angliru, Hugh Carthy (EF Pro Cycling) won ahead of Vlasov, Mas and Carapaz (all +0:16); Roglič lost 26 seconds. Carapaz took the red jersey with a ten-second lead and Carthy moved up to third overall. 13 Tue 3 Nov · Muros → Mirador de Ézaro · Review Roglič wins the Ézaro time trial and leads again Roglič won the 33.7 km mountain time trial from Muros to the Mirador de Ézaro in 46:39, ahead of Will Barta (+0:01) and Nelson Oliveira (+0:10). Carthy was fourth (+0:25). Roglič took the red jersey with a 39-second lead over Carapaz and 47 over Carthy. 14 Wed 4 Nov · Lugo → Ourense · Review Wellens wins in Ourense for a second time Tim Wellens (Lotto–Soudal) took his second stage win of this Vuelta in Ourense, ahead of Michael Woods and Zdeněk Štybar (both on the same time). The general classification was unchanged: Roglič led by 39 seconds over Carapaz and 47 over Carthy. 15 Thu 5 Nov · Mos → Puebla de Sanabria · Review Philipsen wins the longest stage in Puebla de Sanabria Jasper Philipsen (UAE Team Emirates) won the sprint in Puebla de Sanabria after 230.8 km, the longest stage of this Vuelta, ahead of Pascal Ackermann and Jannik Steimle. The top of the general classification did not change: Roglič ahead of Carapaz (+0:39) and Carthy (+0:47). 16 Fri 6 Nov · Salamanca → Ciudad Rodrigo · Review Cort wins in Ciudad Rodrigo, Roglič second Magnus Cort (EF Pro Cycling) won in Ciudad Rodrigo ahead of Roglič and Dion Smith (all on the same time). Roglič extended his general classification lead to 45 seconds over Carapaz and 53 over Carthy. 17 Sat 7 Nov · Sequeros → Alto de la Covatilla · Review Gaudu wins at La Covatilla, Carapaz gains time David Gaudu won the final mountain stage at La Covatilla ahead of Gino Mäder (+0:28) and Ion Izagirre (+1:05). Carapaz cut his deficit to Roglič from 45 to 24 seconds; Roglič kept the red jersey and Carthy remained third (+0:47). 18 Sun 8 Nov · Hipódromo de la Zarzuela → Madrid · Review Ackermann wins in Madrid, Roglič seals the overall Pascal Ackermann (Bora–Hansgrohe) won the final sprint in Madrid ahead of Sam Bennett and Max Kanter for his second stage win. Primož Roglič finished the Vuelta as overall winner in 72:46:12, ahead of Richard Carapaz (+0:24) and Hugh Carthy (+1:15), and also won the points classification.
