Stories

The big stories of the Vuelta and the story of every stage – updated continuously.

Headlines

🏆Stage 21

Enric Mas wins his first Vuelta

Enric Mas (Movistar Team) won the Vuelta in 73:52:55, 2:15 ahead of Primož Roglič and 2:44 ahead of Felix Gall. He inherited the red jersey after Pogačar's exit on stage 8, won stage 9 on the Alto de Aitana and defended his lead all the way to Granada.

⚠️Stage 8

Pogačar's Vuelta ends in a crash on stage 8

Tadej Pogačar was the heavy favourite and won three of the first six stages (1, 4 and 6). On stage 8 he crashed about 30 km from the finish, could not continue because of his injuries and had to abandon. The red jersey passed to Enric Mas.

⚡2026

Brennan wins five stages, more than anyone else

Matthew Brennan (Visma–Lease a Bike) won stages 2, 5, 11, 16 and 17, more than any other rider in this Vuelta. His teammate Wout van Aert helped lead out several of those sprints. Brennan finished second in the points classification with 273 points.

⚡Stage 15

Van Aert takes the green jersey, two stages and the combativity award

Wout van Aert won the points classification with 326 points, ahead of his teammate Matthew Brennan (273). He won stage 13 in Loja and stage 15 in Córdoba from the front group, and also took the overall combativity award.

🌧️Stage 3

Hail and heat: stage 3 cancelled, stage 15 shortened

On stage 3 the race was neutralised and then cancelled about 15 km from the finish in Font-Romeu because of a severe hailstorm, with no winner. Stage 15 to Córdoba was shortened from 181.2 km to 110.2 km because of extreme heat.

🏆Stage 14

Roglič and Gall fight for second place

Primož Roglič started despite having been hit by a car in training three weeks earlier, and finished second (2:15). Felix Gall (Decathlon CMA CGM) moved ahead of him by four seconds on stage 14, before Roglič regained second place in the stage 18 time trial. Gall finished third (2:44), ahead of Richard Carapaz (6:54) and Sepp Kuss (8:45).

•Stage 1

Grand Départ in Monaco, finish in Granada

The 81st Vuelta started on 22 August with a 9 km time trial in Monaco and crossed France and Andorra before reaching Spain. After 21 stages and 3,310.6 km it finished in Granada on 13 September.

🔥2026

Breakaways decide five of the hardest stages

Andreas Leknessund (stage 7), Jakob Omrzel (12), Marco Brenner (14), Eddie Dunbar (19) and Mikel Landa (20) all won tough stages from the breakaway. Landa won the queen stage solo at the Collado del Alguacil.

•2026

Buitrago, Onley and Decathlon take the other classifications

Santiago Buitrago (Bahrain Victorious) won the mountains classification with 78 points, and Oscar Onley (Netcompany INEOS), sixth overall, took the young rider classification. Decathlon CMA CGM won the team classification, with Felix Gall finishing third overall.

Stage by stage

1 Sat 22 Aug · Monaco ↻ · Review Pogačar wins the Monaco time trial and the first red jersey Tadej Pogačar won the 9 km opening time trial through the streets of Monaco in 10:57 and took the first red jersey. Ethan Hayter was second at the same listed time, Joshua Tarling third at 0:04. Tarling became the first leader of the young rider classification and Callum Thornley of the mountains classification. 2 Sun 23 Aug · Monaco → Manosque · Review Brennan sprints to victory in Manosque, Pogačar stays in red Matthew Brennan won the sprint in Manosque in 4:47:47, after an attack by his Visma–Lease a Bike teammate Wout van Aert on the final climb. Pau Miquel was second and Pogačar third, keeping the red jersey. Brennan took the white jersey, and Van Aert moved up to second overall at 0:09 with Brennan third at 0:10. 3 Mon 24 Aug · Gruissan → Font Romeu · Review Hailstorm cancels stage 3 in the Pyrenees The stage from Gruissan to Font-Romeu was neutralised and then cancelled with about 15 km remaining because of a severe hailstorm. No stage winner was declared and the general classification stayed unchanged, with Pogačar in the red jersey. 4 Tue 25 Aug · Andorra la Vella ↻ · Review Pogačar wins solo in Andorra after a 50 km attack Tadej Pogačar attacked 50 km from the finish on the Col de Beixalís and won in Andorra la Vella in 2:40:25. Jarno Widar led the chasers home 2:39 behind, with Felix Gall and Oscar Onley in the same group. Primož Roglič moved up to second overall at 3:21 ahead of Enric Mas (3:32), and Onley took the white jersey. 5 Wed 26 Aug · Falset → Roquetes · Review Brennan wins a second sprint in Roquetes Matthew Brennan won the bunch sprint in Roquetes in 3:47:02 ahead of Jordi Meeus and Vincenzo Albanese, led out in the finale by Christophe Laporte and Wout van Aert. Pogačar kept the red jersey and the top of the general classification was unchanged (Roglič at 3:21). 6 Thu 27 Aug · Alcossebre → Castellón · Review Pogačar beats Mas in a two-man sprint after El Bartolo gravel Tadej Pogačar attacked on the gravel tracks of the Puerto El Bartolo, was caught at the summit by Enric Mas and nearly crashed on the descent. He then beat the Spaniard in the sprint in Castellón in 4:12:40, with the chasers 0:46 behind. Mas moved up to second overall at 3:34, ahead of Roglič (4:21). 7 Fri 28 Aug · Vall d'Alba → Aramón Valdelinares · Review Leknessund wins solo from the breakaway at Valdelinares Andreas Leknessund, in the breakaway from kilometre zero, won solo at the Aramón Valdelinares summit in 3:49:47, 0:34 ahead of Tobias Halland Johannessen. It was the first stage win of this Vuelta for a breakaway rider. Pogačar kept the red jersey and Mas stayed second overall at 3:50. 8 Sat 29 Aug · Puçol → Xeraco · Review Pogačar crashes out, Coquard wins, Mas takes the red jersey Tadej Pogačar crashed about 30 km from the finish, could not continue because of his injuries and left the race. Bryan Coquard won the bunch sprint in 3:47:06 ahead of Mads Pedersen and Jordi Meeus, taking his first Grand Tour stage win. Enric Mas inherited the red jersey, leading Roglič by 1:00 and Gall by 1:11. 9 Sun 30 Aug · Villajoyosa → Alto de Aitana · Review Mas wins on the Alto de Aitana and extends his lead Enric Mas won the mountain stage to the Alto de Aitana in 5:10:40, beating Oscar Onley, the only rider able to follow him in the final kilometre, in the sprint. Roglič was third at 0:12 and Gall fourth at 0:18. Mas now led Roglič by 1:18 and Gall by 1:39, and Santiago Buitrago took the polka-dot jersey. 10 Tue 1 Sept · Alcaraz → Elche de la Sierra · Review Tronchon surprises the sprinters in Elche de la Sierra Bastien Tronchon (Groupama–FDJ United) won a frantic finale in Elche de la Sierra in 4:07:41 ahead of Magnus Cort and Thibau Nys. Wout van Aert was fourth despite the heavy work of Visma–Lease a Bike. Mas kept the red jersey, with Roglič still 1:18 behind. 11 Wed 2 Sept · Cartagena → Lorca · Review Brennan wins his third stage in Lorca Matthew Brennan won the sprint in Lorca in 3:17:18 ahead of Bryan Coquard and Jordi Meeus, led out by Wout van Aert. It was his third stage win of this Vuelta. The top of the general classification was unchanged: Mas led Roglič (1:18) and Gall (1:39). 12 Thu 3 Sept · Vera → Calar Alto · Review Omrzel wins at Calar Alto, Mas gains time on his rivals Jakob Omrzel, the youngest rider in the race, won from the breakaway at the Calar Alto summit in 4:29:53, 1:56 ahead of Urko Berrade and 2:13 ahead of Santiago Buitrago. In the favourites' group Enric Mas attacked and took 0:27 on Roglič and Gall. Roglič was now 1:45 behind the red jersey and Gall 2:06. 13 Fri 4 Sept · Almuñécar → Loja · Review Van Aert wins from the breakaway in Loja Wout van Aert, part of a long breakaway, attacked 3 km from the finish and beat Valentin Paret-Peintre in the sprint in Loja in 4:34:00. Matthew Brennan was third at 0:24. It was Van Aert's first stage win of this Vuelta, and Mas kept the red jersey. 14 Sat 5 Sept · Jaén → Sierra de la Pandera · Review Brenner wins at the Pandera, Gall moves up to second overall Marco Brenner (Tudor) won from the breakaway at the summit of the Sierra de la Pandera in 4:15:09, 0:01 ahead of Santiago Buitrago and 0:11 ahead of Cristián Rodríguez. Enric Mas and Felix Gall were the first of the favourites. Gall moved up to second overall (2:06), four seconds ahead of Roglič (2:10). 15 Sun 6 Sept · Palma del Río → Córdoba · Review Van Aert wins the heat-shortened stage to Córdoba Because of extreme heat the stage was shortened from 181.2 km to 110.2 km. Wout van Aert got away about 30 km from the finish and won the sprint of a group of five in Córdoba in 2:35:00, ahead of Alessandro Romele and Thibau Nys. The general classification was unchanged, with Mas leading Gall (2:06). 16 Tue 8 Sept · Cortegana → Palos de la Frontera · Review Brennan takes his fourth stage in Palos de la Frontera Matthew Brennan won the sprint in Palos de la Frontera in 3:56:33 ahead of Bryan Coquard and Vito Braet. It was his fourth stage win of this Vuelta. Mas stayed in the red jersey, with Gall second at 2:06. 17 Wed 9 Sept · Dos Hermanas → Seville · Review Brennan sprints to a fifth win in Seville Matthew Brennan won the sprint in Seville in 4:07:15 ahead of Jordi Meeus and Magnus Cort. It was his fifth stage win, after stages 2, 5, 11 and 16, more than any other rider in this Vuelta. The general classification was unchanged, with Mas ahead of Gall and Roglič. 18 Thu 10 Sept · El Puerto de Santa María → Jerez de la Frontera · Review Küng wins the Jerez time trial, Mas limits his losses to Roglič Stefan Küng won the 32.5 km time trial from El Puerto de Santa María to Jerez de la Frontera in 35:22, 0:03 ahead of Callum Thornley and 0:09 ahead of João Almeida. Roglič was fourth (0:19). Mas conceded only about half a minute to Roglič, keeping a 1:37 lead, while Gall dropped to third overall at 3:01. 19 Fri 11 Sept · Vélez-Málaga → Peñas Blancas · Review Dunbar wins at Peñas Blancas, Mas stays in control Eddie Dunbar (Pinarello–Q36.5) won from the breakaway at Peñas Blancas in 5:00:54, passing Santiago Buitrago about 500 m from the line. Buitrago was second at 0:14 and Thomas Gloag third at 0:23. The general classification was unchanged: Mas led Roglič (1:37) and Gall (3:01). 20 Sat 12 Sept · La Calahorra → Collado del Alguacil · Review Landa wins the queen stage, Mas defends the red jersey Mikel Landa won solo at the Collado del Alguacil in 4:58:01 after dropping the other escapees, 0:47 ahead of Tobias Halland Johannessen. Felix Gall attacked on the final climb and took back time, but the podium stayed the same: Mas ahead of Roglič (2:15) and Gall (2:44). Sepp Kuss moved up to fifth overall. 21 Sun 13 Sept · Granada ↻ · Review Johannessen wins in Granada, Mas is crowned Vuelta winner Tobias Halland Johannessen won the short final stage in Granada in 2:54:08, beating Alessandro Romele in a two-man sprint. Times were frozen 10 km from the finish, so the general classification leaders did not contest the stage. Enric Mas won the Vuelta by 2:15 over Roglič and 2:44 over Gall.
