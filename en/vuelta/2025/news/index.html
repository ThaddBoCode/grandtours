Stories

The big stories of the Vuelta and the story of every stage – updated continuously.

Headlines

🏆2025

Vingegaard wins his first Vuelta by 1:16

Jonas Vingegaard won the Vuelta for the first time in his career, taking stages 2, 9 and 20 and finishing 1:16 ahead of João Almeida. He got sick during the second week and had to defend his red jersey before sealing the overall win on the Bola del Mundo, while Almeida could only take time through bonus seconds and the time trial. Tom Pidcock was third (+3:11), his first Grand Tour podium.

🔥2025

Protests alter or end several stages

Pro-Palestinian protests over the Gaza war and the participation of Israel–Premier Tech affected several stages: the team's ride in the team time trial (stage 5) was blocked, stage 11 was neutralised with no winner, the finish line of stage 16 was moved, the time trial (stage 18) was cut from 27.2 to 12.2 km and stage 21 was cancelled. From stage 14 onwards, Israel–Premier Tech riders wore jerseys without the team name.

🔥Stage 21

Madrid finale cancelled: no official podium ceremony

The final stage to Madrid, shortened by 8 km the day before, ended when demonstrators invaded the circuit, the riders were stopped and the organisers cancelled the stage at 18:30. There was no stage winner and no official podium ceremony; riders and teams later held a private ceremony in a car park. Jonas Vingegaard expressed his disappointment at not being able to celebrate his overall win with his team and the fans.

•2025

UAE win seven stages and the team classification

UAE Team Emirates XRG won seven stages: the team time trial (5), Jay Vine on stages 6 and 10, Juan Ayuso on 7 and 12, João Almeida on the Angliru (13) and Marc Soler at La Farrapona (14). They also took the team classification, 23:01 ahead of Visma–Lease a Bike, second place overall for Almeida and the mountains jersey for Vine.

🏆Stage 6

Træen wears the red jersey for four stages

Torstein Træen of Bahrain Victorious took the red jersey on stage 6 to Pal, finishing second about three and a half minutes ahead of the main GC group. He kept it through stage 9, where his lead over Jonas Vingegaard was cut to 37 seconds; on stage 10 he lost more than a minute and Vingegaard took over. Træen finished the Vuelta ninth overall.

⚡2025

Pedersen wins the green jersey while Philipsen takes three sprints

Mads Pedersen of Lidl–Trek led the points classification from stage 4 to the end and won it with 277 points, ahead of Jonas Vingegaard (197) and Jasper Philipsen (135). He collected many high placings in the sprints and at the intermediate sprints, and won stage 15 from a breakaway. Philipsen took the bunch sprints on stages 1, 8 and 19.

🔥Stage 6

Ayuso loses seven minutes, then wins two stages

Juan Ayuso, one of UAE's two leaders, lost seven minutes on the final climb to Pal on stage 6 and dropped out of general classification contention. A day later he won from the break at Cerler (stage 7), and on stage 12 he beat Javier Romo in a two-up sprint.

✨2025

Pidcock reaches his first Grand Tour podium in third

Tom Pidcock (Q36.5) finished third overall, 3:11 behind, for his first Grand Tour podium. He was consistent in the mountains, took second on stages 9 and 17, attacked twice on stage 11 and held off Jai Hindley, who finished fourth (+3:41), over the final two weeks.

•Stage 1

Grand Départ in Turin, stages in four countries

The Vuelta started in Turin, which became the second city after Utrecht to host the start of all three Grand Tours. The first three stages were in Italy, stage 4 finished in Voiron in France, the race reached Spain on stage 5 in Figueres, and stage 6 ended in Andorra.

Stage by stage

1 Sat 23 Aug · Turin → Novara · Review Philipsen wins the opening sprint in Novara A six-man break was caught before the finish in Novara, and the bunch sprint went to Jasper Philipsen ahead of Ethan Vernon and Orluis Aular. Philipsen took the first red jersey, with Vernon in the white jersey. 2 Sun 24 Aug · Alba → Limone Piemonte · Review Vingegaard edges Ciccone in a photo finish at Limone Piemonte A crash before the final climb involved several riders, including Jonas Vingegaard, who all finished the stage. The win was decided in a reduced bunch sprint, where Vingegaard beat Giulio Ciccone in a photo finish and took the red jersey from Jasper Philipsen. 3 Mon 25 Aug · San Maurizio Canavese → Ceres · Review Gaudu out-sprints Pedersen after diving into the final corner On the small uphill finish in Ceres, David Gaudu dive-bombed the final corner to take the lead from Mads Pedersen and win the sprint. Jonas Vingegaard took third and four bonus seconds, which kept him in the red jersey on the same time as Gaudu. 4 Tue 26 Aug · Susa → Voiron · Review Turner wins in Voiron, Gaudu takes the red jersey The race crossed from Italy into France, and despite three categorised climbs the stage was decided by a bunch sprint, which Ben Turner won narrowly from Jasper Philipsen for his first Grand Tour stage. David Gaudu took the red jersey on the lowest sum of stage placings after finishing level on time with Jonas Vingegaard, and Mads Pedersen moved into the lead of the points classification. 5 Wed 27 Aug · Figueres ↻ · Review UAE win the team time trial, Vingegaard back in red In the 24.1 km team time trial in Figueres, UAE Team Emirates XRG clocked 25:26, eight seconds ahead of Visma–Lease a Bike and nine ahead of Lidl–Trek. Jonas Vingegaard regained the red jersey as David Gaudu lost 16 seconds; protesters blocked the ride of Israel–Premier Tech, which was credited with 15 seconds. 6 Thu 28 Aug · Olot → Pal · Review Vine wins at Pal as Træen takes red and Ayuso loses seven minutes On the first proper mountain stage, finishing at Pal in Andorra, Jay Vine attacked from a ten-man break over the Alto de la Comella and held off Torstein Træen on the final climb. Træen finished about three and a half minutes ahead of the main GC group and took the red jersey, while Juan Ayuso lost seven minutes; Vine moved into the polka-dot jersey and Giulio Pellizzari into the white jersey. 7 Fri 29 Aug · Andorra la Vella → Cerler · Review Ayuso bounces back with a win at Cerler, UAE's third in a row A day after his collapse, Juan Ayuso won from the break by dropping his companions on the final climb to Cerler, UAE's third consecutive stage win. Despite an attack from João Almeida, the main GC contenders finished together, so Torstein Træen kept the red jersey. 8 Sat 30 Aug · Monzón → Zaragoza · Review Philipsen sprints to his second win in Zaragoza On the flat stage to Zaragoza, with no categorised climbs, Jasper Philipsen won the expected bunch sprint for his second stage of this Vuelta. Philipsen came around Elia Viviani, who was later relegated for deviating too much during the sprint. 9 Sun 31 Aug · Alfaro → Valdezcaray · Review Vingegaard soloes to Valdezcaray, Træen narrowly keeps red After Matteo Jorgenson set a furious pace, Jonas Vingegaard attacked on the final climb to the Valdezcaray ski resort; only Giulio Ciccone followed for a kilometre before Vingegaard soloed to the win, 24 seconds ahead of Tom Pidcock and João Almeida. Torstein Træen kept the red jersey, with his lead over Vingegaard cut to 37 seconds. 10 Tue 2 Sept · Arguedas → El Ferial Larra Belagua · Review Vine wins solo at Belagua as Vingegaard retakes red After a fight for the break that lasted more than 100 km, 28 riders got away, and Jay Vine proved strongest on the Puerto de Belagua to win his second stage solo, 35 seconds ahead of Pablo Castrillo. Torstein Træen lost more than a minute among the favourites, and Jonas Vingegaard regained the red jersey with 26 seconds on Træen and 38 on João Almeida. 11 Wed 3 Sept · Bilbao ↻ · Review No winner in Bilbao: protests neutralise the stage Because of pro-Palestinian protests at the finish, the race announced on the approach to the Alto de Pike that general classification times would be taken at the 3 km mark and that there would be no stage winner. Tom Pidcock attacked twice there and Jonas Vingegaard came back each time, and the pair crossed the 3 km mark ten seconds ahead of João Almeida and other chasers; Pidcock moved up to third overall. 12 Thu 4 Sept · Laredo → Los Corrales de Buelna · Review Ayuso beats Romo in a two-up sprint at Los Corrales de Buelna The stage was tailor-made for a breakaway, with a 53-man group contesting the win. On the final climb, the Collada de Brenes, Juan Ayuso and Javier Romo gapped the rest, and Ayuso outsprinted Romo for his second stage of the race. 13 Fri 5 Sept · Cabezón de la Sal → L'Angliru · Review Almeida wins on the Angliru, Vingegaard holds red UAE lifted the pace from the Alto del Cordal, and on the steepest part of the Angliru João Almeida accelerated; only Jonas Vingegaard could follow. In the sprint Almeida held off the Dane for UAE's sixth win of the race, cutting Vingegaard's lead in the general classification to 46 seconds. 14 Sat 6 Sept · Avilés → Alto de La Farrapona · Review Soler wins solo at La Farrapona; Vingegaard takes second Marc Soler attacked from a 24-man break ahead of the final climb and soloed to the win, UAE's seventh stage of the race. Among the favourites a headwind kept things quiet until the last kilometre, when Jai Hindley attacked; Jonas Vingegaard outsprinted João Almeida for second place and extended his lead to 48 seconds. 15 Sun 7 Sept · Vegadeo → Monforte de Lemos · Review Pedersen wins from the break in Monforte de Lemos A group of 47 riders went away after the first climb, and Jay Vine and Louis Vervaeke attacked from it before being caught by a seven-man chase group with 6.8 km to go. Mads Pedersen outsprinted Orluis Aular for his first win of the race, with the peloton crossing the line more than 13 minutes later. 16 Tue 9 Sept · Poio → Mos · Review Bernal wins at a shortened finish, Landa second A 17-man break survived to the finish, and Egan Bernal and Mikel Landa were left in front after Clément Braz Afonso punctured. Because of protests on the final climb, the organisers moved the finish line to the 8 km mark, and Bernal won the sprint ahead of Landa, his first Grand Tour stage since his career-threatening crash in 2022. Felix Gall lost 54 seconds, and Giulio Pellizzari moved above him in the general classification. 17 Wed 10 Sept · O Barco de Valdeorras → Ponferrada · Review Pellizzari wins his first Grand Tour stage at El Morredero After Visma–Lease a Bike set the pace on the final climb, Jai Hindley attacked, and João Almeida and Giulio Pellizzari were briefly distanced but came back. Near the top Pellizzari got a gap while the favourites looked at each other, and he held on for his first Grand Tour stage win, ahead of Tom Pidcock and Hindley; Jonas Vingegaard extended his lead over Almeida to 50 seconds. 18 Thu 11 Sept · Valladolid ↻ · Review Ganna wins the shortened time trial, Vingegaard leads by 40 seconds The day before, the organisers shortened the individual time trial in Valladolid from 27.2 to 12.2 km because of the threat of protests. Filippo Ganna won in exactly 13:00, with only Jay Vine coming close at one second. Jonas Vingegaard lost ten seconds to João Almeida, who was third on the stage, and led the general classification by 40 seconds. 19 Fri 12 Sept · Rueda → Guijuelo · Review Philipsen takes a third stage win in Guijuelo On the transition stage from Rueda to Guijuelo, Jasper Philipsen won the bunch sprint comfortably ahead of Mads Pedersen and Orluis Aular, his third stage of the race after stages 1 and 8. Earlier, Visma–Lease a Bike had delivered Jonas Vingegaard to the front at the intermediate sprint, where he took four bonus seconds and stretched his lead to 44 seconds. 20 Sat 13 Sept · Robledo de Chavela → Bola del Mundo · Review Vingegaard wins on the Bola del Mundo and seals the red jersey UAE set a hard pace all day to try to crack Jonas Vingegaard, but on the Bola del Mundo he dropped the rest before the final kilometre to win his third stage, eleven seconds ahead of Sepp Kuss. His lead over João Almeida grew to 1:16; Tom Pidcock lost only nine seconds to Jai Hindley to secure third overall, and Matthew Riccitello took the white jersey from Giulio Pellizzari, who lost more than two minutes. 21 Sun 14 Sept · Valdeolmos-Alalpardo → Madrid · Review Madrid finale cancelled: no stage winner, no podium ceremony On the way to Madrid, where protests against the Gaza war and the participation of Israel–Premier Tech had been called, demonstrators invaded the road at several points of the final circuit at around 18:00. The riders were ordered to stop and the organisers cancelled the stage at 18:30, with no stage winner and no official podium ceremony. Jonas Vingegaard was confirmed as overall winner, with the standings after stage 20 standing as final.
