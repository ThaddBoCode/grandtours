Stories

The big stories of the Vuelta and the story of every stage – updated continuously.

Headlines

🏆2022

Evenepoel wins the Vuelta, his first Grand Tour

Remco Evenepoel took the red jersey on stage 6 and kept it all the way to Madrid. He won in 80:26:59, 2:02 ahead of Enric Mas and 4:57 ahead of Juan Ayuso, for his first Grand Tour victory, and also won the young rider classification. Spain's Mas and Ayuso joined him on the podium.

🏆Stage 6

Pico Jano: Evenepoel takes the red jersey

In the rain at Pico Jano, Jay Vine caught the breakaway rider Mark Padun and won the stage. Remco Evenepoel dropped everyone except Enric Mas about 8 km from the summit and finished second, 15 seconds behind Vine; Primož Roglič lost 1:28 to him once bonus seconds are counted. Evenepoel became the sixth leader in six days, 21 seconds ahead of Rudy Molard.

⚠️Stage 16

Roglič crashes out of second place

In the finale of stage 16, Primož Roglič, the three-time defending champion, collided with Fred Wright in a five-man sprint and crashed heavily, crossing the line with cuts on his right side. He was second overall, 1:26 behind Remco Evenepoel, but did not start stage 17. Evenepoel then led Enric Mas by 2:01.

⚡2022

Pedersen: three stage wins and the green jersey

Mads Pedersen won stages 13, 16 and 19 and took the green jersey from Sam Bennett on stage 8, keeping it to Madrid. He finished the points classification with 409 points, ahead of Fred Wright (186) and Enric Mas (138). Bennett had won stages 2 and 3 in the Netherlands and left with COVID-19 before stage 10.

•2022

Carapaz: three stage wins and the polka-dot jersey

Richard Carapaz won stages 12, 14 and 20, each time from a breakaway, and took the polka-dot jersey from Jay Vine on stage 18 after Vine abandoned following a crash. He finished the mountains classification with 73 points, ahead of Robert Stannard (36), and placed 14th overall.

🔥Stage 11

COVID-19 thins the peloton: Yates and Sivakov out

The start list marks 25 of the 49 non-finishers as COVID-19 withdrawals. Before stage 11, Simon Yates and Pavel Sivakov, both in the top ten, left the race together with three Equipo Kern Pharma riders. Juan Ayuso tested positive before stage 13 but stayed in the race after a PCR test showed a low risk of infectivity.

•Stage 1

The Vuelta starts in Utrecht with a team time trial

The 77th Vuelta opened with a team time trial in Utrecht, won by Jumbo–Visma in 24:40, 13 seconds ahead of INEOS Grenadiers; Robert Gesink wore the first red jersey. It was the first team time trial in a WorldTour race since the opening stage of the 2019 Vuelta. After three stages in the Netherlands the race moved on to Spain, and the red jersey had six different leaders in the first six days.

✨Stage 6

Jay Vine wins the first two summit finishes

Jay Vine won the first two summit finishes of the race: Pico Jano on stage 6, his first professional win, and Colláu Fancuaya on stage 8. He led the mountains classification from stage 8 until a crash on stage 18 forced him to abandon, after which Richard Carapaz took the polka-dot jersey.

⚠️Stage 11

World champion Alaphilippe abandons with a dislocated shoulder

Julian Alaphilippe slid down in the peloton about 64 km from the finish of stage 11 and could not continue; it was later revealed that he had a dislocated shoulder. The stage ended in a bunch sprint won by Kaden Groves.

Stage by stage

1 Fri 19 Aug · Utrecht ↻ · Review Jumbo–Visma win the Utrecht team time trial Jumbo–Visma won the opening team time trial in Utrecht in 24:40, 13 seconds ahead of INEOS Grenadiers and 14 ahead of Quick-Step Alpha Vinyl. Robert Gesink crossed the line first and took the first red jersey, with Primož Roglič second. 2 Sat 20 Aug · 's-Hertogenbosch → Utrecht · Review Bennett wins the sprint in Utrecht, Teunissen in red A five-man break was caught 58.4 km from the finish, and Sam Bennett won the bunch sprint ahead of Mads Pedersen and Tim Merlier, taking the lead in the points classification. Mike Teunissen finished fourth and took the red jersey from his Jumbo–Visma teammate Robert Gesink. 3 Sun 21 Aug · Breda ↻ · Review Bennett doubles up in Breda, Affini takes the lead Sam Bennett won his second stage in a row in the sprint in Breda, again ahead of Mads Pedersen, with Daniel McLay third. Edoardo Affini took the red jersey before the first rest day, and Michael Woods left the race after a crash about 139 km from the finish. 4 Tue 23 Aug · Vitoria-Gasteiz → Laguardia · Review Roglič wins in Laguardia and takes the red jersey After the first rest day and the move to Spain, Primož Roglič started his sprint 200 m from the line in Laguardia and beat Mads Pedersen, with Enric Mas third. Only 12 riders finished in the front group, seven seconds clear of the second group, and Roglič took over the red jersey. 5 Wed 24 Aug · Irun → Bilbao · Review Soler wins solo in Bilbao, Molard takes red Marc Soler bridged to a 17-man break, went clear on the second ascent of the Alto del Vivero and held off the chasers, who came within six seconds, to win in Bilbao. Daryl Impey and Fred Wright followed at four seconds; with the peloton more than five minutes down, fourth-placed Rudy Molard took the red jersey, two seconds ahead of Wright. 6 Thu 25 Aug · Bilbao → Pico Jano · Review Vine wins at Pico Jano, Evenepoel takes the lead On the first summit finish of the Vuelta, Jay Vine caught the breakaway rider Mark Padun in the rain and took his first professional win. Remco Evenepoel finished second, 15 seconds back and one second ahead of Enric Mas, and took the red jersey, 21 seconds ahead of Rudy Molard. Primož Roglič lost 1:28 to Evenepoel. 7 Fri 26 Aug · Camargo → Cistierna · Review Herrada wins from the break in Cistierna Six riders went clear early and held off the peloton, with Fred Wright starting the sprint 300 m out. Jesús Herrada won his second Vuelta stage ahead of Samuele Battistella and Wright. Sam Bennett led the peloton home 29 seconds later, and Remco Evenepoel stayed in red as the top ten did not change. 8 Sat 27 Aug · La Pola Llaviana → Colláu Fancuaya · Review Vine wins again at Colláu Fancuaya, Pedersen takes green Jay Vine won his second stage from the break after dropping his companions 6.2 km out; Marc Soler was second at 43 seconds, level with Rein Taaramäe, and Thibaut Pinot fourth at 47 seconds. Vine took the polka-dot jersey, and Mads Pedersen the green jersey at the intermediate sprint. Evenepoel, Mas and Roglič finished 1:20 behind Vine, and the overall order stayed the same. 9 Sun 28 Aug · Villaviciosa → Les Praeres · Review Meintjes wins at Les Praeres, Evenepoel extends his lead Louis Meintjes won from the break for his first World Tour victory, 1:01 ahead of Samuele Battistella, with Edoardo Zambanini third. Remco Evenepoel dropped his rivals on the final climb, which averages 12.9 percent, finished 1:34 behind Meintjes and 34 seconds ahead of Juan Ayuso, and stretched his lead over Enric Mas to 1:12. 10 Tue 30 Aug · Elche → Alicante · Review Evenepoel wins the time trial in Alicante In the only individual time trial, from Elche to Alicante, Remco Evenepoel set the fastest time in 33:18, 48 seconds ahead of Primož Roglič, with Rémi Cavagna third at 1:00. It was Evenepoel's first Grand Tour stage win. His lead grew to 2:41 over Roglič, who moved up to second, and 3:03 over Enric Mas; Floris De Tier did not start because of saddle sores, and seven other riders stayed out, most of them with COVID-19. 11 Wed 31 Aug · ElPozo Alimentación → Cabo de Gata · Review Groves sprints to victory as Yates, Sivakov and Alaphilippe leave Kaden Groves timed his sprint perfectly to win his first Grand Tour stage, ahead of Danny van Poppel and Tim Merlier. Before the start, Simon Yates and Pavel Sivakov, both in the top ten, had withdrawn with COVID-19 along with three Equipo Kern Pharma riders, and Julian Alaphilippe abandoned after a crash with a dislocated shoulder. Ben O'Connor and Thymen Arensman entered the top ten. 12 Thu 1 Sept · Salobreña → Peñas Blancas · Review Carapaz wins from the break at Peñas Blancas Richard Carapaz attacked just under 2 km from the finish out of a 32-man break and took his first Vuelta stage win, 9 seconds ahead of Wilco Kelderman, with Marc Soler third at 24 seconds. Remco Evenepoel crashed with teammate Ilan Van Wilder without injury, and the favourites finished about seven and a half minutes down. Kelderman and Jan Polanc moved into the top ten. 13 Fri 2 Sept · Ronda → Montilla · Review Pedersen wins in Montilla Mads Pedersen passed Pascal Ackermann, who had launched an early sprint on the uphill drag to Montilla, and took his first stage win of this Vuelta, ahead of Bryan Coquard and Ackermann. Juan Ayuso had tested positive for COVID-19 before the stage but stayed in the race after a PCR test showed a low risk of infectivity. Remco Evenepoel kept the red jersey. 14 Sat 3 Sept · Montoro → Sierra de La Pandera · Review Carapaz wins again at La Pandera, Roglič closes in Richard Carapaz dropped his break companions 3.3 km from the finish and won solo, 8 seconds ahead of Miguel Ángel López and Primož Roglič. Roglič attacked from the red jersey group and gained 52 seconds on a struggling Remco Evenepoel, cutting his deficit to 1:49; Enric Mas was third overall, 2:43 down. 15 Sun 4 Sept · Martos → Sierra Nevada · Review Arensman wins at Sierra Nevada, Evenepoel holds on Thymen Arensman attacked from the break 8.7 km from the finish, dropped Marc Soler and took his first Grand Tour stage win. Enric Mas was second at 1:23 and Miguel Ángel López third at 1:25. Remco Evenepoel kept the red jersey, 1:34 ahead of Primož Roglič and 2:01 ahead of Mas. 16 Tue 6 Sept · Sanlúcar de Barrameda → Tomares · Review Pedersen wins in Tomares as Roglič crashes In a five-man sprint, Mads Pedersen won ahead of Pascal Ackermann and Danny van Poppel, but Primož Roglič collided with Fred Wright and crashed heavily, finishing with cuts on his right side. The peloton came in eight seconds later, with Remco Evenepoel credited with its time after a mechanical problem inside the last 3 km. Roglič was given the lead group's time and stayed second overall, 1:26 behind. 17 Wed 7 Sept · Aracena → Monasterio de Tentudía · Review Urán wins at Tentudía as Roglič abandons Rigoberto Urán won from a 13-man break after Lawson Craddock was caught shortly before the final kilometre, holding off Quentin Pacher, with Jesús Herrada third. The win completed his set of stage wins at all three Grand Tours. Primož Roglič did not start after his crash, leaving Remco Evenepoel 2:01 ahead of Enric Mas, with Juan Ayuso moving onto the podium. 18 Thu 8 Sept · Trujillo → Alto del Piornal · Review Evenepoel wins at Alto del Piornal as Vine abandons Remco Evenepoel won his second stage: he and Enric Mas caught Robert Gesink, the last survivor of a 41-man break, in the final hundred metres before Evenepoel sprinted clear; Mas and Gesink were both 2 seconds behind. Jay Vine crashed early and abandoned, so the polka-dot jersey passed to Richard Carapaz. Evenepoel extended his lead over Mas to 2:07, and Miguel Ángel López moved up to fourth overall. 19 Fri 9 Sept · Talavera de la Reina ↻ · Review Pedersen takes a third stage win in Talavera A three-man break was caught 50 km from the finish and the bunch stayed calm until the sprint, which Mads Pedersen won for his third stage victory, ahead of Fred Wright and Gianni Vermeersch. The general classification was unchanged, with Remco Evenepoel leading Enric Mas by 2:07. 20 Sat 10 Sept · Moralzarzal → Puerto de Navacerrada · Review Carapaz wins a third time, Evenepoel keeps Mas at bay Richard Carapaz escaped on the final climb to the Puerto de Cotos and won solo for the third time, 8 seconds ahead of Thymen Arensman, with Juan Ayuso and Jai Hindley 13 seconds back. The top three overall arrived together, with Remco Evenepoel leading Enric Mas by 2:05. Carapaz had already secured the polka-dot jersey over the Puerto de Morcuera. 21 Sun 11 Sept · Las Rozas → Madrid · Review Molano wins in Madrid, Evenepoel wins the Vuelta Juan Sebastián Molano won the bunch sprint in Madrid ahead of green jersey Mads Pedersen and Pascal Ackermann. Remco Evenepoel completed the Vuelta in 80:26:59, 2:02 ahead of Enric Mas and 4:57 ahead of Juan Ayuso; Pedersen won the points classification, Richard Carapaz the mountains and UAE Team Emirates the team classification.
