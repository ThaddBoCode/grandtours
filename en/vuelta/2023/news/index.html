Stories

The big stories of the Vuelta and the story of every stage – updated continuously.

Headlines

🏆2023

Sepp Kuss wins the Vuelta – Jumbo-Visma take the whole podium

Sepp Kuss won the 2023 Vuelta in 76:48:21, 17 seconds ahead of his team-mate Jonas Vingegaard, with Primož Roglič third at 1:08. Kuss took the red jersey on stage 8 and kept it all the way to Madrid. Jumbo-Visma also won the team classification.

🏆Stage 17

Angliru: Roglič wins, Kuss saves the red jersey by 8 seconds

On the Alto de L’Angliru, the red-jersey group caught Remco Evenepoel 5.5 km from the finish and was reduced to the three Jumbo-Visma leaders. About 1.5 km out Roglič and Vingegaard dropped Kuss and Roglič won their duel, with Kuss third at 19 seconds. His lead over Vingegaard shrank to 8 seconds, with Roglič at 1:08.

🔥Stage 13

Tourmalet: Jumbo-Visma 1-2-3 as Evenepoel cracks

On stage 13 to the Col du Tourmalet, Jumbo-Visma filled the first three places: Vingegaard won ahead of Kuss (+0:30) and Roglič (+0:33). Kuss stayed in red, with Roglič and Vingegaard moving up to second and third overall. Defending champion Remco Evenepoel cracked, ending his overall hopes.

•Stage 18

Evenepoel hits back: three wins, polka-dot jersey, combativity prize

Remco Evenepoel won stages 3, 14 and 18 and briefly wore the red jersey after his win in Arinsal. After cracking on the Tourmalet he went on the attack in breakaways, winning the mountains classification (135 points) and the combativity award. He finished 12th overall, 16:44 behind Kuss.

⚡Stage 21

Groves takes green with stage wins 4, 5 and 21

Kaden Groves won the sprints in Tarragona and Burriana and finished the Vuelta with the win in Madrid. He wore the green jersey from stage 4 to the end and won the points classification with 315 points, 79 ahead of Remco Evenepoel. The other sprint wins went to Geoffrey Soupe (stage 7), Juan Sebastián Molano (12) and Alberto Dainese (19).

🌧️Stage 1

An opening in rain and darkness: criticism of the team time trial

The 14.8 km team time trial in Barcelona ended after the 20:33 sunset, in rain and poor visibility, with the later starters facing worse conditions than the early ones. Remco Evenepoel called the conditions ridiculous, while race director Javier Guillén said a storm of that magnitude had not been forecast. Team dsm–firmenich won in 17:30; Laurens De Plus crashed and left the race.

🏆Stage 8

Kuss: a solo win from the break, then the red jersey

On stage 6 to Javalambre, Sepp Kuss won solo from a break of around 40 riders, 26 seconds ahead of Lenny Martinez, who took the red jersey with Kuss just 8 seconds behind. Two stages later, after Roglič’s win at the Xorret de Catí, the red jersey passed to Kuss, who kept it to Madrid.

🌧️Stage 2

Rain and mud: GC times taken early on stages 2 and 9

Because of bad weather, the GC time on stage 2 was taken 9 km from the finish, with the Montjuïc climb neutralised for the classification. This gave the red jersey to Andrea Piccolo, who had been in the day’s break, while Andreas Kron won the stage. On stage 9 the time was taken 2.05 km from the finish.

•Stage 4

Sepúlveda takes the polka-dot jersey from the break

Eduardo Sepúlveda (Lotto Dstny) was in the break on stage 4 and took the lead of the mountains classification. He kept the polka-dot jersey through stage 10, while Kaden Groves won the sprint in Tarragona.

Stage by stage

1 Sat 26 Aug · Barcelona ↻ · Review dsm–firmenich win the opening team time trial in the dark The Vuelta opened with a 14.8 km team time trial in Barcelona, run in rain and fading light. Team dsm–firmenich set the best time of 17:30 with Movistar on the same time, and Lorenzo Milesi pulled on the first red jersey. Laurens De Plus crashed and left the race, and a puncture for Jonas Vingegaard cost Jumbo-Visma time. 2 Sun 27 Aug · Mataró → Barcelona · Review Kron wins on Montjuïc, Piccolo takes the red jersey In the rain, the GC time was taken 9 km from the finish, which gave the red jersey to Andrea Piccolo of the day’s break; after that the favourites eased off. Andreas Kron attacked on Montjuïc and won solo ahead of Kaden Groves and Andrea Vendrame. Oscar Onley left the race after a crash. 3 Mon 28 Aug · Súria → Arinsal · Review Evenepoel wins in Arinsal and takes the red jersey Eleven riders formed the day’s break, but Lennard Kämna was caught shortly after going solo on the final climb to Arinsal. From a group of favourites Remco Evenepoel attacked 250 m from the line and won one second ahead of Jonas Vingegaard and Juan Ayuso. Evenepoel took the red jersey, 5 seconds ahead of Enric Mas, and also led the young rider classification. 4 Tue 29 Aug · Andorra la Vella → Tarragona · Review Groves wins the sprint in Tarragona Eduardo Sepúlveda, Ander Okamika and David González formed the break and were caught about 20 km from the finish. After a crash 4 km out, Kaden Groves won the sprint ahead of Juan Sebastián Molano and Edward Theuns. Groves took the green jersey and Sepúlveda the polka-dot jersey, while Remco Evenepoel stayed in red. 5 Wed 30 Aug · Morella → Burriana · Review Groves wins again, Evenepoel grabs a time bonus Eric Fagúndez led alone for more than 110 km before being caught on the Collado de la Ibola by Eduardo Sepúlveda. Remco Evenepoel won the intermediate sprint for a six-second time bonus. In Burriana Kaden Groves took the sprint ahead of Filippo Ganna and Dries Van Gestel, his second stage win in a row. 6 Thu 31 Aug · La Vall d'Uixó → Observatorio Astrofísico de Javalambre · Review Kuss wins solo at Javalambre, Lenny Martinez in red A break of around 40 riders built a lead of more than six minutes early on. On the final climb to the Observatorio Astrofísico de Javalambre, Sepp Kuss went clear 2 km from the line and won 26 seconds ahead of Lenny Martinez and 31 seconds ahead of Romain Bardet. Martinez took the red jersey with Kuss second overall at 8 seconds, while Remco Evenepoel lost around 30 seconds to Roglič and Vingegaard. 7 Fri 1 Sept · Utiel → Oliva · Review Soupe sprints to victory in Oliva ahead of Aular José Herrada and Ander Okamika attacked from kilometre 0 and were caught after about 160 km. Two crashes 10 and 5 km from the finish did not change the overall standings. Geoffrey Soupe won the sprint in Oliva ahead of Orluis Aular and Edward Theuns, and Lenny Martinez stayed in red. 8 Sat 2 Sept · Dénia → Xorret de Catí · Review Roglič wins at Xorret de Catí, Kuss takes the red jersey Four escapees including Damiano Caruso and Andreas Kron had only about 30 seconds left on the final climb and were caught. Sepp Kuss attacked 1.8 km from the summit and Remco Evenepoel led the favourites back up to him, but Primož Roglič won the sprint ahead of Evenepoel and Juan Ayuso. Lenny Martinez lost contact and Kuss took the red jersey with a 43-second lead over Marc Soler. 9 Sun 3 Sept · Cartagena → Collado de la Cruz de Caravaca · Review Kämna wins from the break, GC times taken 2.05 km out Eight escapees still had around five minutes on the final climb in rain and wind. Because of mud on the road the GC time was taken 2.05 km from the finish; Lennard Kämna shook off his companions and won ahead of Matteo Sobrero and Chris Hamilton. The favourites finished together and Kuss stayed in red, 43 seconds ahead of Marc Soler. 10 Tue 5 Sept · Valladolid ↻ · Review Ganna wins the Valladolid time trial, Evenepoel second Filippo Ganna won the 25.8 km time trial in Valladolid in 27:39, 16 seconds ahead of Remco Evenepoel and 36 seconds ahead of Primož Roglič. Evenepoel took the white jersey back and moved up to third overall, 1:09 behind Kuss. Sepp Kuss stayed in red, with Marc Soler 26 seconds behind. 11 Wed 6 Sept · Lerma → La Laguna Negra · Review Herrada wins from a 26-rider breakaway A 26-rider break stayed away to the finish at La Laguna Negra, with the favourites finishing well behind. Jonathan Caicedo attacked 1.5 km out but was passed 250 m from the line by Jesús Herrada, who won ahead of Romain Grégoire and Andreas Kron. Herrada took the polka-dot jersey, while the top of the overall standings did not change. 12 Thu 7 Sept · Ólvega → Zaragoza · Review Molano wins the sprint in Zaragoza ahead of Groves Abel Balderstone and Jetse Bol made up the break and were caught 44 km from the finish, after which Bol went clear again. In the sprint in Zaragoza Juan Sebastián Molano, led out by Rui Oliveira, won ahead of green jersey Kaden Groves and Boy van Poppel. The overall standings did not change, with Kuss in red ahead of Marc Soler. 13 Fri 8 Sept · Formigal → Col du Tourmalet · Review Vingegaard wins on the Tourmalet as Evenepoel loses over 27 minutes No break formed on the stage, and Michael Storer attacked on the Aubisque. On the Tourmalet Jonas Vingegaard went 8 km from the finish, and Jumbo-Visma filled the top three: Vingegaard, Sepp Kuss (+0:30) and Primož Roglič (+0:33). Kuss stayed in red with Roglič (+1:37) and Vingegaard (+1:44) now second and third; Vingegaard took the polka-dot jersey and Juan Ayuso the white jersey. 14 Sat 9 Sept · Sauveterre-de-Béarn → Larra-Belagua · Review Evenepoel wins solo at Larra-Belagua, Bardet second Remco Evenepoel, who had cracked the day before, rode in a break of around 20 with Romain Bardet and dropped him 4 km from the finish. He won 1:12 ahead of Bardet, with the red-jersey group arriving far behind. Evenepoel took the polka-dot jersey and the day’s combativity prize. 15 Sun 10 Sept · Pamplona → Lekunberri · Review Rui Costa passes Kämna on the line in Lekunberri Ten riders, including Remco Evenepoel, formed the break. Rui Costa and Santiago Buitrago attacked 14 km from the finish and Lennard Kämna rejoined them despite crashing on the descent. In Lekunberri Costa edged Kämna and Buitrago on the line, with the red-jersey group arriving 2:52 behind; Kuss stayed leader. 16 Tue 12 Sept · Liencres Playa → Bejes · Review Vingegaard attacks to win at Bejes, Kuss leads by only 29 seconds After a six-rider break was caught 10 km out, Jonas Vingegaard attacked 4 km from the summit finish at Bejes. He won 43 seconds ahead of Finn Fisher-Black and 49 seconds ahead of Wout Poels. Kuss stayed in red, but his lead over Vingegaard shrank to 29 seconds, with Roglič at 1:33. 17 Wed 13 Sept · Ribadesella → Alto de L'Angliru · Review Angliru: Roglič beats Vingegaard, Kuss keeps red by 8 seconds Remco Evenepoel rode in front for a long time but was caught 5.5 km from the finish by the red-jersey group. On the Angliru only the three Jumbo-Visma riders were left, and Roglič and Vingegaard dropped Kuss 1.5 km out, with Roglič winning on the same time as Vingegaard. Kuss was third at 19 seconds, kept the red jersey, and led Vingegaard by 8 seconds and Roglič by 1:08. 18 Thu 14 Sept · Pola de Allande → La Cruz de Linares · Review Evenepoel wins solo and secures the polka-dot jersey Remco Evenepoel was part of a 14-rider break that built a lead of up to ten minutes, and went clear of his last companions 29 km from the finish. He won solo by 4:44 over Damiano Caruso, his third stage win, and secured the mountains classification. The favourites came in more than nine minutes later; Vingegaard conceded a few seconds and Kuss led by 17 seconds. 19 Fri 15 Sept · La Bañeza → Íscar · Review Dainese wins the sprint in Íscar ahead of Ganna Four escapees were never more than three minutes clear and were caught 20 km from the finish, after which an attack by Samuele Battistella was also reeled in. A crash at the flamme rouge brought down green jersey Kaden Groves. Alberto Dainese won the sprint ahead of fellow Italian Filippo Ganna, and the overall standings did not change. 20 Sat 16 Sept · Manzanares el Real → Guadarrama · Review Poels wins in Guadarrama, narrowly ahead of Evenepoel A 31-rider break formed after half an hour and built a lead of up to nine minutes. On the last climb Wout Poels and Lennert Van Eetvelt attacked, and Marc Soler, Pelayo Sánchez and Remco Evenepoel joined them. Poels started the sprint early and held off Evenepoel on the line, while Kuss and his team-mates finished with the favourites and kept the red jersey with a 17-second lead. 21 Sun 17 Sept · Hipódromo de la Zarzuela → Madrid · Review Groves wins in Madrid as Kuss is crowned Vuelta winner On the short final stage to Madrid a group of six including Remco Evenepoel, Kaden Groves and Filippo Ganna went clear. Evenepoel accelerated 500 m out, but Groves caught him and won ahead of Ganna for his third stage win. Sepp Kuss won the overall classification by 17 seconds over Jonas Vingegaard and 1:08 over Primož Roglič, and Groves took the green jersey.
