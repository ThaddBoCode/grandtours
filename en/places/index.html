Climb · Lombardia / Südtirol

Passo dello Stelvio

2,758 m 21.5 km · 7.1 % 13 Visits First time 1953

The Stelvio Pass (2,758 m) is the highest point the Giro has ever reached and has been the Cima Coppi every time it was crossed since 1965. It was first included in 1953, when Fausto Coppi dropped Hugo Koblet here on his way to a fifth Giro win.

 Climb · Lombardia

Passo di Gavia

2,621 m 17.3 km · 0 % First time 1960
Giro 2027 · Stage 16

The Gavia (2,621 m) links Bormio with Ponte di Legno and has been the Giro's Cima Coppi several times since its debut in 1960. The 1988 stage in a snowstorm, after which Andrew Hampsten took the pink jersey, is legendary.

 Climb · Veneto

Tre Cime di Lavaredo

2,333 m 7 km · 8.2 % 8 Visits First time 1967
Giro 2023 · Stage 19

The Tre Cime di Lavaredo finish ends at the Rifugio Auronzo (2,333 m) in the Sexten Dolomites; the last four kilometres average over 12 % with ramps of 19 %. The Giro has come here eight times since 1967, most recently in 2023 when Santiago Buitrago won.

 Climb · Madrid / Castilla y León

Bola del Mundo

2,257 m 22 km · 6.1 % 9 Visits First time 1982
Vuelta 2025 · Stage 20Vuelta 2022 · Stage 20

The Bola del Mundo (2,257 m) in the Sierra de Guadarrama carries TV and radio antennas and hosted the finish of the penultimate Vuelta stage in 2010, 2012 and 2025; the climb is about 22 km at 6.1% with ramps up to 20%. Before that the race finished several times at the nearby Puerto de Navacerrada (1,858 m).

 Climb · Trentino / Veneto

Passo Pordoi

2,239 m 12 km · 6 % 42 Visits First time 1940

The Pordoi (2,239 m) between Arabba and Canazei is the most-climbed ascent in Giro history and has been the Cima Coppi more often than any other pass since 1965. A monument to Fausto Coppi stands on the pass, and stages finished here four times (1990, 1991, 1996, 2001).

 Climb · Veneto

Passo Giau

2,236 m 10.1 km · 9.1 % First time 1973

The Passo Giau (2,236 m) between Selva di Cadore and Cortina d'Ampezzo is, at 10.1 km and 9.1 % from Colle Santa Lucia, one of the hardest Dolomite passes. The Giro first climbed it in 1973, and it was the Cima Coppi in 1973, 2011, 2021 and 2026.

 Climb · Hautes-Pyrénées

Col du Portet

2,215 m 17.8 km · 7.9 % 2 Visits First time 2018
Tour 2027 · Stage 18Tour 2021 · Stage 17

The Col de Portet above Saint-Lary-Soulan is, at about 2,215 m, one of the highest paved passes in the French Pyrenees. The Tour first used it as a summit finish in 2018.

 Climb · Piemonte

Colle delle Finestre

2,178 m 18.6 km · 9.1 % First time 2005

The Colle delle Finestre (2,178 m) links the Susa valley with the Val Chisone; the north side from Susa is 18.6 km at 9.1 %, its last eight or so kilometres above the Colletto di Meana on gravel. The Giro has climbed it five times since 2005, and in 2018 Chris Froome launched his race-winning solo attack here.

 Climb · Abruzzo

Blockhaus

2,143 m 0 km · 0 % 8 Visits First time 1967
Giro 2026 · Stage 7Giro 2022 · Stage 9

The Blockhaus (2,143 m) is a peak of the Maiella massif in Abruzzo, named after a wooden fort built around 1863 to fight brigands. Since Eddy Merckx won the first stage here in 1967, its long climb has been one of the Giro's classic summit finishes.

 Finish · Abruzzo

Campo Imperatore (Gran Sasso)

2,128 m 6 Visits First time 1971
Giro 2023 · Stage 7

Campo Imperatore is a high plateau in the Gran Sasso massif; the road from Assergi or Fonte Cerreto climbs 27–31 km to about 2,128 m near the hotel where Mussolini was held in 1943. The Giro has finished here six times, first in 1971 and most recently in 2023.

 Climb · Hautes-Pyrénées

Col du Tourmalet

2,115 m 18.3 km · 7.7 % 88 Visits First time 1910
Tour 2027 · Stage 18Vuelta 2023 · Stage 13

The Tourmalet (2,115 m) is the most frequently crossed pass in the Tour de France, first climbed in 1910 with Octave Lapize leading. The Souvenir Jacques Goddet is awarded at its summit.

 Climb · Andalucía

Sierra Nevada (Pradollano)

2,100 m 14 Visits First time 1979
Vuelta 2022 · Stage 15

Sierra Nevada near Granada is home to Europe’s southernmost ski resort, with its base at about 2,100 m. Fourteen Vuelta stages have finished here since 1979, the latest in 2022; in 2017 the finish was at the Alto Hoya de la Mora.

 Climb · Trentino / Veneto

Passo Fedaia (Marmolada)

2,057 m 0 km · 0 % First time 1975
Giro 2022 · Stage 20

The Passo Fedaia (2,057 m) at the foot of the Marmolada is often simply called 'Marmolada' by cyclists; its notorious straight above Malga Ciapela runs about three kilometres at over 12 %. The Giro first crossed it in 1975 and finished on top in 2008 and 2022.

 Finish · Piemonte

Sestriere

2,035 m 9 Visits First time 1911
Giro 2025 · Stage 20Giro 2020 · Stage 20

Sestriere (2,035 m) is a ski resort on the pass of the same name between the Val Chisone and the Susa valley. The Giro first went above 2,000 m here in 1911, and nine stages have finished in the resort since 1991, often after the Colle delle Finestre.

 Climb · Castilla y León

La Covatilla

2,000 m 19.9 km · 5.8 % 6 Visits First time 2002
Vuelta 2020 · Stage 17

La Covatilla is a ski resort in the Sierra de Béjar (Salamanca) that rises to 2,368 m. The climb from Béjar is 19.9 km at 5.8% with sections up to 16.4%; six Vuelta stages have finished here since 2002, the latest in 2020.

 Climb · Aragón

Observatorio Astrofísico de Javalambre

1,957 m 2 Visits First time 2019
Vuelta 2023 · Stage 6

The Javalambre Astrophysical Observatory sits on the Pico del Buitre (1,957 m) in the Sierra de Javalambre in Teruel province. The road up to it is a well-known cycling climb; the Vuelta finished here in 2019 with Ángel Madrazo and in 2023 with Sepp Kuss.

 Finish · Sicilia

Etna

1,900 m 7 Visits First time 1967
Giro 2020 · Stage 3

Mount Etna (about 3,400 m), Europe's highest active volcano, has hosted seven Giro stage finishes since 1967, mostly at the Rifugio Sapienza at about 1,900 m. The climb from the coast near Catania gains some 1,850 m at 6–7 % through lava fields.

 Climb · Andalucía

La Pandera

1,872 m 7 Visits First time 2002
Vuelta 2026 · Stage 14Vuelta 2022 · Stage 14

La Pandera (1,872 m) is the highest point of the Sierra Sur de Jaén and carries a military station on its summit. The Vuelta finished here in 2002, 2003, 2006, 2009, 2017, 2022 and 2026, starting with a win for Roberto Heras.

 Climb · Lombardia

Passo del Mortirolo

1,852 m 12.4 km · 10.5 % First time 1990
Giro 2027 · Stage 16

The Mortirolo (Passo della Foppa, 1,852 m) between Valtellina and Valcamonica is, from Mazzo, one of the hardest climbs in Europe. It debuted in the Giro in 1990, and a monument recalls Marco Pantani, who made his breakthrough here in 1994.

 Climb · Asturias

Alto del Gamoniteiru

1,791 m 15.1 km · 9.7 % 1 Visits First time 2021
Vuelta 2021 · Stage 18

The Gamoniteiru (1,791 m) in Asturias is 15.1 km long from Pola de Lena, averaging 9.7% with 1,465 m of ascent. After being resurfaced it hosted its first Vuelta finish in 2021, when Miguel Ángel López won stage 18.

 Finish · Friuli-Venezia Giulia

Monte Lussari

1,790 m 1 Visits First time 2023
Giro 2023 · Stage 20

Monte Santo di Lussari (1,790 m) above Tarvisio in the Julian Alps is topped by a pilgrimage village with a sanctuary founded in the 16th century. In 2023 Primož Roglič won the Giro here in the mountain time trial.

 Climb · Veneto

Monte Grappa

1,775 m

Monte Grappa (1,775 m) in the Venetian Prealps, crowned by a First World War memorial, is a long climb above Bassano del Grappa. Nairo Quintana won the 2014 mountain time trial to Cima Grappa, and in 2024 the penultimate stage crossed it twice.

 Climb · Friuli-Venezia Giulia

Monte Zoncolan

1,750 m 10 km · 11.9 % 7 Visits First time 2003
Giro 2027 · Stage 20Giro 2021 · Stage 14

Monte Zoncolan (1,750 m) in the Carnic Alps is, from Ovaro, one of the steepest summit finishes in pro cycling, averaging almost 12 % with ramps of 22 %. Seven Giro stages have finished on top since 2003, and it returns in 2027.

 Climb · Savoie

Montée de Bisanne

1,723 m 12.4 km · 8.2 % 2 Visits First time 2016
Tour 2027 · Stage 11

The Montée de Bisanne climbs the D123 from Villard-sur-Doron to the junction below the Signal de Bisanne (1941 m), the high point of the Les Saisies resort. At 12.4 km and 8.2% it is rated hors catégorie; in 2027 it precedes the finish at Les Saisies (stage 11).

 Climb · Asturias / León

Alto de La Farrapona

1,708 m 18.7 km · 5.8 % 4 Visits First time 2011
Vuelta 2025 · Stage 14Vuelta 2020 · Stage 11

The Alto de La Farrapona (1,708 m) links Asturias and León between the Somiedo and Babia y Luna nature parks, with the Lagos de Saliencia near the top. The climb is 18.7 km at 5.8%, the last 6.7 km averaging over 8%. Four Vuelta stages have finished here since 2011.

 Climb · Savoie

Col du Pré

1,703 m 12.2 km · 7.9 % 3 Visits First time 2018
Tour 2027 · Stage 11

The Col du Pré in the Beaufortain separates Arêches-Beaufort from the Lac de Roselend; the narrow road from Beaufort is steep and irregular. The Tour has crossed it three times, each time rated hors catégorie.

 Finish · Aragón

Valdelinares

1,695 m 3 Visits First time 2005
Vuelta 2026 · Stage 7

Valdelinares (1,695 m) in Teruel province is the highest municipal seat in Spain; the Aramón Valdelinares ski resort lies seven kilometres away. The Vuelta finished here in 2005 with Roberto Heras, in 2014 with Winner Anacona and in 2026 with Andreas Leknessund.

 Climb · Savoie

Les Saisies

1,650 m 14.8 km · 5 % 14 Visits First time 1979
Tour 2027 · Stage 11

The Col des Saisies links the Val d'Arly with the Beaufortain, and the ski resort of the same name, known for cross-country skiing, sits on the pass. The Tour has crossed it 14 times as a classified climb; a stage finish at Les Saisies is announced for the first time in 2027 (stage 11).

 Climb · Haute-Savoie

Col de la Colombière

1,607 m 16.3 km · 6.8 % 23 Visits First time 1960
Tour 2027 · Stage 11

The Col de la Colombière in the Bornes massif links the Arve valley at Scionzier with Le Grand-Bornand. It has featured 23 times in the Tour since 1960, rated first category since 1978.

 Climb · Castilla y León

La Camperona

1,597 m 3 Visits First time 2014

La Camperona (1,597 m) is a mountain in the Cantabrian Mountains near Sabero in León province; its road was built to serve a TV relay. Beyond Sotillos de Sabero it climbs at up to 19.5%, and the Vuelta finished here in 2014, 2016 and 2018.

 Climb · Asturias

Alto de l'Angliru

1,573 m 12.5 km · 10.1 % 10 Visits First time 1999
Vuelta 2025 · Stage 13Vuelta 2023 · Stage 17Vuelta 2020 · Stage 12

The Angliru in Asturias (1,573 m) is one of the toughest summit finishes in pro cycling, averaging 10.1% over 12.5 km with ramps of around 24%. The Vuelta wanted a rival to Alpe d’Huez and the Mortirolo and first used it in 1999; ten stages have finished here, the latest in 2025.

 Climb · Hautes-Pyrénées

Hourquette d'Ancizan

1,564 m 10.3 km · 7.8 % 6 Visits First time 2011
Tour 2027 · Stage 18

The Hourquette d’Ancizan (1,564 m) links the Payolle valley with the Vallée d’Aure. The Tour first crossed it in 2011.

 Climb · Pyrénées-Atlantiques

Col de Soudet

1,533 m 22 km · 5.5 % 10 Visits First time 1987
Tour 2027 · Stage 19

The Col du Soudet lies near the Spanish border below the La Pierre-Saint-Martin ski station. It links the Barétous valley with the Soule.

 Finish · Trentino

Madonna di Campiglio

1,522 m 3 Visits First time 1999
Giro 2027 · Stage 18Giro 2020 · Stage 17

Madonna di Campiglio (1,522 m) is a ski resort in Trentino and the main gateway to the Brenta Dolomites. Marco Pantani won here in 1999 before being expelled the next morning for a high haematocrit level; the Giro returns in 2027.

 Climb · Aragón

Cerler

1,500 m 12 Visits First time 1987
Vuelta 2025 · Stage 7

Cerler in the Benasque valley (Huesca province) is a ski resort whose slopes reach 2,650 m. Twelve Vuelta stages have finished here since Laudelino Cubino won in 1987, the latest in 2025 with Juan Ayuso.

 Climb · Ain

Grand Colombier

1,498 m 18.3 km · 6.9 % 5 Visits First time 2012
Tour 2027 · Stage 9Tour 2023 · Stage 13Tour 2020 · Stage 15

The Grand Colombier at the southern end of the Jura is considered one of the hardest climbs in France, with four ascents and ramps above 20% on the western side. The Tour first came in 2012, with summit finishes in 2020 and 2023; another is planned for 2027 (stage 9).

 Climb · Hautes-Pyrénées

Col du Soulor

1,474 m 19.5 km · 5.2 % 55 Visits First time 1910
Tour 2027 · Stage 18

The Col du Soulor lies on the road from Argelès-Gazost to the Col d’Aubisque and is usually crossed together with it. Only some of its Tour crossings counted for the mountains classification.

 Climb · Haute-Savoie

Col de la Croix Fry

1,467 m 12.8 km · 6.6 % 6 Visits First time 1994
Tour 2027 · Stage 11

The Col de la Croix Fry in the Aravis range links the Manigod valley with the Nom valley near Saint-Jean-de-Sixt. The Tour has crossed it six times, always rated first category.

 Finish · Abruzzo

Prati di Tivo

1,450 m 14.5 km · 7 % 2 Visits First time 1975
Giro 2024 · Stage 8

Prati di Tivo is a mountain resort below the Corno Piccolo on the Teramo side of the Gran Sasso; the climb via Pietracamela is 14.5 km at 7 %. The Giro finished here in 1975 and 2024, won by Giovanni Battaglin and Tadej Pogačar.

 Finish · Lazio

Monte Livata (Subiaco)

1,429 m 19 km · 6 %
Giro 2027 · Stage 9

Monte Livata is a ski and holiday resort in the Monti Simbruini above Subiaco; the road from Subiaco climbs about 1,200 m over roughly 19 km at around 6 %. Stage 9 of the 2027 Giro finishes here; until now the climb was known from Tirreno–Adriatico in 1975.

 Finish · Toscana / Emilia-Romagna

Passo dei Due Santi (Zeri)

1,392 m
Giro 2027 · Stage 12

The Passo dei Due Santi (1,392 m), formerly Passo del Faggio Crociato, links Zeri in Lunigiana with Albareto in the Parma Apennines, with the small Zum Zeri ski area at the top. It is named after two statues of the Virgin Mary and St Anthony, and in 2027 a Giro stage finishes here for the first time.

 Climb · Pyrénées-Atlantiques

Col de Bagargui

1,330 m 13 km · 5.4 % 3 Visits First time 1986
Tour 2027 · Stage 19

The Col Bagargui (Basque Bagargi) lies in the Iraty forest in the Basque part of the Pyrénées-Atlantiques. The Tour crossed it three times: 1986, 1987 and 2003.

 Finish · Lombardia

Aprica

1,181 m 10 Visits First time 1962
Giro 2027 · Stage 16Giro 2022 · Stage 16

Aprica sits on the pass of the same name (about 1,180 m) between Valtellina and Valcamonica, close to the Mortirolo, Santa Cristina and Gavia. It has been a Giro stage finish ten times since 1962 and hosts one again in 2027.

 Finish · Piemonte

Santuario di Oropa

1,150 m 13 km · 5.8 % 7 Visits First time 1963
Giro 2024 · Stage 2

The Marian sanctuary of Oropa lies at about 1,150 m above Biella; the road from Biella gains about 750 m over 13 km. The Giro has finished here seven times, most famously in 1999 when Marco Pantani recovered from a dropped chain to win.

 Climb · Pyrénées-Atlantiques

Col de Burdincurutcheta

1,141 m 13 km · 0 % 3 Visits First time 1986
Tour 2027 · Stage 19

The Col de Burdincurutcheta (Basque Burdinkurutxeta, "iron cross") lies in Lower Navarre and leads into the Iraty forest. The Tour crossed it in 1986, 1987 and 2003.

 Climb · Asturias

Lagos de Covadonga

1,134 m 14 km · 6.9 % 23 Visits First time 1983
Vuelta 2024 · Stage 16Vuelta 2021 · Stage 17

The Lagos de Covadonga (1,134 m) are two glacial lakes, Enol and Ercina, in the Picos de Europa; the climb from the Covadonga sanctuary is 14 km at 6.9%. Since Marino Lejarreta won here in 1983, 23 Vuelta stages have finished at the lakes, the latest in 2024.

 Climb · Comunitat Valenciana

Xorret de Catí

1,100 m 4 km · 10.9 % 7 Visits First time 1998
Vuelta 2023 · Stage 8

The Xorret de Catí in the Sierra del Fraile (Alicante province, about 1,100 m) is a short, brutal climb: around 4 km with 437 m of ascent and ramps of up to 22%. Seven Vuelta stages have finished on it since José María Jiménez won here in 1998.

 Finish · Abruzzo

Campo di Giove

1,064 m
Giro 2027 · Stage 7

Campo di Giove (1,064 m) is a mountain village and ski resort on the south-western slope of the Maiella above Sulmona. In 2027 it hosts the finish of the stage 7 individual time trial (19.4 km from Sulmona).

 Finish · Lozère

Côte de la Croix Neuve

1,045 m 3.1 km · 10.1 % 6 Visits First time 1995
Tour 2027 · Stage 14Tour 2022 · Stage 14

The Côte de la Croix Neuve climbs steeply from Mende onto the causse to the Mende-Brenoux aerodrome, where Tour stages finish. Since 2005 it has also been called "Montée Laurent Jalabert" after his 1995 win.

 Climb · Ain

Col de Richemond

1,036 m 8.5 km · 3.9 % 1 Visits First time 2012
Tour 2027 · Stage 9

The Col de Richemond in the Jura links the Valromey with the Genevois near Bellegarde-sur-Valserine; a memorial at the top honours resistance fighters killed in 1944. According to French Wikipedia the Tour first crossed it in 2012 on the way to Bellegarde.

 Climb · Pyrénées-Atlantiques

Col de Marie Blanque

1,035 m 9.3 km · 7.7 % 16 Visits First time 1978
Tour 2027 · Stage 19

The Col de Marie-Blanque (1,035 m) links the Aspe and Ossau valleys. The western side from Escot is short but very steep over its final four kilometres.

 Climb · Cantabria

Alto de Los Machucos

921 m 14 km · 7.8 % 2 Visits First time 2017

The Alto de Los Machucos (921 m) in the Pasiegos mountains of Cantabria is a road built only in 2003–2007: about 14 km long, with ramps of 15–20% and up to 28%. The Vuelta finished here in 2017 (Stefan Denifl) and 2019 (Tadej Pogačar); a monument to the Pasiega cow stands at the top.

 Finish · Andalucía

Valdepeñas de Jaén

920 m 4 Visits First time 2010
Vuelta 2021 · Stage 11

Valdepeñas de Jaén (920 m) lies in the Sierra Sur of Jaén province. The final kilometre of the Vuelta finish has ramps of up to 23%; a stage has ended here four times (2010, 2011, 2013 and 2021), the latest won by Primož Roglič.

 Climb · Asturias

Les Praeres

892 m 4.7 km · 13.5 % 2 Visits First time 2018
Vuelta 2022 · Stage 9

Les Praeres (892 m) near Nava in Asturias is a short wall of a climb: 4.7 km with 637 m of ascent, averaging 13.5%. The Vuelta finished here in 2018 with a win for Simon Yates and in 2022 with one for Louis Meintjes.

 Climb · Haute-Savoie

Côte de Domancy

810 m 2.5 km · 9.4 % 3 Visits First time 2016
Tour 2027 · Stage 10

The short, steep Côte de Domancy above Sallanches is famous from the 1980 World Road Championships, where Bernard Hinault built his winning move here; the road is now called 'Route Bernard Hinault'. In the Tour it featured in two mountain time trials (2016, 2023) and as a classified climb on an Alpine stage in 2021.

 Finish · Comunidad de Madrid

Madrid

660 m 55 Visits First time 1935
Vuelta 2025 · Stage 21Vuelta 2024 · Stage 21Vuelta 2023 · Stage 21Vuelta 2022 · Stage 21Vuelta 2020 · Stage 18

Madrid is the Vuelta’s traditional finish: the last stage ended in the capital in 55 of the 80 editions so far, first in 1935 with Gustaaf Deloor. It is usually a sprint finish, and in 2002 the line was at the Santiago Bernabéu stadium.

 Climb · Asturias

Alto del Naranco

600 m 5.5 km · 6.9 % 14 Visits First time 1974

Monte Naranco above Oviedo is a Vuelta classic: 5.5 km at 6.9% up to about 600 m. José Manuel Fuente won the first stage finish here in 1974, and the Vuelta has ended on the hill 14 times, most recently in 2016.

 Climb · Cantabria

Peña Cabarga

569 m 5 Visits First time 1979

Peña Cabarga (569 m) is a limestone massif on the southern shore of the Bay of Santander in Cantabria. A Vuelta stage has finished here five times (1979, 2010, 2011, 2013 and 2016), with Chris Froome winning in 2011 and 2016.

 Climb · Galicia

Mirador de Ézaro

274 m 1.8 km · 14.8 % 4 Visits First time 2012
Vuelta 2020 · Stage 13

The Mirador de Ézaro (274 m), above the mouth of the Río Xallas on Galicia’s Costa da Morte, is a short climb of 1.8 km at 14.8% on average and up to 28%. The Vuelta came here in 2012, 2013, 2016 and 2020, with the stage ending on top three times.

 Finish · Galicia

Santiago de Compostela

260 m 8 Visits First time 1942
Vuelta 2021 · Stage 21

Santiago de Compostela has been a Vuelta finish eight times, the first in 1942. Three editions ended here with a final time trial: 1993 (Alex Zülle), 2014 (Adriano Malori) and 2021 (Primož Roglič).

 Finish · Morbihan

Côte de Cadoudal

156 m 1.7 km · 6.2 % 6 Visits First time 1982
Tour 2027 · Stage 5

The Côte de Cadoudal is the 1.7 km, 6.2% rise from the Manoir de Cadoudal up into Plumelec in the Landes de Lanvaux. The Tour has finished here five times (including the 1985 prologue and two team time trials), with another finish planned for 2027.

 Climb · Paris

Côte de la Butte Montmartre

130 m 1.1 km · 5.9 % 2 Visits First time 2025
Tour 2027 · Stage 21

The Côte de la Butte Montmartre, via the cobbled Rue Lepic, was added to the Tour’s final stage after the 2024 Olympics. In 2025 and 2026 it was climbed three times before the Champs-Élysées finish.

 Finish · Paris

Paris Champs-Élysées

51 Visits First time 1975
Tour 2027 · Stage 21Tour 2026 · Stage 21Tour 2025 · Stage 21Tour 2023 · Stage 21Tour 2022 · Stage 21Tour 2021 · Stage 21Tour 2020 · Stage 21

Since 1975 the Tour de France has finished on the Champs-Élysées, usually with a bunch sprint after several laps. The only exception was 2024, when the race ended in Nice because of the Olympics.

 City · Saône-et-Loire

Le Creusot

3 Visits First time 1998
Tour 2027 · Stage 8Tour 2021 · Stage 7

Le Creusot is an old industrial town in Saône-et-Loire; to its west rises the Signal d'Uchon (681 m), one of Burgundy's steepest climbs (3.2 km at 9.2%). The Tour visited in 1998, 2006 and 2021, and the longest stage of 2027 (Bourges–Le Creusot) finishes here again.

 Climb · Hérault

Cirque de Navacelles

0
Tour 2027 · Stage 14

The Cirque de Navacelles is a cut-off meander of the Vis river between the Causse du Larzac and the Causse de Blandas, on the Hérault–Gard border, labelled 'Grand Site de France' since 2016. No Tour de France passage is documented in the sources so far, so the history is empty.

 City · Lazio

Roma (Fori Imperiali)

52 Visits
Giro 2027 · Stage 21Giro 2026 · Stage 21Giro 2025 · Stage 21Giro 2024 · Stage 21Giro 2023 · Stage 21

Since 2023 Rome has been the Giro's permanent final venue, with the last stage on a circuit around the Colosseum, the Via dei Fori Imperiali and the Circus Maximus. Before that the race had ended in the capital only in 1911, 1950, 2009 and 2018.

 City · Friuli-Venezia Giulia

Trieste

22 Visits
Giro 2027 · Stage 1

Trieste has been a Giro stage town 40 times (18 starts, 22 finishes) and hosted the final finish three times, in 1966, 1973 and 2014. The 1981 Giro began here with a prologue from the Piazza Unità d'Italia, and the 2027 race starts in Trieste again.

 Finish · Piemonte

Alpe di Mera

1 Visits First time 2021
Giro 2021 · Stage 19

Alpe di Mera is a ski area in the Valsesia above Scopello in the Pennine Alps, lying between 1,500 and 1,700 m. It hosted stage 19 of the 2021 Giro, won by Simon Yates.

 Climb · Comunitat Valenciana

Cumbre del Sol (Benitachell)

1 Visits First time 2015

The Cumbre del Sol near Benitachell (Alicante province), also called the Alto de Puig Llorença, is a climb of just over 3 km to a residential development. The Vuelta finished here once: Tom Dumoulin won stage 9 in 2015.
