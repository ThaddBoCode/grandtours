In the headlines

Giro 2026 · Stage 11

Narváez makes it three in Chiavari

Jhonatan Narváez (UAE Team Emirates XRG) made the day's breakaway and beat Enric Mas in a two-up sprint in Chiavari for his third stage win, with Diego Ulissi and Chris Harper 11 seconds back. Stage 6 winner Davide Ballerini abandoned, while Afonso Eulálio stayed 27 seconds ahead of Jonas Vingegaard overall.

 Tour 2024 · Stage 17

Carapaz wins solo at SuperDévoluy

Richard Carapaz went clear of the chasing group on the Col du Noyer, caught and dropped Simon Yates and won alone at SuperDévoluy, 37 seconds ahead of Yates and 57 ahead of Enric Mas. It was his first Tour stage win, making him a stage winner in all three Grand Tours. Among the favourites, Remco Evenepoel gained a few seconds on Pogačar and Vingegaard.

 Tour 2024 · Stage 20

Pogačar wins on the Couillole, Vingegaard secures second

On the last mountain stage, Remco Evenepoel attacked twice on the final climb to the Col de la Couillole, but Jonas Vingegaard countered and only Tadej Pogačar could follow. The pair caught Enric Mas and Richard Carapaz 2.5 km from the top, and Pogačar launched his sprint 150 m from the line to win 7 seconds ahead of Vingegaard. Evenepoel lost 53 seconds, which secured second overall for Vingegaard.

 Tour 2023 · Stage 1

Basque Grand Départ: Yates twins one-two, Mas and Carapaz out

The Tour started in Bilbao, the second Grand Départ in the Basque Country after 1992. Twins Adam and Simon Yates broke away in the finale of stage 1 and finished first and second, giving Adam the first yellow jersey. A crash ended the race for pre-race favourites Enric Mas and Richard Carapaz, who did not start stage 2 with a fractured kneecap.

 Tour 2022 · Stage 19

Laporte takes the first French stage win of this Tour

Protesters on the road briefly neutralised the race early on, and Enric Mas had left with COVID-19 before the start. After Alexis Gougeard, Fred Wright and Jasper Stuyven had been reeled in, Christophe Laporte jumped from the peloton in the final two kilometres and held off the sprinters in Cahors by one second, with Jasper Philipsen second. It was the first stage win by a French rider in this Tour.

 Tour 2021 · Stage 18

Pogačar doubles up at Luz Ardiden and takes polka dots

Pierre Latour was first over the Col du Tourmalet, and David Gaudu was the last of the breakaway to be caught on the final climb. Tadej Pogačar attacked 3.3 km from the top of Luz Ardiden, closed down two moves by Enric Mas in the final kilometre and won ahead of Jonas Vingegaard and Richard Carapaz, both 2 seconds back. With 80 points from his two summit wins Pogačar also took the polka-dot jersey, and his lead grew to 5:45 over Vingegaard; Rigoberto Urán lost almost nine minutes.
