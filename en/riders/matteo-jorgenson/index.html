In the headlines

Tour 2026 · Stage 18

Carapaz solos to victory at Orcières-Merlette

Pedersen joined a large breakaway and took maximum points at the intermediate sprint, extending his lead over Philipsen to 32 points. Valentin Paret-Peintre chased mountain points over the climbs, but Carapaz was strongest on the final ascent, going clear 3.5 km from the line to win ahead of Mauro Schmid and Matteo Jorgenson. The favourites finished together, while illness in the UAE team forced Pogačar's teammate Brandon McNulty out of the race.

 Vuelta 2025 · Stage 9

Vingegaard soloes to Valdezcaray, Træen narrowly keeps red

After Matteo Jorgenson set a furious pace, Jonas Vingegaard attacked on the final climb to the Valdezcaray ski resort; only Giulio Ciccone followed for a kilometre before Vingegaard soloed to the win, 24 seconds ahead of Tom Pidcock and João Almeida. Torstein Træen kept the red jersey, with his lead over Vingegaard cut to 37 seconds.

 Tour 2024 · Stage 19

Pogačar reels in the breakaway at Isola 2000

After the Col de Vars and the Cime de la Bonette, Tadej Pogačar attacked 10 km from the summit finish at Isola 2000 and caught the last escapee, Matteo Jorgenson, 1.9 km from the line. He won 21 seconds ahead of Jorgenson, with Simon Yates third, and extended his lead over Vingegaard to 5:03. Remco Evenepoel could not drop Jonas Vingegaard, and Richard Carapaz took the polka-dot jersey from the breakaway.

 Tour 2023 · Stage 9

The Tour returns to the Puy de Dôme after 35 years

For the first time since 1988 a stage finished on top of the Puy de Dôme. Matteo Jorgenson attacked from the breakaway 47 km from the finish and led alone until Michael Woods passed him in the final 500 m to win; behind, Pogačar took 8 seconds out of Vingegaard.

 Tour 2023 · Stage 12

Ion Izagirre solos to Belleville-en-Beaujolais

Fabio Jakobsen did not start and David de la Cruz crashed out. After a fierce fight for the breakaway, Ion Izagirre went clear on the Col de la Croix Rosier and rode the last 30 km or so alone, winning by 58 seconds from Mathieu Burgaudeau and Matteo Jorgenson. It was Cofidis' second stage win of the Tour after Lafay's.
