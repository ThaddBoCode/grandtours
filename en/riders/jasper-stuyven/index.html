In the headlines

Giro 2026 · Stage 6

Last-corner crash in Naples hands Ballerini the win

A few hundred metres from the finish in Naples, the Unibet Rose Rockets lead-out crashed on wet cobbles approaching the final corner, disrupting the sprint. Only Davide Ballerini and Jasper Stuyven, on the inside of the bend, were unaffected – Ballerini won, with Paul Magnier recovering to third.

 Giro 2026 · Stage 13

Bettiol solos to victory in Verbania

The peloton let the breakaway go, and Alberto Bettiol (XDS Astana) dropped his companions on the final climb. He won in Verbania 26 seconds ahead of Andreas Leknessund and 44 ahead of Jasper Stuyven, with no change in the overall standings.

 Tour 2022 · Stage 5

Chaos on the cobbles: Roglič dislocates his shoulder

Stage 5 brought cobbles back to the Tour for the first time since 2018. Vingegaard needed three bike changes after a mechanical and Roglič crashed, dislocated his shoulder and lost more than two minutes, while Pogačar attacked with Jasper Stuyven and gained time on all his rivals. Roglič did not start stage 15, the day Steven Kruijswijk also crashed out.

 Tour 2022 · Stage 19

Laporte takes the first French stage win of this Tour

Protesters on the road briefly neutralised the race early on, and Enric Mas had left with COVID-19 before the start. After Alexis Gougeard, Fred Wright and Jasper Stuyven had been reeled in, Christophe Laporte jumped from the peloton in the final two kilometres and held off the sprinters in Cahors by one second, with Jasper Philipsen second. It was the first stage win by a French rider in this Tour.

 Tour 2021 · Stage 7

Mohorič solos to Le Creusot as Roglič loses time

On the 249.1 km stage, the longest of the race, a 29-rider group including Mathieu van der Poel and Wout van Aert went clear and Matej Mohorič attacked alone on the Signal d'Uchon. He won by 1:20 over Jasper Stuyven, took the polka-dot jersey and completed his set of stage wins in all three Grand Tours. Van der Poel extended his lead to 30 seconds over van Aert, while Primož Roglič lost more than three minutes to the other favourites.

 Tour 2020 · Stage 19

Kragh Andersen doubles up in Champagnole

After Rémi Cavagna's early solo attack was neutralised, a 12-rider group including Sam Bennett, Peter Sagan and Matteo Trentin went clear in the finale. Søren Kragh Andersen attacked 16 km from the finish and won alone by 53 seconds ahead of Luka Mezgec and Jasper Stuyven – his second stage win of this Tour. The general classification remained unchanged before the time trial.
