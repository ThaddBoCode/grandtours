In the headlines

Tour 2024 · Stage 5

Cavendish takes his 35th Tour stage win and the record

On the flat run to Saint-Vulbas, Mark Cavendish won the sprint ahead of Jasper Philipsen and Alexander Kristoff for his 35th Tour de France stage win, breaking the record he had shared with Eddy Merckx. Mads Pedersen crashed in the final metres, and Phil Bauhaus was relegated for a dangerous change of line. Biniam Girmay took the green jersey, the first African rider to wear it.

 Tour 2024 · Stage 16

Philipsen's third win in Nîmes as Girmay crashes

Thomas Gachignard rode alone at the front for much of the second half but was caught 25 km from Nîmes. Green jersey Biniam Girmay crashed 1.5 km from the finish, and Jasper Philipsen, led out by Robbe Ghys and Mathieu van der Poel, won ahead of Phil Bauhaus and Alexander Kristoff. It was Philipsen's third stage win, matching Girmay's tally.

 Tour 2022 · Stage 21

Philipsen wins on the Champs-Élysées, Vingegaard crowned in Paris

Jasper Philipsen won the sprint on the Champs-Élysées ahead of Dylan Groenewegen and Alexander Kristoff for his second stage win of the Tour. Vingegaard crossed the line with his teammates, losing 51 seconds, and won the Tour by 2:43 over Pogačar, who took the white jersey for the third time, equalling Jan Ullrich and Andy Schleck. Van Aert won the green jersey, Vingegaard the polka-dot jersey and Ineos Grenadiers the team classification.

 Tour 2020 · Stage 1

Rain and crashes mar the Grand Départ in Nice

The opening stage around Nice was raced on rain-soaked roads that brought crash after crash; the peloton agreed a truce, and a big pile-up still struck 3 km from the finish. Philippe Gilbert (broken kneecap) and Rafael Valls (broken leg) did not start the next day and John Degenkolb finished outside the time limit, while Alexander Kristoff won the sprint for the first yellow jersey.

 Tour 2020 · Stage 2

Alaphilippe attacks, wins in Nice and takes yellow

On the last climb, the Col des Quatre Chemins, Julian Alaphilippe attacked and only Marc Hirschi and Adam Yates could join him. The trio stayed two seconds clear of the peloton and Alaphilippe beat Hirschi in the sprint to take the yellow jersey, 4 seconds ahead of Yates; Hirschi took white. Alexander Kristoff was dropped early and lost more than 28 minutes.
