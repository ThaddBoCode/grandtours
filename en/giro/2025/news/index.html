Stories

The big stories of the Giro and the story of every stage – updated continuously.

Headlines

🏆Stage 20

Simon Yates wins the Giro on the Colle delle Finestre

Simon Yates (Visma–Lease a Bike) won his first Giro d'Italia in 82h 31' 01", taking the maglia rosa only on the penultimate stage. He finished 3:56 ahead of Isaac del Toro and 4:43 ahead of former Giro winner Richard Carapaz – without winning a stage himself.

🔥Stage 20

Yates attacks as del Toro and Carapaz watch each other

On stage 20, Yates attacked 15 km from the summit of the Colle delle Finestre while del Toro and Carapaz marked each other and refused to cooperate. On the descent he was paced by teammate Wout van Aert, who had been in the break; Yates gained more than five minutes on both and turned a 1:21 deficit into a 3:56 lead.

🏆Stage 9

Del Toro leads for eleven stages and takes the maglia bianca

Isaac del Toro (UAE Team Emirates XRG), Juan Ayuso's teammate, took the maglia rosa on the gravel stage to Siena and led from stage 9 to stage 19, winning stage 17 in Bormio. He finished second overall and won the young rider classification, 5:32 ahead of Giulio Pellizzari.

⚠️Stage 16

Pre-race favourites Roglič and Ayuso both abandon

The two pre-race favourites did not reach Rome. Primož Roglič, the 2023 winner, lost time after crashing and puncturing on the gravel to Siena, was caught up in the crash on stage 14 and abandoned on stage 16. Juan Ayuso, winner at Tagliacozzo and second overall for much of the race, was dropped on stage 16 and withdrew on stage 18 after a bee sting.

⚡2025

Pedersen wins four stages and the maglia ciclamino

Mads Pedersen won stages 1, 3, 5 and 13, led the general classification after stage 1 and after stages 3 to 6, and won the points classification with 295 points ahead of Olav Kooij (185) and Wout van Aert (127).

•Stage 1

Grande Partenza in Albania

The Giro started on 9 May in Durrës, Albania with three stages there: Durrës to Tirana, a time trial in Tirana and a loop from Vlorë over the Llogara pass. A rest day allowed the transfer across the Adriatic before the race resumed in Alberobello; Mikel Landa crashed out 5 km from the finish of the opening stage.

⚠️Stage 6

Mass crash on the way to Naples: race neutralised, Hindley out

On the 227 km stage 6 to Naples, the race was neutralised between km 156 and 166.5 after a mass crash in the peloton; former Giro winner Jai Hindley abandoned. No bonus seconds were awarded at the finish, the time gaps did not count for the general classification, and many favourites such as Pedersen and Roglič dropped back for safety reasons.

🔥Stage 9

Van Aert wins on the white roads to Siena

Stage 9 crossed five gravel sectors of the Strade Bianche on the way to the Piazza del Campo. Wout van Aert was the only rider able to follow del Toro's attacks and passed him in the final metres, while Roglič crashed, punctured and dropped to tenth overall, 2:25 down.

•Stage 16

Fortunato takes the maglia azzurra and the combativity award

Lorenzo Fortunato (XDS Astana Team) led the mountains classification from stage 3 to Rome and won it with 355 points ahead of teammate Christian Scaroni (201); he was also named the race's most combative rider. On stage 16 the two rode clear together to San Valentino, where Fortunato left the win to Scaroni.

•Stage 21

Final stage starts in the Vatican Gardens

On 29 April it was announced that the final stage would start in the Vatican Gardens to celebrate the 2025 Jubilee and pay homage to Pope Francis, who died on 21 April. Pope Leo XIV welcomed the riders at the start; in Rome, Olav Kooij won the sprint.

Stage by stage

1 Fri 9 May · Durrës → Tirana · Review Pedersen edges van Aert in Tirana for the first maglia rosa The Giro opened in Albania with a hilly stage from Durrës to Tirana, where a crash 5 km from the finish forced Mikel Landa out. Mads Pedersen won the sprint of a reduced group just ahead of Wout van Aert and Orluis Aular to take the maglia rosa and the maglia ciclamino. Sylvain Moniquet took the mountains lead from the day's break, Francesco Busatto the maglia bianca. 2 Sat 10 May · Tirana ↻ · Review Tarling wins the Tirana time trial, Roglič takes pink by a second Joshua Tarling won the 13.7 km time trial in Tirana by one second from Primož Roglič, with Jay Vine third at 3 seconds. Roglič took the maglia rosa, one second ahead of Mads Pedersen, and Mathias Vacek moved into the maglia bianca. 3 Sun 11 May · Vlorë ↻ · Review Pedersen sprints to victory in Vlorë and reclaims the maglia rosa Lorenzo Fortunato and Pello Bilbao crossed the Llogara pass together in front but were caught 17 km from the finish in Vlorë. Led out by Giulio Ciccone and Mathias Vacek, Mads Pedersen won the sprint ahead of Corbin Strong and Orluis Aular and took back the maglia rosa, 9 seconds ahead of Roglič. Fortunato moved into the maglia azzurra. 4 Tue 13 May · Alberobello → Lecce · Review Van Uden wins in Lecce as the Giro returns to Italy After the rest day and the transfer across the Adriatic, the race resumed on Italian soil in Alberobello. Francisco Muñoz spent more than 130 km alone in front, and in Lecce Casper van Uden won the sprint clearly ahead of Olav Kooij and Maikel Zijlaard. Pedersen stayed in pink. 5 Wed 14 May · Ceglie Messapica → Matera · Review Pedersen edges Zambanini in a photo finish at Matera Mads Pedersen lost ground on the short climb 2 km from the finish but came back to beat Edoardo Zambanini in a photo finish, with Tom Pidcock third. It was his third win in four road stages, and his lead over Roglič grew to 17 seconds. 6 Thu 15 May · Potenza → Naples · Review Groves wins in Naples after a crash and neutralisation The 227 km stage to Naples, the longest of the race, was neutralised between km 156 and 166.5 after a mass crash; Jai Hindley, Juri Hollmann and Dion Smith abandoned. No points or bonus seconds were awarded at the last intermediate sprint and the finish, and the time gaps did not count for the general classification. Kaden Groves won the sprint ahead of Milan Fretin and Paul Magnier. 7 Fri 16 May · Castel di Sangro → Tagliacozzo · Review Ayuso wins at Tagliacozzo, Roglič back in pink On the first big mountain stage Pedersen was dropped early on the final climb to Tagliacozzo. Juan Ayuso went clear 500 m from the finish and won by 4 seconds from his teammate Isaac del Toro, Egan Bernal and Primož Roglič. Roglič took back the maglia rosa, 4 seconds ahead of Ayuso, who also took the maglia bianca. 8 Sat 17 May · Giulianova → Castelraimondo · Review Plapp solos to Castelraimondo, Ulissi takes the maglia rosa Luke Plapp won from the breakaway with a 46 km solo, finishing 38 seconds ahead of Wilco Kelderman and Diego Ulissi. Ulissi took the maglia rosa, 12 seconds ahead of Lorenzo Fortunato, while the group of favourites finished 4:50 behind the winner. 9 Sun 18 May · Gubbio → Siena · Review Van Aert beats del Toro on the gravel to Siena Five gravel sectors split the race on the way to Siena, where Roglič and Tom Pidcock crashed and punctured. Isaac del Toro attacked twice, only Wout van Aert could follow, and the Belgian passed the Mexican in the final metres at the Piazza del Campo. Del Toro took the maglia rosa, 1:13 ahead of Ayuso, and the maglia bianca, while Roglič dropped to tenth at 2:25. 10 Tue 20 May · Lucca → Pisa · Review Hoole wins the Pisa time trial, del Toro defends pink Rain arrived during the 28.6 km time trial from Lucca to Pisa and favoured the early starters. Daan Hoole won by 7 seconds from Joshua Tarling and 10 from Ethan Hayter. Del Toro kept the maglia rosa, but his lead over Ayuso shrank to 25 seconds. 11 Wed 21 May · Viareggio → Castelnovo ne' Monti · Review Carapaz attacks late to win at Castelnovo ne' Monti Lorenzo Fortunato attacked from a large break 103 km from the finish, but the escapees were caught on the Pietra di Bismantova. Richard Carapaz went clear of the maglia rosa group 8 km from the line and won by 10 seconds from del Toro, Giulio Ciccone and Tom Pidcock. Del Toro stayed in pink, 31 seconds ahead of Ayuso. 12 Thu 22 May · Modena → Viadana · Review Kooij takes the bunch sprint in Viadana Italians Giosuè Epis, Andrea Pietrobon and Manuele Tarozzi formed the early break, with Pietrobon the last to be caught 29 km from the finish. Olav Kooij won the bunch sprint ahead of Casper van Uden and Ben Turner, and del Toro kept the maglia rosa. 13 Fri 23 May · Rovigo → Vicenza · Review Pedersen holds off van Aert uphill in Vicenza for his fourth win The finish in Vicenza climbed to the sanctuary of Monte Berico, with ramps of up to 12 %. Romain Bardet and Mathias Vacek were caught 500 m from the line, and maglia ciclamino Mads Pedersen launched his sprint from distance to hold off Wout van Aert for his fourth stage win, with del Toro third, 2 seconds back. Del Toro's lead over Ayuso grew to 38 seconds. 14 Sat 24 May · Treviso → Nova Gorica · Review Asgreen solos to Nova Gorica as a crash hits the favourites Kasper Asgreen, Mirco Maestri and Martin Marcellusi led for much of a rainy stage before Asgreen went alone 6 km from the finish in Nova Gorica, Slovenia, and won by 16 seconds from Kaden Groves and Olav Kooij. A crash 22 km from the finish brought down or held up Juan Ayuso, Primož Roglič, Antonio Tiberi and Giulio Ciccone, who did not start the next day. Simon Yates moved up to second overall, 1:20 behind del Toro. 15 Sun 25 May · Fiume Veneto → Asiago · Review Verona wins alone in Asiago, Roglič loses time Carlos Verona attacked from the break on the climb of Dori, 43 km from the finish, and won alone in Asiago, 22 seconds ahead of Florian Stork and 23 ahead of Christian Scaroni. Primož Roglič finished almost two minutes behind the winner and dropped to tenth overall, 3:53 down. Del Toro kept the maglia rosa, 1:20 ahead of Simon Yates. 16 Tue 27 May · Piazzola sul Brenta → San Valentino · Review Scaroni wins at San Valentino as Roglič quits and del Toro wobbles On a stage that began in very difficult weather, Primož Roglič abandoned and Juan Ayuso was dropped on the climb of Santa Barbara. XDS Astana teammates Christian Scaroni and Lorenzo Fortunato rode clear on the final climb, and Scaroni won at San Valentino. Del Toro was dropped and lost 1:36 to Richard Carapaz but kept the maglia rosa by 26 seconds over Simon Yates and 31 over Carapaz. 17 Wed 28 May · San Michele all'Adige → Bormio · Review Del Toro rebounds to win in Bormio After the Tonale and the Mortirolo, Richard Carapaz and maglia rosa Isaac del Toro caught Romain Bardet, the last of the break, before del Toro went clear alone to win in Bormio – his first Giro stage win. Bardet and Carapaz finished 4 seconds back; Carapaz moved up to second overall at 41 seconds, with Simon Yates third at 51 seconds. 18 Thu 29 May · Morbegno → Cesano Maderno · Review Denz solos to Cesano Maderno as Ayuso abandons Juan Ayuso abandoned early in the stage, while a 34-rider breakaway gained more than ten minutes. Nico Denz attacked from a leading group of eleven 18 km from the finish and won alone in Cesano Maderno, 1:01 ahead of Mirco Maestri and Edward Planckaert. The maglia rosa group finished 13:51 down, and the top of the standings was unchanged. 19 Fri 30 May · Biella → Champoluc · Review Prodhomme wins at Champoluc, del Toro answers Carapaz Nicolas Prodhomme attacked from the break on the Col de Joux, 28 km from the finish, and won alone in Champoluc. Maglia rosa Isaac del Toro answered every attack from Richard Carapaz, and the pair finished 58 seconds behind the winner and 24 seconds ahead of the other favourites. Before the last mountain stage del Toro led Carapaz by 43 seconds and Simon Yates by 1:21. 20 Sat 31 May · Verrès → Sestriere · Review Harper wins at Sestriere, Yates seizes pink on the Finestre On the Colle delle Finestre, Simon Yates attacked 15 km from the summit while Isaac del Toro and Richard Carapaz marked each other, and on the descent he was helped by teammate Wout van Aert from the break. Chris Harper won alone at Sestriere, 1:49 ahead of Alessandro Verre, with Yates third at 1:57. Yates took the maglia rosa with a lead of 3:56 over del Toro and 4:43 over Carapaz. 21 Sun 1 Jun · Vatican Gardens → Rome · Review Kooij wins in Rome, Yates seals his Giro The final stage started in the Vatican Gardens, where Pope Leo XIV welcomed the riders, and finished in Rome after eight laps of a local circuit. Olav Kooij won the sprint ahead of Kaden Groves and Matteo Moschetti, and his Visma–Lease a Bike teammate Simon Yates sealed overall victory, 3:56 ahead of Isaac del Toro.
