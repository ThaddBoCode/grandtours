Stories

The big stories of the Giro and the story of every stage – updated continuously.

Headlines

★2026

Vingegaard wins the Giro and completes the Grand Tour set

Jonas Vingegaard won the Giro d'Italia at his first attempt, becoming the eighth rider to win all three Grand Tours and the first to do so since Chris Froome in 2018. The Dane won five stages, four of them in the mountains, took the maglia rosa on stage 14 and finished in Rome 5:22 ahead of Felix Gall and 6:25 ahead of Jai Hindley.

🏆Stage 14

Solo to Pila: Vingegaard takes the maglia rosa

Despite wins at Blockhaus and Corno alle Scale, Vingegaard still trailed Afonso Eulálio until Visma–Lease a Bike set him up on stage 14 to Pila: he won solo and took the maglia rosa with a 2:26 lead. He extended it with further wins at Carì and Piancavallo; Felix Gall, second, took his first Grand Tour podium, with Jai Hindley third.

🏆Stage 5

Eulálio: nine days in pink and the maglia bianca

Portugal's Afonso Eulálio (Bahrain Victorious) took the maglia rosa after a chaotic breakaway stage to Potenza and held it from stage 5 to stage 13 – keeping it by 27 seconds after the Massa time trial. He only lost it to Vingegaard at Pila, and finished sixth overall and winner of the young rider classification.

⚠️Stage 2

Mass crash in Bulgaria ends Yates' and Buitrago's Giro

On stage 2 to Veliko Tarnovo, around ten riders – several of them GC contenders – went down 23 km from the finish on a slippery road at high speed. Santiago Buitrago and UAE's Jay Vine and Marc Soler abandoned, and podium contender Adam Yates did not start stage 3.

•Stage 1

Grande Partenza in Bulgaria, a stage in Switzerland

The 109th edition started on 8 May in Nessebar on the Black Sea coast – the first time Bulgaria hosted the start of the Giro – with the first three stages raced there before the race moved to Calabria after a rest day. Stage 16, from Bellinzona to Carì, took place entirely in Switzerland, and the race finished in Rome.

★Stage 2

Thomas Silva: first Uruguayan to win a stage and wear pink

Guillermo Thomas Silva (XDS Astana) won the reduced sprint at Veliko Tarnovo ahead of Florian Stork and Giulio Ciccone, after Vingegaard had countered an attack by Christian Scaroni on the final climb. He became the first Uruguayan to win a Giro stage and wear the maglia rosa, which he kept for two days.

⚡2026

Magnier takes three stages and the maglia ciclamino

France's Paul Magnier won at Burgas, Sofia and Pieve di Soligo and briefly wore the maglia rosa after stage 1. From stage 14 he duelled for the points lead with three-time stage winner Jhonatan Narváez, the jersey changing hands four times; when Narváez abandoned on stage 19, the maglia ciclamino was all but Magnier's.

•Stage 19

Ciccone claims the maglia azzurra in the Dolomites

Giulio Ciccone, who spent a day in pink after stage 4, took the mountains lead from Vingegaard on the queen stage to Alleghe, which crossed the Passo Giau as the roof of the race, and went on to win the maglia azzurra as well as the Cima Coppi. It was his second mountains title at the Giro and his third in a Grand Tour.

🔥Stage 6

Last-corner crash in Naples hands Ballerini the win

A few hundred metres from the finish in Naples, the Unibet Rose Rockets lead-out crashed on wet cobbles approaching the final corner, disrupting the sprint. Only Davide Ballerini and Jasper Stuyven, on the inside of the bend, were unaffected – Ballerini won, with Paul Magnier recovering to third.

Stage by stage

1 Fri 8 May · Nesebar → Burgas · Review Magnier sprints to the first maglia rosa in Burgas The almost flat opening stage from Nessebar to Burgas crossed Cape Agalina twice. Paul Magnier (Soudal–Quick-Step) won the sprint of an eleven-man group ahead of Tobias Lund Andresen and Ethan Vernon, taking the maglia rosa, maglia ciclamino and maglia bianca with a 4-second lead over Lund Andresen. Diego Pablo Sevilla took the first maglia azzurra. 2 Sat 9 May · Burgas → Veliko Tarnovo · Review Thomas Silva wins in Veliko Tarnovo and makes history Around ten riders crashed on a slippery road 23 km from the finish; Santiago Buitrago, Jay Vine and Marc Soler abandoned. Jonas Vingegaard countered an attack by Christian Scaroni on the final climb, but a small group sprinted for the win, taken by Guillermo Thomas Silva (XDS Astana) ahead of Florian Stork and Giulio Ciccone. Silva took the maglia rosa and maglia bianca, the first Uruguayan to win a Giro stage and wear pink. 3 Sun 10 May · Plovdiv → Sofia · Review Magnier again: sprint victory in Sofia The last Bulgarian stage crossed the Borovets Pass, its only climb, on the way to the capital. Paul Magnier won his second sprint ahead of Jonathan Milan and Dylan Groenewegen, strengthening his hold on the maglia ciclamino, while Guillermo Thomas Silva stayed in pink. Adam Yates and Andrea Vendrame did not start after the previous day's crash. 4 Tue 12 May · Catanzaro → Cosenza · Review Narváez wins in Cosenza, bonus seconds put Ciccone in pink Back in Italy, Movistar tried to drop the sprinters on Cozzo Tunno for Orluis Aular; Kaden Groves and Arnaud De Lie abandoned. In the reduced sprint, though, Jhonatan Narváez (UAE Team Emirates XRG) beat Aular and Giulio Ciccone. Ciccone took the maglia rosa thanks to the bonus seconds, 4 seconds ahead of Jan Christen, the new maglia bianca, and Florian Stork. 5 Wed 13 May · Praia a Mare → Potenza · Review Crashes and a wrong turn: Arrieta wins, Eulálio in pink Afonso Eulálio and Igor Arrieta went clear from the breakaway and both crashed on a descent; 2 km from the line Arrieta took a wrong turn, but he got back and won in Potenza 2 seconds ahead of Eulálio. The Portuguese rider took the maglia rosa and maglia bianca with a 2:51 lead over Arrieta. 6 Thu 14 May · Paestum → Naples · Review Ballerini profits from a last-corner crash in Naples The stage to Naples was heading for a bunch sprint until the leading Unibet Rose Rockets train crashed on wet cobbles approaching the final corner, a few hundred metres from the line. Davide Ballerini (XDS Astana) and Jasper Stuyven came through unhindered on the inside, and Ballerini won ahead of Stuyven and Paul Magnier. Afonso Eulálio stayed in pink. 7 Fri 15 May · Formia → Blockhaus · Review Vingegaard strikes on the Blockhaus At the end of the longest stage, 244 km, Jonas Vingegaard (Visma–Lease a Bike) attacked 5 km from the top of the Blockhaus and won solo; only Felix Gall limited his loss to under a minute, at 13 seconds. Afonso Eulálio kept the maglia rosa but lost about half his advantage, now leading Vingegaard – the new maglia azzurra – by 3:17. 8 Sat 16 May · Chieti → Fermo · Review Narváez solos to victory in Fermo On the hilly transition stage from Chieti to Fermo, Jhonatan Narváez (UAE Team Emirates XRG) took his second stage win, finishing solo 32 seconds ahead of Andreas Leknessund and 42 ahead of Martin Tjøtta. Nothing changed at the top of the overall standings, with Afonso Eulálio staying in pink ahead of Jonas Vingegaard. 9 Sun 17 May · Cervia → Corno alle Scale · Review Vingegaard counters Gall at Corno alle Scale Decathlon CMA CGM rode for Felix Gall, but Jonas Vingegaard countered 1 km from the finish and won 12 seconds ahead of the Austrian. Gall still gained time on the other contenders, while Giulio Pellizzari struggled on the final climb. Afonso Eulálio stayed in pink, his lead over Vingegaard down to 2:24. 10 Tue 19 May · Viareggio → Massa · Review Ganna dominates the time trial to Massa Filippo Ganna (Netcompany INEOS) won the 42 km individual time trial from Viareggio to Massa by 1:54 over teammate Thymen Arensman, who gained the most among the favourites and moved up to third overall. Jonas Vingegaard missed the top ten but closed to 27 seconds, so Afonso Eulálio kept the maglia rosa despite a poor time trial. 11 Wed 20 May · Porcari → Chiavari · Review Narváez makes it three in Chiavari Jhonatan Narváez (UAE Team Emirates XRG) made the day's breakaway and beat Enric Mas in a two-up sprint in Chiavari for his third stage win, with Diego Ulissi and Chris Harper 11 seconds back. Stage 6 winner Davide Ballerini abandoned, while Afonso Eulálio stayed 27 seconds ahead of Jonas Vingegaard overall. 12 Thu 21 May · Imperia → Novi Ligure · Review Segaert surprises the bunch in Novi Ligure Movistar again tried to shake off the fastest sprinters, and those who remained lacked teammates in the finale. Alec Segaert (Team Bahrain Victorious) took advantage, attacking 3 km from the line and holding on to win by 3 seconds from Toon Aerts and Guillermo Thomas Silva. Afonso Eulálio stayed in pink, 33 seconds ahead of Jonas Vingegaard. 13 Fri 22 May · Alessandria → Verbania · Review Bettiol solos to victory in Verbania The peloton let the breakaway go, and Alberto Bettiol (XDS Astana) dropped his companions on the final climb. He won in Verbania 26 seconds ahead of Andreas Leknessund and 44 ahead of Jasper Stuyven, with no change in the overall standings. 14 Sat 23 May · Aosta → Pila · Review Vingegaard wins at Pila and takes the maglia rosa On the short mountain stage around Aosta, Visma–Lease a Bike set up Jonas Vingegaard, who won solo at Pila for his third stage victory, 49 seconds ahead of Felix Gall and 58 ahead of Jai Hindley. Afonso Eulálio resisted for a long time but had to surrender the maglia rosa and now trailed by 2:26. Jhonatan Narváez took the maglia ciclamino, and Christian Scaroni abandoned. 15 Sun 24 May · Voghera → Milan · Review Breakaway survives: Dversnes surprises in Milan The flat stage to Milan looked made for the sprinters, but a four-man breakaway held on: Fredrik Dversnes (Uno-X Mobility) won ahead of Mirco Maestri, Martin Marcellusi and Mattia Bais, with the bunch 57 seconds behind. Paul Magnier, fifth, took back the maglia ciclamino, and Jonas Vingegaard stayed in pink. 16 Tue 26 May · Bellinzona → Carì · Review Swiss showcase: Vingegaard wins at Carì On the stage from Bellinzona to Carì, held entirely in Ticino, Switzerland, Jonas Vingegaard won solo again after strong work by his team, 1:09 ahead of Felix Gall and 1:11 ahead of Jai Hindley. Gall moved up to second at 4:03, Afonso Eulálio dropped to fifth, and Giulio Pellizzari lost more than ten minutes. 17 Wed 27 May · Cassano d'Adda → Andalo · Review Valgren outfoxes his breakaway companions in Andalo Michael Valgren (EF Education–EasyPost) caught out his breakaway companions in the final kilometre and won in Andalo 3 seconds ahead of Andreas Leknessund and 6 ahead of Damiano Caruso. Jhonatan Narváez was stuck in a chasing group but regained the maglia ciclamino thanks to the intermediate sprint; nothing changed at the top of the overall standings. 18 Thu 28 May · Fai della Paganella → Pieve di Soligo · Review Magnier wins in Pieve di Soligo and regains ciclamino The sprinters survived the day's only climb, a very steep one, and fought out the win among themselves. Paul Magnier took his third stage victory ahead of Edoardo Zambanini and Jonathan Milan, winning back the maglia ciclamino from Jhonatan Narváez, while Jonas Vingegaard stayed 4:03 ahead of Felix Gall. 19 Fri 29 May · Feltre → Alleghe · Review Kuss wins the queen stage, Hindley moves onto the podium On the Dolomites stage over six climbs, including the Passo Giau as the roof of the race, Sepp Kuss (Visma–Lease a Bike) won from the breakaway at Alleghe, 13 seconds ahead of Derek Gee-West and 36 ahead of Giulio Ciccone, who took the maglia azzurra. Thymen Arensman cracked and lost third place to Jai Hindley, and Jhonatan Narváez abandoned. 20 Sat 30 May · Gemona del Friuli → Piancavallo · Review Fifth stage win: Vingegaard triumphs at Piancavallo The start in Gemona del Friuli commemorated the 1976 earthquake, and the finale climbed twice to Piancavallo. Jonas Vingegaard attacked 10 km from the finish and won his fifth stage, 1:15 ahead of Felix Gall and Jai Hindley, who secured their podium places. He went into the final day with a 5:22 lead. 21 Sun 31 May · Rome ↻ · Review Milan sprints to victory in Rome as Vingegaard celebrates Jonathan Milan (Lidl–Trek) won the bunch sprint on the final stage in Rome ahead of Giovanni Lonardi and Paul Penhoët. Jonas Vingegaard won the Giro by 5:22 over Felix Gall and 6:25 over Jai Hindley, with Paul Magnier taking the maglia ciclamino, Giulio Ciccone the maglia azzurra and Afonso Eulálio the maglia bianca.
