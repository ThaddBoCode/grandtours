Legendary places on the route

Climb · Lombardia

Passo di Gavia

2,621 m 17.3 km · 0 %
Stage 16

The Gavia (2,621 m) links Bormio with Ponte di Legno and has been the Giro's Cima Coppi several times since its debut in 1960. The 1988 stage in a snowstorm, after which Andrew Hampsten took the pink jersey, is legendary.

 Climb · Lombardia

Passo del Mortirolo

1,852 m 12.4 km · 10.5 %
Stage 16

The Mortirolo (Passo della Foppa, 1,852 m) between Valtellina and Valcamonica is, from Mazzo, one of the hardest climbs in Europe. It debuted in the Giro in 1990, and a monument recalls Marco Pantani, who made his breakthrough here in 1994.

 Climb · Friuli-Venezia Giulia

Monte Zoncolan

1,750 m 10 km · 11.9 %
Stage 20

Monte Zoncolan (1,750 m) in the Carnic Alps is, from Ovaro, one of the steepest summit finishes in pro cycling, averaging almost 12 % with ramps of 22 %. Seven Giro stages have finished on top since 2003, and it returns in 2027.

 Finish · Trentino

Madonna di Campiglio

1,522 m
Stage 18

Madonna di Campiglio (1,522 m) is a ski resort in Trentino and the main gateway to the Brenta Dolomites. Marco Pantani won here in 1999 before being expelled the next morning for a high haematocrit level; the Giro returns in 2027.

 Finish · Lazio

Monte Livata (Subiaco)

1,429 m 19 km · 6 %
Stage 9

Monte Livata is a ski and holiday resort in the Monti Simbruini above Subiaco; the road from Subiaco climbs about 1,200 m over roughly 19 km at around 6 %. Stage 9 of the 2027 Giro finishes here; until now the climb was known from Tirreno–Adriatico in 1975.

 Finish · Toscana / Emilia-Romagna

Passo dei Due Santi (Zeri)

1,392 m
Stage 12

The Passo dei Due Santi (1,392 m), formerly Passo del Faggio Crociato, links Zeri in Lunigiana with Albareto in the Parma Apennines, with the small Zum Zeri ski area at the top. It is named after two statues of the Virgin Mary and St Anthony, and in 2027 a Giro stage finishes here for the first time.

 Finish · Lombardia

Aprica

1,181 m
Stage 16

Aprica sits on the pass of the same name (about 1,180 m) between Valtellina and Valcamonica, close to the Mortirolo, Santa Cristina and Gavia. It has been a Giro stage finish ten times since 1962 and hosts one again in 2027.

 Finish · Abruzzo

Campo di Giove

1,064 m
Stage 7

Campo di Giove (1,064 m) is a mountain village and ski resort on the south-western slope of the Maiella above Sulmona. In 2027 it hosts the finish of the stage 7 individual time trial (19.4 km from Sulmona).

 City · Lazio

Roma (Fori Imperiali)

Stage 21

Since 2023 Rome has been the Giro's permanent final venue, with the last stage on a circuit around the Colosseum, the Via dei Fori Imperiali and the Circus Maximus. Before that the race had ended in the capital only in 1911, 1950, 2009 and 2018.

 City · Friuli-Venezia Giulia

Trieste

Stage 1

Trieste has been a Giro stage town 40 times (18 starts, 22 finishes) and hosted the final finish three times, in 1966, 1973 and 2014. The 1981 Giro began here with a prologue from the Piazza Unità d'Italia, and the 2027 race starts in Trieste again.
