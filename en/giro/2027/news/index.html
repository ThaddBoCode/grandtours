Stories

The big stories of the Giro and the story of every stage – updated continuously.

🕐 As of 4 November 2026

The storylines before the start

🏆Stage 16

Mortirolo twice, with the Gavia in between

The queen stage from Sondrio to Aprica packs 5,700 m of climbing into 192 km. The Mortirolo (12.8 km at 10.1%) comes twice, with the Passo di Gavia in between – at 2,618 m the Cima Coppi, back in the race for the first time since 2014. The second Mortirolo summit is 33.3 km from the finish.

🏆2027

Pogačar, Vingegaard, Seixas: who will ride?

Pogačar has named the Tour, the Worlds and the Vuelta as his 2027 goals, though he says he would rather ride the Giro; defending champion Vingegaard's programme is still open. Paul Seixas is considering a Giro debut, and Bahrain have Antonio Tiberi on their Giro programme for now. Several decisions are due at the teams' December training camps.

🏆Stage 20

The Zoncolan returns on the penultimate day

Stage 20 finishes on Monte Zoncolan from the Ovaro side – 10.1 km at 11.9% with ramps of 22%. It is the mountain's eighth Giro finish and the first since Lorenzo Fortunato won in 2021; on this side Chris Froome won in 2018.

🏆Stage 7

56.9 km against the clock – including an uphill time trial

Stage 7 is a 19.6 km cronoscalata to Campo di Giove, the Giro's first mountain time trial since 2023; stage 15 is a flat 37.3 km from the Monza circuit to Milan. That is more time trialling than in 2025 and 2026 (about 42 km each), but less than in 2023 and 2024.

•Stage 1

Grande Partenza in Trieste – with two days in Slovenia

After starts in Albania and Bulgaria, the Giro begins in Italy again on 8 May. Stage 1 loops out of Trieste briefly into Slovenia, and stage 2 runs entirely on Slovenian roads from Ljubljana to Koper – only the fourth Giro stage ever to start in Slovenia.

⚡Stage 4

Sprints on race tracks: Misano and Mugello

Stage 4 finishes on the Misano World Circuit and stage 11 on the Mugello circuit, while the stage 15 time trial starts inside the Monza Autodromo. New Giro boss Luca Guercilena says the circuits are meant to make finishes safer; at the 2023 Tour, however, a sprint on the Nogaro track ended in several crashes.

•Stage 5

Filottrano remembers Michele Scarponi

Stage 5 starts in Filottrano, home town of the 2011 winner Michele Scarponi, ten years after his death. It brings the first summit finish: 13.1 km at 7.4% up to Sassotetto in the Sibillini Mountains.

★Stage 21

Rome for the fifth year in a row – a first

The Giro ends in the capital for the ninth time and, for the first time, in five consecutive years: eight laps of a city circuit of around 9 km. Naples hosts a finish for the sixth year running – the first such run since 1946–1955.

Stage by stage

1 Sat 8 May · Trieste ↻ · Preview The 110th Giro opens with a loop out of Trieste that dips into Slovenia before three short climbs in the last 40 km – Hrvatini, Prebeneg and Cattinara–Sella di Longera. The final summit comes 13.3 km from the line, so puncheurs and versatile sprinters will fight for the first maglia rosa. 2 Sun 9 May · Ljubljana → Koper · Preview An all-Slovenian stage from the capital Ljubljana via Postojna and the Karst down to the Adriatic at Koper. The short kick up Markovec, topping out 3.3 km from the line, could see off the purest sprinters and set up a reduced bunch finish. 3 Mon 10 May · Cervignano del Friuli → Possagno · Preview Flat for the first 100 km across Friuli and Veneto, then the Montello (Santa Maria della Vittoria) and Forcella Mostaccin liven things up inside the final 40 km. The road rises gently to the finish in Possagno, Antonio Canova's birthplace at the foot of Monte Grappa – a finale for puncheurs and tough sprinters. 4 Tue 11 May · Argenta → Misano Adriatico (Autodromo Marco Simoncelli) · Preview The first clear chance for the sprinters: 151 km across Emilia-Romagna with only the short GPM at Bertinoro to break up the flat. The finish is on the Misano World Circuit Marco Simoncelli, where a fast bunch sprint is all but certain. 5 Wed 12 May · Filottrano → Sarnano-Sassotetto · Preview The first summit finish, starting in Filottrano, hometown of Michele Scarponi, ten years after his death. Only 139 km but 3,300 m of climbing over Camerino, San Ginesio and Gualdo before the 13.1 km climb to Sassotetto (7.4%, max 14%) in the Sibillini Mountains – the first real gaps in the fight for the maglia rosa are likely. 6 Thu 13 May · Alba Adriatica → Lanciano · Preview After 50 flat kilometres along the Adriatic past Pescara, the route turns inland over the GPMs at Chieti and Ripa Teatina and up through the Abruzzo hills to Orsogna. From the coast at Marina di San Vito the last 13 km climb steadily to Lanciano – a finish for explosive riders. 7 Fri 14 May · Sulmona → Campo di Giove · Preview A cronoscalata of 19.6 km: the first 7.1 km out of Sulmona rise only gently, then the climb to Campo di Giove averages 4.4% over 12.5 km (max 9%) up to 1,068 m. Pacing is everything, and the GC contenders will know where they stand after this test. 8 Sat 15 May · Trasacco → Naples · Preview One of the longest stages: 219 km from the Marsica in Abruzzo down the Liri valley past Sora and Cassino to the Tyrrhenian coast. The Campi Flegrei add a sting in the last 30 km with the GPM at Monte di Procida and the rises around Pozzuoli, before Naples hosts a finish for the sixth year in a row – the sprinters must hang on. 9 Sun 16 May · Itri → Subiaco-Monte Livata · Preview The first week ends with a summit finish. After a flat start across the Pontine plain, the GPMs at Bellegra and Colle Ferro soften the legs before the 13.9 km climb from Subiaco to Monte Livata (6.9%, max 17%), whose steepest section – 10.4% on average – comes in the first 4 km. A key test for the maglia rosa contenders before the first rest day. 10 Tue 18 May · Palombara Sabina → Norcia · Preview A long, restless 218 km through the central Apennines with 3,600 m of climbing, from the Sabina past Rieti. The decisive part comes late: Forca di Presta (1,536 m) and, after the Castelluccio plateau, Forca Ventolosa (1,521 m), followed by almost 20 km of descent to Norcia – a day for strong breakaway riders. 11 Wed 19 May · Gualdo Tadino → Scarperia (Autodromo del Mugello) · Preview The longest stage of the race at 220 km crosses Umbria and Tuscany via Gubbio, Arezzo and the Valdarno. The GPM at Vetta Le Croci, crested 29 km from the finish, could trouble the sprinters, but the flat finale on the Mugello motor racing circuit should still favour a bunch sprint. 12 Thu 20 May · Montecatini Terme → Zeri (Loc. Passo dei Due Santi) · Preview This year's Tappa Bartali brings a new summit finish at Passo dei Due Santi in the Zum Zeri ski area. After the Garfagnana and Foce Carpinelli, the race drops into the Lunigiana before climbing again from Pontremoli; the final ascent is 8.7 km at 8.7% (max 13%) and the last 2.4 km average 11.6% – terrain for pure climbers. 13 Fri 21 May · Borgo Val di Taro → Reggio Emilia · Preview From the Parma Apennines the road drops steadily into the Po Valley; the only GPM, at Borsea, comes almost 100 km from the finish. After that it is pan-flat via Guastalla and Correggio to Reggio Emilia, birthplace of the Italian flag – a stage made for the sprinters. 14 Sat 22 May · Cantù Arena → Piani di Bobbio/Valtorta · Preview 4,900 m of climbing on the roads of Il Lombardia: Madonna del Ghisallo, Culmine di San Pietro, Miragolo San Salvatore and Alino come in quick succession before the summit finish at Piani di Bobbio. The final climb via Valtorta is 10.1 km at 9.0% with ramps of 18% – the hardest test of the race so far, with the time trial waiting the next day. 15 Sun 23 May · Monza (Autodromo Nazionale) → Milan · Preview A flat, fast 37.3 km time trial starting inside the Autodromo Nazionale Monza and finishing in Milan, via Concorezzo, Cernusco sul Naviglio and Piazzale Loreto. A day for the specialists, while the climbers must limit their losses the day after Piani di Bobbio; Filippo Ganna won the last two time trials into Milan in 2020 and 2021. 16 Tue 25 May · Sondrio → Aprica · Preview The queen stage: 192 km and 5,700 m of climbing with the Mortirolo from Mazzo di Valtellina (12.8 km at 10.1%, the Montagna Pantani) twice and, in between, the Passo di Gavia – the Cima Coppi at 2,618 m, back for the first time since 2014 (16.5 km at 8.0%). The second Mortirolo summit is 33.3 km from the line, followed by the descent to Edolo and the uphill run to Aprica – the Giro can be turned upside down here. 17 Wed 26 May · Chiari → Vo' · Preview A largely flat stage from Lombardy into the Veneto via Solferino and the plains around Verona. The sting is in the tail: in the Euganean Hills the short, steep muri of Passo Roverello and Castelnuovo, the latter cresting 9 km from the finish in Vo', could undo the sprinters' plans. 18 Thu 27 May · Rovereto → Madonna di Campiglio · Preview An all-Trentino mountain stage with 4,450 m of climbing: past Lake Garda to Passo Ballino, then a circuit of around 50 km between Ponte Arche and Spiazzo with two ascents of Passo Durone and the tough Passo Daone. The final climb from Pinzolo to Madonna di Campiglio is more regular – 12.5 km at 5.7% – but after this much climbing, attacks from the GC men are likely. 19 Fri 28 May · Rovereto → Sacile · Preview Vigolo Vattaro and Bieno come early on this 199 km stage out of Trentino, followed by a long descending and flat stretch through the Valsugana and past Feltre. In the finale the short, steep climbs of Montaner and Castello di Caneva precede the run-in to Sacile – with 3,000 m of climbing, a good chance for attackers on the eve of the Zoncolan. 20 Sat 29 May · Pordenone → Monte Zoncolan · Preview The last big mountain day: after a long approach through Friuli via Spilimbergo and Tolmezzo, Passo Duron (1,069 m) and Sella Valcalda (958 m) prepare the final climb of Monte Zoncolan from Ovaro – 10.1 km at 11.9% with ramps of 22%. It is the eighth Zoncolan finish in Giro history and the first on the Ovaro side since Chris Froome won in 2018; the maglia rosa should be decided here. 21 Sun 30 May · Rome ↻ · Preview The traditional finale in the capital for the fifth year in a row: 131 km, completely flat, heading out towards Ostia before eight laps of a city circuit of around 9 km past Rome's monuments. A last bunch sprint for the stage, while the maglia rosa lifts the Trofeo Senza Fine.
