Stories

The big stories of the Giro and the story of every stage – updated continuously.

Headlines

🏆Stage 21

Geoghegan Hart wins the Giro without ever wearing pink

Tao Geoghegan Hart won the Giro by 39 seconds over Jai Hindley after the pair started the final time trial into Milan level on time, with Hindley in pink on countback. The British rider became the first in Giro history to win the race without having worn the maglia rosa before the final stage, and he also took the maglia bianca. Wilco Kelderman finished third at 1:29.

•2020

A Giro in October: postponed by the pandemic

Originally scheduled for 9 to 31 May with a Grande Partenza in Budapest, the 103rd Giro was postponed because of the COVID-19 pandemic after Hungary would not allow the start to go ahead. It was raced from 3 to 25 October instead, from Monreale in Sicily to Milan, overlapping with the Vuelta a España.

⚠️Stage 3

Pre-race favourite Thomas crashes out

Geraint Thomas, the 2018 Tour de France winner and pre-race favourite, crashed on a stray bidon in the neutralised section before stage 3 and reached the top of Etna about 12 minutes down. He did not start stage 4, and Tao Geoghegan Hart took over as INEOS Grenadiers' leader.

🔥Stage 10

COVID-19 positives: Mitchelton–Scott and Jumbo–Visma pull out

Simon Yates tested positive for COVID-19 and did not start stage 8. Before stage 10, Mitchelton–Scott and Team Jumbo–Visma withdrew all their remaining riders following positive tests – among them Steven Kruijswijk, who had tested positive himself – and Michael Matthews also left the race.

🏆Stage 3

Almeida's 15 days in the maglia rosa

João Almeida took the maglia rosa on Etna on stage 3 and defended it for 15 days, still leading Wilco Kelderman by 17 seconds after stage 17. He cracked on the Stelvio on stage 18 and finished the Giro fourth at 2:57.

🏆Stage 18

Stelvio shake-up: Kelderman in pink, Hindley wins

On the queen stage over the Stelvio, the Cima Coppi, Rohan Dennis set the pace for Tao Geoghegan Hart and distanced João Almeida, Vincenzo Nibali and the other favourites. Jai Hindley won at Laghi di Cancano ahead of Geoghegan Hart, while his Sunweb teammate Wilco Kelderman took the maglia rosa, 12 seconds ahead of Hindley and 15 ahead of Geoghegan Hart.

🔥Stage 20

Dead heat after Sestriere: Hindley and Geoghegan Hart level

Stage 20 was shortened because French COVID-19 rules kept the race out of France, cutting the Colle dell'Agnello and the Izoard. On the climbs to Sestriere INEOS Grenadiers, driven by Rohan Dennis, dropped race leader Wilco Kelderman; Tao Geoghegan Hart beat Jai Hindley in the sprint and, thanks to the bonus seconds, drew level with him on time before the final day.

•2020

World champion Ganna wins four stages

Fresh from winning the time trial world title in Imola, Filippo Ganna won all three time trials – in Palermo, Valdobbiadene and Milan – and added a solo road win at Camigliatello Silano that also put him in the maglia azzurra. With further wins from Jhonatan Narváez and Tao Geoghegan Hart, INEOS Grenadiers took seven stages.

⚡2020

Démare's four sprint wins and the maglia ciclamino

Arnaud Démare won four bunch sprints – at Villafranca Tirrena, Matera, Brindisi and Rimini – and took the maglia ciclamino with 233 points to Peter Sagan's 184. Sagan, second to Démare in three of those sprints, finally won his first Giro stage in Tortoreto, while the maglia azzurra went to Ruben Guerreiro.

🌧️Stage 19

Rider protest cuts stage 19 to 124.5 km

On the morning of stage 19 riders protested against the long distance in bad weather. After about 10 km the peloton stopped and was taken by team bus to Abbiategrasso, cutting the stage by around 130 km to 124.5 km; Josef Černý won solo in Asti.

Stage by stage

1 Sat 3 Oct · Monreale → Palermo · Review Ganna storms to Palermo and the first maglia rosa Newly crowned time trial world champion Filippo Ganna won the 15 km opening time trial from Monreale to Palermo, 22 seconds ahead of João Almeida and Mikkel Bjerg. Geraint Thomas was fourth at 23 seconds, while Miguel Ángel López abandoned on the opening day. 2 Sun 4 Oct · Alcamo → Agrigento · Review Ulissi wins the uphill finish in Agrigento Diego Ulissi won a three-man sprint on the uphill finish in Agrigento, beating Peter Sagan and Mikkel Frølich Honoré, and moved into the maglia ciclamino. A five-man break including Thomas De Gendt was caught 9 km from the line; Filippo Ganna kept the maglia rosa and Sagan took the maglia azzurra. 3 Mon 5 Oct · Enna → Etna · Review Caicedo wins on Etna as Thomas loses 12 minutes Jonathan Caicedo won from the breakaway on Etna, 21 seconds ahead of Giovanni Visconti, and took the maglia azzurra. João Almeida moved into the maglia rosa, level on time with Caicedo, while Simon Yates lost more than three minutes and Geraint Thomas, who had crashed before the start, finished about 12 minutes down. 4 Tue 6 Oct · Catania → Villafranca Tirrena · Review Démare edges Sagan in a photo finish Arnaud Démare beat Peter Sagan and Davide Ballerini in a photo finish at Villafranca Tirrena after a three-man break was caught 15 km from the end. Sagan took the maglia ciclamino, João Almeida gained two bonus seconds at an intermediate sprint to stay in pink, and Geraint Thomas did not start. 5 Wed 7 Oct · Mileto → Camigliatello Silano · Review Ganna the climber: solo win at Camigliatello Silano Filippo Ganna dropped his breakaway companions on the climb of the Valico di Montescuro and won alone at Camigliatello Silano, also taking a surprise maglia azzurra. João Almeida collected four bonus seconds for third place, Simon Yates lost more ground and Pello Bilbao moved up to second overall. 6 Thu 8 Oct · Castrovillari → Matera · Review Démare again, this time in Matera After the four-man break was caught 15 km from the finish, attacks by Vincenzo Nibali and Peter Sagan on the gentle rise to the line in Matera were shut down and Arnaud Démare sprinted to his second win, ahead of Michael Matthews and Fabio Felline. Démare took the maglia ciclamino, and João Almeida stayed in pink. 7 Fri 9 Oct · Matera → Brindisi · Review Démare's hat-trick on the fastest stage in Giro history Arnaud Démare won again in Brindisi, with Peter Sagan second for the third time and Michael Matthews third. Pushed along by a strong wind, the short stage was ridden at 51.234 km/h, the highest average speed for a stage in Giro history, and all four jerseys stayed put. 8 Sat 10 Oct · Giovinazzo → Vieste · Review Yates out with COVID-19, Dowsett wins solo in Vieste The day began with the news that Simon Yates had tested positive for COVID-19 and left the race. Alex Dowsett went clear from a large break and won alone in Vieste, 1:15 ahead of Salvatore Puccio, while the peloton finished about 14 minutes down and João Almeida stayed in pink. 9 Sun 11 Oct · San Salvo → Roccaraso (Aremogna) · Review Guerreiro wins at Roccaraso and takes the maglia azzurra Ruben Guerreiro won from the break on the Aremogna climb above Roccaraso, eight seconds ahead of Jonathan Castroviejo, and moved into the maglia azzurra. On the steep finish Jakob Fuglsang, Wilco Kelderman and Domenico Pozzovivo gained time, Kelderman moving up to second overall, 30 seconds behind João Almeida. 10 Tue 13 Oct · Lanciano → Tortoreto · Review Sagan finally wins a Giro stage Before the start in Lanciano, Mitchelton–Scott and Team Jumbo–Visma pulled out after positive COVID-19 tests, and Michael Matthews was also gone. Peter Sagan, part of the break, attacked on the steepest part of the climb at Tortoreto and won solo for his first Giro stage, ahead of Brandon McNulty and João Almeida, who gained four bonus seconds. 11 Wed 14 Oct · Porto Sant'Elpidio → Rimini · Review Démare makes it four in Rimini Arnaud Démare took his fourth stage win in Rimini, once again ahead of Peter Sagan, after the break was caught 6 km from the finish. Elia Viviani was involved in a crash with a motorbike, and all the jerseys stayed unchanged. 12 Thu 15 Oct · Cesenatico ↻ · Review Narváez wins the Nove Colli stage in Cesenatico On Marco Pantani's home roads, over a route modelled on the Nove Colli gran fondo, a 14-rider break stayed clear. Jhonatan Narváez won alone in Cesenatico, 1:08 ahead of Mark Padun, who had been slowed by a mechanical problem; the maglia rosa group finished about eight minutes down with no change among the leaders. 13 Fri 16 Oct · Cervia → Monselice · Review Ulissi strikes again in Monselice Arnaud Démare and Peter Sagan were dropped on the day's two climbs, leaving about twenty riders to contest the finish in Monselice. Diego Ulissi won the sprint for his second stage victory, ahead of João Almeida, who gained six bonus seconds, and Patrick Konrad. 14 Sat 17 Oct · Conegliano → Valdobbiadene · Review Ganna wins the time trial to Valdobbiadene Filippo Ganna won the 34.1 km time trial from Conegliano to Valdobbiadene, 26 seconds ahead of INEOS teammate Rohan Dennis and more than a minute ahead of everyone else. João Almeida extended his lead over Wilco Kelderman to 56 seconds, and Brandon McNulty, third on the stage, moved up to fourth overall. 15 Sun 18 Oct · Base Aerea Rivolto → Piancavallo · Review Geoghegan Hart wins at Piancavallo, Almeida holds on Jai Hindley set the pace on the final climb for his Sunweb leader Wilco Kelderman, and only Tao Geoghegan Hart could follow, going on to win ahead of Kelderman and Hindley. João Almeida lost 37 seconds but kept the maglia rosa, 15 seconds ahead of Kelderman, and Giovanni Visconti took the maglia azzurra. 16 Tue 20 Oct · Udine → San Daniele del Friuli · Review Tratnik wins in San Daniele del Friuli A 28-rider break went clear from the start, including mountains rivals Giovanni Visconti and Ruben Guerreiro. Jan Tratnik dropped Ben O'Connor on the climb to the finish and won by seven seconds, while João Almeida attacked in the last few hundred metres to gain two seconds on his rivals. 17 Wed 21 Oct · Bassano del Grappa → Madonna di Campiglio · Review O'Connor wins solo at Madonna di Campiglio Once again the break decided the stage: Ben O'Connor, second the day before, won alone at Madonna di Campiglio ahead of Hermann Pernsteiner and Thomas De Gendt. The favourites finished together, so the overall standings did not change, and Ruben Guerreiro, in the break, regained the maglia azzurra. 18 Thu 22 Oct · Pinzolo → Laghi di Cancano · Review Hindley wins at Laghi di Cancano, Kelderman takes pink Rohan Dennis led over the Stelvio, the Cima Coppi, with Tao Geoghegan Hart and Jai Hindley, after João Almeida, Vincenzo Nibali, Pello Bilbao and Jakob Fuglsang had been dropped. Hindley won the two-up sprint at Laghi di Cancano ahead of Geoghegan Hart, and Wilco Kelderman took the maglia rosa, 12 seconds ahead of his teammate Hindley and 15 ahead of Geoghegan Hart; Almeida fell to fifth at 2:16. 19 Fri 23 Oct · Abbiategrasso → Asti · Review Černý wins in Asti after a protest shortens the stage After riders protested against the distance in bad weather, the stage was halted after about 10 km and restarted in Abbiategrasso, cutting it to 124.5 km. Josef Černý attacked from a 14-rider break 20 km from the finish and won alone in Asti, 18 seconds ahead of Victor Campenaerts; the peloton finished about 16 minutes down. 20 Sat 24 Oct · Alba → Sestriere · Review Geoghegan Hart wins at Sestriere and draws level with Hindley The stage was shortened because French COVID-19 rules meant the Colle dell'Agnello and the Izoard had to be cut. On the second ascent to Sestriere, Rohan Dennis's pace dropped Wilco Kelderman, and Tao Geoghegan Hart beat Jai Hindley in the final sprint to wipe out his four-second deficit with the bonus seconds. Hindley took the maglia rosa level on time with Geoghegan Hart, ahead on the hundredths of a second from the time trials. 21 Sun 25 Oct · Cernusco sul Naviglio → Milan · Review Ganna wins in Milan, Geoghegan Hart wins the Giro With Jai Hindley and Tao Geoghegan Hart level on time, the 15.7 km time trial into Milan decided the Giro. Filippo Ganna won his fourth stage ahead of Victor Campenaerts and Rohan Dennis, while Geoghegan Hart beat Hindley by 39 seconds to take the overall win and João Almeida moved past Pello Bilbao into fourth.
