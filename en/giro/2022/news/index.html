Stories

The big stories of the Giro and the story of every stage – updated continuously.

Headlines

🏆2022

Hindley becomes the first Australian to win the Giro

Jai Hindley won the 105th Giro d'Italia by 1:18 over Richard Carapaz, with Mikel Landa third at 3:24 – his first Grand Tour victory and the first Giro win by an Australian. He started as one of Bora–Hansgrohe's three potential leaders and took over the team leadership by winning stage 9 on the Blockhaus.

🏆Stage 20

Hindley breaks Carapaz on the Marmolada

Carapaz and Hindley were separated by just three seconds after stage 16 and stayed inseparable until the penultimate stage. On the steep final kilometres of the Passo Fedaia, Hindley attacked, linked up with teammate Lennard Kämna and dropped Carapaz inside the last 3 km, taking the maglia rosa with a lead of 1:25 that he defended in the Verona time trial.

🏆Stage 14

Carapaz seizes the maglia rosa on a wild day around Turin

Bora–Hansgrohe blew the race apart with a ferocious pace on stage 14, catching the breakaway before the first ascent of Superga. Carapaz attacked on the second climb of Superga and, although Simon Yates won the stage, the Ecuadorian took the maglia rosa seven seconds ahead of Hindley; Juan Pedro López lost four and a half minutes after ten days in pink.

•Stage 1

Grande Partenza in Hungary: van der Poel opens in pink

The Giro started in Budapest with three stages in Hungary. Mathieu van der Poel won the uphill sprint at Visegrád to take the first maglia rosa, kept it after finishing three seconds behind Simon Yates in the Budapest time trial and only lost it on Etna; Mark Cavendish won the bunch sprint in Balatonfüred, his 16th Giro stage win.

•Stage 4

Juan Pedro López wears pink for ten days

Juan Pedro López attacked from the breakaway on Etna and lost the two-man sprint to Lennard Kämna, but took the maglia rosa. The Trek–Segafredo rider held it for ten days, saved it on the Blockhaus by just 12 seconds, lost it in Turin and finished tenth overall – winning the maglia bianca as best young rider.

★Stage 10

Girmay makes history in Jesi – then a cork hits his eye

Biniam Girmay launched a long sprint in Jesi and held off Mathieu van der Poel to become the first Black African rider to win a Grand Tour stage. On the podium the cork of his Prosecco bottle hit his left eye; he was taken to hospital and did not start the next stage to let the injury heal.

⚡2022

Démare rules the sprints and wins the maglia ciclamino

Arnaud Démare was the race's best sprinter, winning in Messina, Scalea and Cuneo, and took the points classification with 254 points ahead of Fernando Gaviria and Mark Cavendish. He led the classification from stage 5 all the way to Verona.

•2022

Bouwman wins the maglia azzurra and two stages

Koen Bouwman of Team Jumbo–Visma won from breakaways in Potenza and at the Santuario di Castelmonte and took the mountains classification with 294 points ahead of Giulio Ciccone. By taking maximum points on all four climbs of stage 19 he made the maglia azzurra mathematically his.

🔥2022

Bardet, Yates and Almeida: contenders leave the race

Several favourites left early: Miguel Ángel López abandoned on stage 4 with a hip injury, Romain Bardet, then fourth overall, quit on stage 13 with stomach problems, and Simon Yates, winner of two stages, abandoned on stage 17. João Almeida, also fourth overall at the time, withdrew before stage 18 after testing positive for COVID-19.

Stage by stage

1 Fri 6 May · Budapest → Visegrád · Review Van der Poel wins at Visegrád and takes the first maglia rosa Breakaway riders Mattia Bais and Filippo Tagliani were caught 13.7 km from the finish, and Lennard Kämna's late attack on the climb to Visegrád was reeled in inside the last kilometre. Mathieu van der Poel won the uphill sprint ahead of Biniam Girmay and Pello Bilbao and took the maglia rosa, the maglia ciclamino and the maglia azzurra; Girmay took the maglia bianca. 2 Sat 7 May · Budapest ↻ · Review Simon Yates wins in Budapest, van der Poel stays in pink Simon Yates set the best time of 11:50 on the 9.2 km time trial in Budapest, three seconds faster than Mathieu van der Poel, the last rider to start, and five faster than Tom Dumoulin. Van der Poel kept the maglia rosa, while Italian time trial champion Matteo Sobrero took the maglia bianca. 3 Sun 8 May · Kaposvár → Balatonfüred · Review Cavendish takes his 16th Giro stage win in Balatonfüred Bais and Tagliani were in the breakaway again, this time with Samuele Rivi, while Jan Tratnik became the first rider to abandon after his crash on stage 1. Led out by Michael Mørkøv, Mark Cavendish opened his sprint 300 m out and held off Arnaud Démare and Fernando Gaviria; van der Poel stayed in pink ahead of the first rest day and Rick Zabel took the maglia azzurra. 4 Tue 10 May · Avola → Etna (Rif. Sapienza) · Review Kämna wins on Etna, Juan Pedro López takes pink Lennard Kämna caught Juan Pedro López 2.5 km from the top of Etna and beat him in the two-man sprint after López misjudged the final corner. López took the maglia rosa and the maglia bianca, Kämna the maglia azzurra; Miguel Ángel López abandoned with a hip injury and Tom Dumoulin lost seven minutes. 5 Wed 11 May · Catania → Messina · Review Démare wins in Messina and takes the maglia ciclamino Alpecin–Fenix raised the pace on the Portella Mandrazzi, dropping Caleb Ewan and Mark Cavendish for good, while Arnaud Démare fought his way back after the descent. Démare then beat Fernando Gaviria and Giacomo Nizzolo in the sprint – his first Grand Tour stage win in two years – and took the maglia ciclamino. 6 Thu 12 May · Palmi → Scalea (Riviera dei Cedri) · Review Démare pips Ewan with a bike throw in Scalea Diego Rosa rode alone in front for around 140 km before being caught 27.6 km from the finish. Arnaud Démare came out of Caleb Ewan's slipstream and beat him with a bike throw for his second consecutive stage win, with Mark Cavendish third; Juan Pedro López kept the maglia rosa. 7 Fri 13 May · Diamante → Potenza · Review Bouwman wins in Potenza and takes the maglia azzurra A seven-man breakaway including Tom Dumoulin, Bauke Mollema and Davide Formolo fought out the hilly stage, with Koen Bouwman taking maximum points on Monte Sirino, at Viggiano and on La Sellata. Led out by Dumoulin, Bouwman won the sprint ahead of Mollema and Formolo for his first Grand Tour stage win and took the maglia azzurra; the favourites finished together almost three minutes down. 8 Sat 14 May · Naples ↻ · Review De Gendt outsprints his breakaway companions in Naples From a 21-man breakaway, Davide Gabburo attacked 42.8 km from the finish and was joined by Thomas De Gendt, his Lotto–Soudal teammate Harm Vanhoucke and Jorge Arcas. Led out by Vanhoucke, De Gendt beat Gabburo and Arcas in the sprint; Guillaume Martin, part of the original break, moved up to fourth overall. 9 Sun 15 May · Isernia → Blockhaus · Review Hindley wins the six-man sprint on the Blockhaus Richard Carapaz attacked 4.6 km from the top, followed only by Romain Bardet and Mikel Landa, before João Almeida, Jai Hindley and Domenico Pozzovivo bridged across. Hindley beat Bardet and Carapaz in the six-man sprint, while Juan Pedro López, distanced after unclipping from his pedal, kept the maglia rosa by 12 seconds over Almeida; Diego Rosa took the maglia azzurra. 10 Tue 17 May · Pescara → Jesi · Review Girmay becomes the first Black African Grand Tour stage winner INEOS Grenadiers' furious pace on the climb to Monsano dropped pure sprinters such as Arnaud Démare, and a string of attacks in the finale, several by Mathieu van der Poel, came to nothing. Biniam Girmay launched a long sprint and held off van der Poel; on the podium the Prosecco cork hit his left eye, and he did not start stage 11. 11 Wed 18 May · Santarcangelo di Romagna → Reggio Emilia · Review Dainese wins in Reggio Emilia, the first Italian stage win On a pan-flat stage, Dries De Bondt's solo attack was caught 1.3 km from the line. Alberto Dainese came round Fernando Gaviria and Simone Consonni to win his first Grand Tour stage, the first by an Italian at this Giro, while Richard Carapaz took three bonus seconds and moved up to second overall. 12 Thu 19 May · Parma → Genoa · Review Oldani takes his first professional win in Genoa On the race's longest stage, 204 km to Genoa, which returned to the Passo del Bocco for the first time since Wouter Weylandt's fatal crash on its descent in 2011, a 25-man breakaway was allowed to fight for the win. Stefano Oldani, Lorenzo Rota and Gijs Leemreize went clear and Oldani beat Rota in the sprint for his first professional victory; Juan Pedro López stayed in pink, while Caleb Ewan had left the race before the start. 13 Fri 20 May · Sanremo → Cuneo · Review Démare takes his third win in Cuneo as Bardet abandons Romain Bardet, fourth overall, abandoned with stomach problems before the Colle di Nava. The five-man break was only caught inside the final kilometre, and Arnaud Démare held off Phil Bauhaus and Mark Cavendish for his third stage win; Juan Pedro López extended his run in pink to ten days. 14 Sat 21 May · Santena → Turin · Review Yates wins in Turin, Carapaz takes the maglia rosa Bora–Hansgrohe's ferocious pace split the peloton, Carapaz attacked on the second climb of Superga, and Hindley caught him on the Colle della Maddalena. Simon Yates attacked on the Parco del Nobile and won alone, 15 seconds ahead of Hindley and Carapaz, who took the maglia rosa seven seconds ahead of Hindley; João Almeida took the maglia bianca as Juan Pedro López lost four and a half minutes. 15 Sun 22 May · Rivarolo Canavese → Cogne · Review Ciccone solos to victory in Cogne From a 28-man breakaway, Giulio Ciccone, Santiago Buitrago and Antonio Pedrero went clear on the climb to Verrogne, and Ciccone attacked twice on the steepest section of the final climb to Cogne. He won alone, 1:31 ahead of Buitrago; Koen Bouwman regained the maglia azzurra and Richard Carapaz, who had crashed early in the stage, kept pink. 16 Tue 24 May · Salò → Aprica · Review Hirt wins the queen stage at Aprica as Carapaz's lead shrinks On a stage with 5250 m of climbing over the Mortirolo and Santa Cristina, Jan Hirt and Thymen Arensman caught Lennard Kämna, and Hirt went clear 7.9 km from the finish to hold off Arensman by seven seconds for his first Grand Tour stage win. Mikel Landa attacked the favourites, only Carapaz and Hindley could follow, and Hindley's four bonus seconds for third cut Carapaz's lead to three seconds. 17 Wed 25 May · Ponte di Legno → Lavarone · Review Buitrago wins at Lavarone despite an earlier crash Santiago Buitrago caught Gijs Leemreize on the Monterovere and went clear to win by 35 seconds, despite having crashed earlier in the stage. Behind, Mikel Landa attacked and only Richard Carapaz and Jai Hindley could follow, sprinting to gain six seconds on him; Landa moved up to third, while Simon Yates abandoned. 18 Thu 26 May · Borgo Valsugana → Treviso · Review De Bondt wins from the breakaway in Treviso João Almeida, fourth overall, withdrew before the start after testing positive for COVID-19, and Juan Pedro López took back the maglia bianca. The four-man break of Magnus Cort, Edoardo Affini, Davide Gabburo and Dries De Bondt held off the sprinters, and De Bondt beat Affini by half a wheel for his first Grand Tour stage win; Carapaz stayed in pink. 19 Fri 27 May · Marano Lagunare → Santuario di Castelmonte · Review Bouwman wins again at Castelmonte and secures the maglia azzurra The stage crossed into Slovenia for the Kolovrat, where Koen Bouwman, Mauro Schmid, Attila Valter and Alessandro Tonelli emerged from the breakaway, later joined by Andrea Vendrame. Bouwman took the sharp final left-hander first and held off Schmid for his second stage win, with maximum points on all four climbs making the maglia azzurra mathematically his; Carapaz, Hindley and Landa remained inseparable. 20 Sat 28 May · Belluno → Marmolada (Passo Fedaia) · Review Covi wins on the Fedaia, Hindley takes the maglia rosa Alessandro Covi attacked from the breakaway on the Passo Pordoi, took the Cima Coppi and held off Domen Novak by 32 seconds on the Passo Fedaia for his first Grand Tour stage win. Behind, Jai Hindley attacked inside the final 5 km and, with Lennard Kämna setting the pace, dropped Carapaz within the last 3 km; Hindley took the maglia rosa with a lead of 1:25. 21 Sun 29 May · Verona ↻ · Review Sobrero wins in Verona, Hindley seals the Giro Italian time trial champion Matteo Sobrero won the 17.4 km time trial in 22:24, 23 seconds ahead of Thymen Arensman and 40 ahead of Mathieu van der Poel, for his first Grand Tour stage win. Jai Hindley finished 15th to win the Giro by 1:18 over Carapaz, with Landa third; Hugh Carthy moved up to ninth, but Juan Pedro López kept the maglia bianca.
