Stories

The big stories of the Giro and the story of every stage – updated continuously.

Headlines

🏆2023

Roglič becomes the first Slovenian to win the Giro

Primož Roglič won the 106th Giro d'Italia by 14 seconds over Geraint Thomas, with João Almeida third at 1:15 – his fourth Grand Tour victory and the first Giro win by a Slovenian. He only took the maglia rosa on the penultimate day.

🏆Stage 20

Roglič overturns Thomas in the Monte Lussari time trial

Geraint Thomas led by 26 seconds before the 18.6 km mountain time trial from Tarvisio to Monte Lussari. Roglič won it by 40 seconds over Thomas and 42 over Almeida, turning his deficit into a 14-second lead that he held to Rome.

🔥Stage 9

Evenepoel leaves the race in pink after a positive COVID-19 test

Remco Evenepoel, one of the pre-race favourites, won the opening time trial in Ortona and the stage 9 time trial in Cesena, where he beat Thomas by one second to take the maglia rosa back from Andreas Leknessund. Shortly afterwards he tested positive for COVID-19 and withdrew; after the rest day Thomas inherited pink, and by stage 11 four more Soudal–Quick-Step riders had tested positive.

⚠️Stage 11

Geoghegan Hart breaks his hip in a crash on a wet descent

A crash on a wet descent late in stage 11 brought down Thomas, Roglič, Pavel Sivakov and Tao Geoghegan Hart. Thomas and Roglič rejoined the peloton, but Geoghegan Hart, third overall, abandoned with a broken hip and Sivakov lost 13 minutes; the day before, Aleksandr Vlasov, also in the top ten, had abandoned midway through stage 10.

🌧️Stage 13

Bad weather cuts stage 13 to 74.6 km

Stage 13, originally 207 km long and then rerouted to 199 km, was shortened to 74.6 km before the start because of bad weather, with the start moved from Borgofranco d'Ivrea to Le Châble in Switzerland. Einer Rubio won at Crans-Montana ahead of Thibaut Pinot and Jefferson Alexander Cepeda.

•Stage 14

Armirail takes the maglia rosa on the road from Switzerland

On stage 14, which started in Sierre in Switzerland, Nico Denz won from the breakaway for his second stage win and Bruno Armirail of Groupama–FDJ took the maglia rosa, 1:41 ahead of Geraint Thomas. He kept it on the stage to Bergamo before Thomas took it back on Monte Bondone, where João Almeida won.

•2023

Pinot wins the maglia azzurra and finishes fifth

Thibaut Pinot won the mountains classification with 237 points ahead of Derek Gee and finished fifth overall. He was runner-up on two stages: at Crans-Montana behind Einer Rubio and at Zoldo Alto behind Filippo Zana.

⚡Stage 2

Milan wins in San Salvo and keeps the maglia ciclamino to Rome

Jonathan Milan won stage 2 in San Salvo and led the points classification from then on, finishing with 217 points ahead of Derek Gee. He was also second in Salerno, Naples, Tortona and Caorle.

•2023

Derek Gee: four stage second places, runner-up twice more

Derek Gee of Israel–Premier Tech finished second on four stages – at Fossombrone, Viareggio, Cassano Magnago and Tre Cime di Lavaredo – and was runner-up in both the points and the mountains classification.

⚡Stage 21

Cavendish wins the final stage in Rome

Mark Cavendish of Astana Qazaqstan Team won the closing sprint in Rome ahead of Alex Kirsch and Filippo Fiorelli as Roglič sealed his overall victory. It was Cavendish's only stage win at this Giro.

Stage by stage

1 Sat 6 May · Fossacesia → Ortona · Review Evenepoel wins the Ortona time trial and takes the maglia rosa Remco Evenepoel won the 19.6 km time trial from Fossacesia to Ortona in 21:18, 22 seconds ahead of Filippo Ganna and 29 ahead of João Almeida. He took the maglia rosa, while Primož Roglič lost 43 seconds and Geraint Thomas 55; Tao Geoghegan Hart took the maglia azzurra. 2 Sun 7 May · Teramo → San Salvo · Review Milan wins the hectic finish in San Salvo Jonathan Milan won the hectic finish in San Salvo ahead of David Dekker and Kaden Groves and took the maglia ciclamino. Remco Evenepoel stayed in pink, and Paul Lapeira took the maglia azzurra. 3 Mon 8 May · Vasto → Melfi · Review Matthews claims the uphill sprint in Melfi Michael Matthews won the uphill sprint in Melfi ahead of Mads Pedersen and Kaden Groves. Evenepoel stayed in pink, Primož Roglič moved up to third overall at 44 seconds, and Thibaut Pinot took the maglia azzurra. 4 Tue 9 May · Venosa → Lago Laceno · Review Paret-Peintre wins at Lago Laceno, Leknessund takes pink Aurélien Paret-Peintre won at Lago Laceno two seconds ahead of Andreas Leknessund, with Toms Skujiņš third at 57 seconds. Leknessund took the maglia rosa, 28 seconds ahead of Remco Evenepoel, and also led the young rider classification. 5 Wed 10 May · Atripalda → Salerno · Review Groves wins a crash-marred stage in Salerno On a stage marred by crashes, Kaden Groves won the sprint in Salerno ahead of Jonathan Milan and Mads Pedersen. Andreas Leknessund kept the maglia rosa, with Evenepoel still 28 seconds behind. 6 Thu 11 May · Naples ↻ · Review Pedersen wins in Naples as the break is caught at the last gasp On the stage starting and finishing in Naples the breakaway was caught at the last gasp, and Mads Pedersen won the sprint ahead of Jonathan Milan and Pascal Ackermann. Leknessund stayed in pink. 7 Fri 12 May · Capua → Gran Sasso · Review Davide Bais wins from the breakaway on Campo Imperatore A breakaway trio fought out the 218 km stage to Campo Imperatore on the Gran Sasso, where Davide Bais of Eolo–Kometa beat Karel Vacek by nine seconds and Simone Petilli by 16. Bais took the maglia azzurra, and Leknessund kept the maglia rosa. 8 Sat 13 May · Terni → Fossombrone · Review Healy solos 50 km to his first Grand Tour stage win Ben Healy attacked about 50 km from the finish and won alone in Fossombrone, 1:49 ahead of Derek Gee, Filippo Zana and Warren Barguil, for his first Grand Tour stage win. Behind, Remco Evenepoel lost time to Primož Roglič and Geraint Thomas, while Andreas Leknessund kept pink by eight seconds over him. 9 Sun 14 May · Savignano sul Rubicone → Cesena · Review Evenepoel beats Thomas by one second and retakes pink Remco Evenepoel won the 35 km time trial from Savignano sul Rubicone to Cesena one second ahead of Geraint Thomas and two ahead of Tao Geoghegan Hart, reclaiming the maglia rosa with a lead of 45 seconds over Thomas. Shortly afterwards he tested positive for COVID-19 and withdrew from the race. 10 Tue 16 May · Scandiano → Viareggio · Review Cort wins in Viareggio as Thomas takes over the maglia rosa Nine riders, including Evenepoel, Domenico Pozzovivo, Stefan Küng and Rigoberto Urán, did not start after the rest day, so Geraint Thomas rode in pink, two seconds ahead of Primož Roglič. Magnus Cort beat Derek Gee and Alessandro De Marchi, 51 seconds clear of the bunch, while Aleksandr Vlasov abandoned and Jay Vine crashed and lost more than ten minutes. 11 Wed 17 May · Camaiore → Tortona · Review Ackermann wins photo finish in Tortona, Geoghegan Hart crashes out Four Soudal–Quick-Step riders had tested positive for COVID-19 before the stage. Late on, a crash on a wet descent brought down Thomas, Roglič, Pavel Sivakov and Tao Geoghegan Hart; Geoghegan Hart, third overall, abandoned with a broken hip and Sivakov lost 13 minutes. Pascal Ackermann was awarded the photo-finish sprint ahead of Jonathan Milan and Mark Cavendish. 12 Thu 18 May · Bra → Rivoli · Review Denz wins the breakaway sprint in Rivoli Nico Denz won the breakaway sprint in Rivoli ahead of Toms Skujiņš, with Sebastian Berwick third at three seconds. Geraint Thomas kept the maglia rosa, two seconds ahead of Primož Roglič. 13 Fri 19 May · Le Châble → Crans-Montana · Review Rubio wins the shortened stage to Crans-Montana Because of bad weather, the start was moved to Le Châble in Switzerland and the stage shortened to 74.6 km. Einer Rubio beat Thibaut Pinot by six seconds and Jefferson Alexander Cepeda by twelve at Crans-Montana; Pinot took the maglia azzurra and Thomas stayed in pink. 14 Sat 20 May · Sierre → Cassano Magnago · Review Denz doubles up as Armirail takes the maglia rosa The stage from Sierre in Switzerland went to the breakaway, with Nico Denz taking his second stage win in Cassano Magnago ahead of Derek Gee and Alberto Bettiol. Bruno Armirail took the maglia rosa, 1:41 ahead of Geraint Thomas. 15 Sun 21 May · Seregno → Bergamo · Review McNulty wins the Lombardia-style stage to Bergamo Brandon McNulty won from the breakaway in Bergamo, beating Ben Healy and Marco Frigo in a three-man sprint. Bruno Armirail kept the maglia rosa, now 1:08 ahead of Geraint Thomas. 16 Tue 23 May · Sabbio Chiese → Monte Bondone · Review Almeida outduels Thomas on Monte Bondone, Thomas back in pink João Almeida won atop Monte Bondone ahead of Geraint Thomas on the same time, with Primož Roglič and Eddie Dunbar 25 seconds behind. Thomas took back the maglia rosa, 18 seconds ahead of Almeida and 29 ahead of Roglič, and Ben Healy took the maglia azzurra. 17 Wed 24 May · Pergine Valsugana → Caorle · Review Dainese wins the bunch sprint in Caorle Alberto Dainese won the bunch sprint in Caorle ahead of Jonathan Milan and Michael Matthews. Geraint Thomas kept the maglia rosa, 18 seconds ahead of João Almeida. 18 Thu 25 May · Oderzo → Zoldo Alto · Review Zana beats Pinot at Zoldo Alto Filippo Zana beat Thibaut Pinot at Zoldo Alto, with Warren Barguil third at 50 seconds, and Pinot took back the maglia azzurra. Primož Roglič and Geraint Thomas finished 21 seconds ahead of João Almeida, so Roglič moved up to second overall, 29 seconds behind Thomas. 19 Fri 26 May · Longarone → Tre Cime di Lavaredo · Review Buitrago wins the queen stage to Tre Cime di Lavaredo Santiago Buitrago won the queen stage to Tre Cime di Lavaredo, 51 seconds ahead of Derek Gee. Primož Roglič gained three seconds on Geraint Thomas, cutting his deficit to 26 seconds before the mountain time trial, with João Almeida third at 59. 20 Sat 27 May · Tarvisio → Monte Lussari · Review Roglič wins the Monte Lussari time trial and takes pink Primož Roglič won the 18.6 km mountain time trial from Tarvisio to Monte Lussari, 40 seconds ahead of Geraint Thomas and 42 ahead of João Almeida. He took the maglia rosa with a 14-second lead over Thomas. 21 Sun 28 May · Rome ↻ · Review Cavendish wins in Rome, Roglič wins the Giro Mark Cavendish won the final sprint in Rome ahead of Alex Kirsch and Filippo Fiorelli. Primož Roglič secured overall victory by 14 seconds over Geraint Thomas, becoming the first Slovenian to win the Giro; Jonathan Milan won the maglia ciclamino, Thibaut Pinot the maglia azzurra and João Almeida the maglia bianca.
