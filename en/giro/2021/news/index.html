Stories

The big stories of the Giro and the story of every stage – updated continuously.

Headlines

🏆2021

Bernal wins the Giro, the second Colombian to do so

Egan Bernal won the Giro by 1:29 over Damiano Caruso, with Simon Yates third at 4:15 – his second Grand Tour title after the 2019 Tour de France. He became the second Colombian winner after Nairo Quintana in 2014, held the maglia rosa from stage 9 to Milan and also won the maglia bianca.

🏆Stage 9

Bernal takes pink on the gravel of Campo Felice

Stage 9 finished on gravel roads at Campo Felice. Bernal countered an attack by Aleksandr Vlasov, then dropped Giulio Ciccone and won alone, taking the maglia rosa 15 seconds ahead of Remco Evenepoel.

🌧️Stage 16

Shortened queen stage: Bernal solo over the Giau

Heavy rain and snow forced the organisers to remove the Passo Fedaia and the Passo Pordoi from the queen stage to Cortina d'Ampezzo, making the Giau the Cima Coppi. Bernal attacked on the Giau, crossed the summit alone and won by 27 seconds over Romain Bardet and Damiano Caruso, extending his lead to 2:24, while Remco Evenepoel lost more than 24 minutes.

🏆Stage 20

From domestique to runner-up: Caruso's Giro

Damiano Caruso started the Giro as a domestique for Mikel Landa and became Bahrain Victorious' leader when Landa crashed out on stage 5. The Italian was consistently among the best in the mountains, won stage 20 at Alpe Motta for his first Grand Tour stage win and finished second overall.

⚠️Stage 5

Landa crashes out in the finale at Cattolica

In the finale of stage 5 Joe Dombrowski collided with a marshal at a traffic island and Mikel Landa crashed heavily in the chain reaction. Landa was taken to hospital with a broken collarbone and broken ribs, and Dombrowski (concussion), François Bidard and Pavel Sivakov, who also crashed that day, did not start stage 6.

✨Stage 6

Valter becomes the first Hungarian in the maglia rosa

Attila Valter (Groupama–FDJ) finished 17 seconds behind Egan Bernal's group at San Giacomo and took the maglia rosa, the first Hungarian to wear it in the history of the Giro. He kept it for three days, until he was dropped on the gravel at Campo Felice.

⚠️Stage 17

Evenepoel's comeback ends in a crash

Remco Evenepoel was riding his first race since his crash at Il Lombardia in 2020 and was second overall after stage 9. He lost two minutes on the gravel to Montalcino and more than 24 minutes on the stage to Cortina, and after a crash on stage 17 he did not start stage 18.

⚡2021

Sagan in ciclamino, Bouchard in azzurra

Peter Sagan won stage 10 in Foligno, took the maglia ciclamino and kept it to Milan, finishing with 136 points to Davide Cimolai's 118. Geoffrey Bouchard took the maglia azzurra at Campo Felice and held it to the end by repeatedly taking maximum points on the early climbs.

★Stage 21

Ganna opens and closes the Giro with time trial wins

Filippo Ganna won the 8.6 km opening time trial in Turin, taking the first maglia rosa for the second year running, and, despite a puncture, the closing 30.3 km time trial into Milan – his fifth consecutive time trial win at the Giro. In between he worked for Egan Bernal, including on the gravel to Montalcino.

•Stage 19

Mottarone removed after the cable car disaster

Three days before stage 19 the organisers removed the Mottarone climb out of respect for those affected by the Stresa–Mottarone cable car crash of 23 May. The shortened stage still finished at Alpe di Mera, where Simon Yates won alone.

Stage by stage

1 Sat 8 May · Turin ↻ · Review Ganna wins in Turin and opens in pink again Time trial world champion Filippo Ganna won the 8.6 km opener in Turin in 8:47, 10 seconds faster than Edoardo Affini and 13 faster than Tobias Foss. He took the first maglia rosa for the second year in a row, with João Almeida the best of the favourites in fourth. 2 Sun 9 May · Stupinigi (Nichelino) → Novara · Review Merlier wins the first sprint in Novara On the tenth anniversary of Wouter Weylandt's death, marked by a minute's silence, Tim Merlier launched his sprint 200 metres out and held off Giacomo Nizzolo and Elia Viviani in Novara. Merlier took the maglia ciclamino, Vincenzo Albanese became the first wearer of the maglia azzurra and Filippo Ganna stayed in pink. 3 Mon 10 May · Biella → Canale · Review Van der Hoorn holds off the peloton in Canale Taco van der Hoorn escaped from the break, dropped Simon Pellaud and held off the chasing peloton by four seconds in Canale, ahead of Davide Cimolai and Peter Sagan. It was his first Grand Tour stage win and the first for Intermarché–Wanty–Gobert Matériaux on their Giro debut; Filippo Ganna kept the maglia rosa. 4 Tue 11 May · Piacenza → Sestola · Review Dombrowski wins in Sestola, De Marchi takes pink In persistent rain Joe Dombrowski attacked from a 25-rider break on the Colle Passerino and won alone in Sestola. Alessandro De Marchi finished second at 13 seconds and took the maglia rosa, Dombrowski the maglia azzurra, while among the favourites João Almeida lost more than four minutes. 5 Wed 12 May · Modena → Cattolica · Review Ewan wins in Cattolica, Landa crashes out Caleb Ewan came from behind to beat Giacomo Nizzolo and Elia Viviani in Cattolica, with Nizzolo taking the maglia ciclamino. In a crash-strewn finale Mikel Landa went down heavily after Joe Dombrowski hit a marshal and had to abandon with a broken collarbone and broken ribs; Pavel Sivakov also crashed and later left the race. 6 Thu 13 May · Grotte di Frasassi → Ascoli Piceno (San Giacomo) · Review Mäder wins at San Giacomo, Valter in pink Gino Mäder attacked from the break on the first summit finish and held off Egan Bernal's group by 12 seconds at San Giacomo, also taking the maglia azzurra. Attila Valter took the maglia rosa, the first Hungarian ever to wear it, with Remco Evenepoel 11 seconds and Bernal 16 seconds behind. 7 Fri 14 May · Notaresco → Termoli · Review Ewan's second win, in Termoli Fernando Gaviria made a surprise attack in the final kilometre, but Caleb Ewan closed the gap and came past to win ahead of Davide Cimolai and Tim Merlier. Ewan took the maglia ciclamino; Domenico Pozzovivo had not started after crashes the day before, and Attila Valter kept the maglia rosa. 8 Sat 15 May · Foggia → Guardia Sanframondi · Review Lafay wins solo at Guardia Sanframondi Victor Lafay attacked from the break on the final climb and won alone, his first professional victory and Cofidis' first Giro stage win since 2010. Points leader Caleb Ewan abandoned during the stage, so Tim Merlier took back the maglia ciclamino, while Attila Valter stayed in pink. 9 Sun 16 May · Castel di Sangro → Campo Felice (Rocca di Cambio) · Review Bernal attacks on the gravel and takes pink The stage finished on gravel roads at Campo Felice, where Egan Bernal countered Aleksandr Vlasov, dropped Giulio Ciccone and won by seven seconds to take the maglia rosa and the maglia bianca, 15 seconds ahead of Remco Evenepoel. Geoffrey Bouchard took the maglia azzurra, and Matej Mohorič abandoned after a heavy crash on the descent of the Passo Godi. 10 Mon 17 May · L'Aquila → Foligno · Review Sagan wins in Foligno and takes the maglia ciclamino Bora–Hansgrohe set such a hard pace on the Valico della Somma that Tim Merlier, Giacomo Nizzolo and Dylan Groenewegen were dropped. Peter Sagan launched his sprint from a long way out, held off Fernando Gaviria and Davide Cimolai and took the maglia ciclamino – his first Giro stage win had also come on stage 10, a year earlier. 11 Wed 19 May · Perugia → Montalcino · Review Schmid wins on the gravel to Montalcino On a stage with four gravel sectors, Mauro Schmid beat Alessandro Covi in a two-up sprint for his first professional win. Behind them Egan Bernal attacked to finish ahead of all the favourites, while Remco Evenepoel struggled on the gravel and lost about two minutes; Aleksandr Vlasov rose to second overall at 45 seconds. 12 Thu 20 May · Siena → Bagno di Romagna · Review Vendrame wins in Bagno di Romagna An 18-rider break went clear, with Geoffrey Bouchard extending his lead in the mountains classification. Andrea Vendrame beat Chris Hamilton in a two-up sprint in Bagno di Romagna, while early crashes forced out Marc Soler and former race leader Alessandro De Marchi, who suffered fractures to his collarbone, ribs and vertebrae. 13 Fri 21 May · Ravenna → Verona · Review Nizzolo finally wins a Grand Tour stage Giacomo Nizzolo used Fernando Gaviria's slipstream and came past Edoardo Affini, who had attacked late, to win the flat stage in Verona. It was Nizzolo's first Grand Tour stage win after eleven second places, and Peter Sagan was third to keep the maglia ciclamino. 14 Sat 22 May · Cittadella → Monte Zoncolan · Review Fortunato conquers the Zoncolan Lorenzo Fortunato (Eolo–Kometa) bridged across to Jan Tratnik on Monte Zoncolan, dropped him on the steep section and won by 26 seconds for his first professional victory. Egan Bernal followed Simon Yates' attack and then dropped him in the final kilometre to lead Yates by 1:33, while Remco Evenepoel lost more than a minute and a half. 15 Sun 23 May · Grado → Gorizia · Review Campenaerts wins in Gorizia after a mass crash A mass crash soon after the start forced the race to be neutralised, and Emanuel Buchmann, sixth overall, Natnael Berhane and Jos van Emden abandoned. On a route that also crossed into Slovenia, Victor Campenaerts outsprinted fellow escapee Oscar Riesebeek for his first Grand Tour stage win, with the peloton more than 17 minutes down. 16 Mon 24 May · Sacile → Cortina d'Ampezzo · Review Bernal solo over the Giau to Cortina Bad weather led to the Fedaia and the Pordoi being removed, making the Giau the Cima Coppi. Egan Bernal attacked on the Giau, crossed the summit alone and won in Cortina d'Ampezzo 27 seconds ahead of Romain Bardet and Damiano Caruso, who moved up to second overall at 2:24, while Remco Evenepoel lost more than 24 minutes. 17 Wed 26 May · Canazei → Sega di Ala · Review Dan Martin completes his set at Sega di Ala Dan Martin attacked from the break on the final climb and held on to win at Sega di Ala, completing stage wins in all three Grand Tours. João Almeida and Simon Yates were second and third, while Egan Bernal had his first bad day but still led Damiano Caruso by 2:21; a crash in the peloton involved Remco Evenepoel, who did not start the next day. 18 Thu 27 May · Rovereto → Stradella · Review Bettiol wins the longest stage in Stradella On the longest stage of the race, at 231 km, Alberto Bettiol caught Rémi Cavagna on the final climb, dropped him and won by 17 seconds for his first Grand Tour stage win. Remco Evenepoel and Giulio Ciccone, tenth overall, did not start after crashing the previous day, and the top of the standings stayed unchanged. 19 Fri 28 May · Abbiategrasso → Alpe di Mera (Valsesia) · Review Yates wins at Alpe di Mera With the Mottarone removed out of respect for the victims of the cable car disaster, Simon Yates attacked on the final climb and won alone at Alpe di Mera, 11 seconds ahead of João Almeida. Egan Bernal limited his losses to 28 seconds and led Damiano Caruso by 2:29, with Yates 20 seconds further back in third. 20 Sat 29 May · Verbania → Valle Spluga (Alpe Motta) · Review Caruso wins at Alpe Motta, Bernal stays safe Damiano Caruso joined a Team DSM move on the descent of the San Bernardino with teammate Pello Bilbao, dropped Romain Bardet on the final climb and won alone at Alpe Motta for his first Grand Tour stage win. Egan Bernal, paced by Daniel Martínez, finished 24 seconds down and went into the final time trial 1:59 ahead of Caruso. 21 Sun 30 May · Senago → Milan · Review Ganna wins in Milan, Bernal wins the Giro Filippo Ganna won the 30.3 km time trial from Senago to Milan despite a puncture, 12 seconds ahead of Rémi Cavagna, who crashed into the barriers in the final kilometre. Egan Bernal rode safely to the finish to win the Giro by 1:29 over Damiano Caruso, with Simon Yates third, while Peter Sagan and Geoffrey Bouchard confirmed the maglia ciclamino and the maglia azzurra.
