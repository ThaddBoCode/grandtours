Stories

The big stories of the Giro and the story of every stage – updated continuously.

Headlines

★2024

Pogačar wins his first Giro by 9:56

Tadej Pogačar won the Giro on his first participation, taking six stages and the maglia azzurra of the mountains classification as well. His margin of 9:56 over Daniel Martínez was the biggest since 1965, and his six stage wins put him level with Eddy Merckx (1973) as the post-war overall winner with the most stage victories. Geraint Thomas completed the podium in third.

🏆Stage 2

Puncture, crash, then victory: Pogačar takes pink at Oropa

At the foot of the final climb to the Santuario di Oropa, Pogačar punctured and then crashed, but quickly regained the peloton. He attacked about 4.5 km from the finish, won alone by 27 seconds over Daniel Martínez and Geraint Thomas, and took the maglia rosa, which he kept all the way to Rome.

🏆Stage 15

Queen stage to Livigno: Pogačar reels in Quintana

On the longest stage of the Giro at 222 km, Pogačar attacked alone on the final climb to Mottolino, picked off the breakaway riders one by one and caught Nairo Quintana 2 km from the summit. He won by 29 seconds and stretched his lead to 6:41 over Geraint Thomas.

🔥Stage 14

Ganna takes his time-trial revenge on Lake Garda

In the first time trial to Perugia, Filippo Ganna was still 47 seconds ahead of Pogačar at the second checkpoint but lost by 17 seconds on the closing climb. A week later he turned the tables, winning the time trial to Desenzano del Garda by 29 seconds over the man in pink.

🌧️Stage 16

Bad weather: Umbrail Pass dropped, stage cut to 118.7 km

Stage 16 was due to cross the Umbrail Pass after the rest day in Livigno, but with the descent judged too dangerous in the weather conditions, the first 70 or so km were scrapped and, after chaos at the start, the race began in Laas. Only 118.7 km remained – and Pogačar won his fifth stage on Monte Pana.

⚡2024

Milan takes the maglia ciclamino, Merlier wins in Rome

Jonathan Milan won the sprints in Andora, Francavilla al Mare and Cento, led the points classification from stage 4 onwards and carried the maglia ciclamino to Rome. Tim Merlier also finished with three wins – in Fossano, Padua and on the final stage in Rome, each time ahead of Milan.

🔥Stage 12

Alaphilippe wins in Fano after a long-range attack

Julian Alaphilippe attacked with Mirco Maestri more than 125 km from the finish, dropped him on the last climb and won in Fano – his first Grand Tour stage win since the 2021 Tour de France. It made him a stage winner in all three Grand Tours, and he also took the overall combativity award.

•Stage 1

Narváez beats Pogačar on the opening day in Turin

Pogačar attacked on the final climb as early as stage 1, but Jhonatan Narváez and Maximilian Schachmann went with him, and Narváez won the three-man sprint in Turin. The Ecuadorian thus wore the first maglia rosa of the race before Pogačar took it the next day.

•Stage 11

Uijtdebroeks pulls out, Tiberi wins the maglia bianca

Cian Uijtdebroeks, the leader of the young rider classification, did not start stage 11. Antonio Tiberi took over the lead, defended it all the way to Rome and finished fifth overall, 12:49 down.

Stage by stage

1 Sat 4 May · Venaria Reale → Turin · Review Narváez beats Pogačar to take the first maglia rosa Tadej Pogačar and Jhonatan Narváez went clear on the last climb, with Maximilian Schachmann bridging across at the start of the descent. Narváez won the three-man sprint in Turin ahead of Schachmann and Pogačar to take the maglia rosa and maglia ciclamino; Lilian Calmejane led the mountains classification and Alex Baudin the young rider classification. 2 Sun 5 May · San Francesco al Campo → Santuario di Oropa (Biella) · Review Pogačar solos to victory at Oropa and takes pink Tadej Pogačar punctured and crashed at the foot of the final climb but quickly got back into the peloton. He attacked about 4.5 km from the finish and won alone at the Santuario di Oropa, 27 seconds ahead of Daniel Martínez and Geraint Thomas – his first Giro stage win, making him a stage winner in all three Grand Tours. He took the maglia rosa and the mountains lead, while Cian Uijtdebroeks moved into the young rider lead. 3 Mon 6 May · Novara → Fossano · Review Pogačar and Thomas caught at 300 m, Merlier wins in Fossano Crosswinds split the peloton 58 km from the finish before it came back together. On the last rise just over 3 km from Fossano, race leader Tadej Pogačar and Geraint Thomas went clear but were caught 300 m from the line. Tim Merlier won the sprint ahead of Jonathan Milan and Biniam Girmay and took the lead of the points classification. 4 Tue 7 May · Acqui Terme → Andora · Review Milan sprints to victory in Andora and takes ciclamino After a rain-hit stage, the last breakaway riders were caught on the Capo Mele 5 km from the finish; Filippo Ganna attacked a kilometre later and stayed clear until 500 m to go. Jonathan Milan won the bunch sprint ahead of Kaden Groves and Phil Bauhaus and took the maglia ciclamino; Biniam Girmay abandoned. 5 Wed 8 May · Genoa → Lucca · Review Breakaway survives: Benjamin Thomas wins in Lucca A group of four went clear 76 km from the finish and held a lead of a few seconds over the peloton all the way to the line. Benjamin Thomas won the sprint ahead of Michael Valgren and Andrea Pietrobon, with Enzo Paleni 3 seconds back. Pogačar stayed in pink and Jonathan Milan kept the maglia ciclamino. 6 Thu 9 May · Viareggio → Rapolano Terme · Review Sánchez outsprints Alaphilippe after a gravel attack From a seven-man breakaway, Luke Plapp attacked on the Bagnaia gravel sector 43 km from the finish, and only Pelayo Sánchez and Julian Alaphilippe could follow. The trio held off the peloton, and Sánchez won the sprint in Rapolano Terme ahead of Alaphilippe and Plapp. 7 Fri 10 May · Foligno → Perugia · Review Pogačar overturns Ganna's lead on the climb to Perugia Filippo Ganna was fastest at both checkpoints and still 47 seconds up on Tadej Pogačar at the second. On the closing uphill kilometres to Perugia, Pogačar overturned the deficit and won the 40.6 km time trial by 17 seconds. His lead grew to 2:36 over Daniel Martínez, and Luke Plapp took over the young rider lead. 8 Sat 11 May · Spoleto → Prati di Tivo · Review Pogačar wins the favourites' sprint at Prati di Tivo Valentin Paret-Peintre, the last survivor of the breakaway, was caught by the favourites' group 4.2 km from the finish. Tadej Pogačar attacked with 150 m to go to win ahead of Daniel Martínez and Ben O'Connor, and now led Martínez by 2:40 and Geraint Thomas by 2:58. Cian Uijtdebroeks regained the young rider lead. 9 Sun 12 May · Avezzano → Naples · Review Kooij wins in Naples as Narváez is caught near the line In the finale Jhonatan Narváez attacked from the peloton and passed the earlier attacker Julian Alaphilippe, but was swept up by the sprinters a few metres from the line. Olav Kooij won ahead of Jonathan Milan and Juan Sebastián Molano, for whom race leader Pogačar had led the peloton in the final kilometre. 10 Tue 14 May · Pompei → Cusano Mutri · Review Paret-Peintre passes Tratnik to win at Cusano Mutri Jan Tratnik went clear alone from a 26-rider breakaway and started the 17.9 km final climb to Bocca della Selva with a 50-second lead. Valentin Paret-Peintre caught and dropped him to win by 29 seconds over Romain Bardet, with Tratnik third. Nothing changed at the top of the standings; Olav Kooij did not start. 11 Wed 15 May · Foiano di Val Fortore → Francavilla al Mare · Review Milan wins in Francavilla as Merlier is relegated The three-man breakaway was caught 35 km from the finish, and in the sprint in Francavilla al Mare Jonathan Milan came past Tim Merlier, who was relegated for deviating from his line. That left Kaden Groves second and Giovanni Lonardi third. Cian Uijtdebroeks did not start, and Antonio Tiberi took the young rider lead. 12 Thu 16 May · Martinsicuro → Fano · Review Alaphilippe wins in Fano after a 125 km breakaway Julian Alaphilippe and Mirco Maestri attacked more than 125 km from the finish, and their lead rarely went much beyond two minutes. On the last climb Alaphilippe dropped Maestri and won in Fano, 31 seconds ahead of Jhonatan Narváez – making him a stage winner in all three Grand Tours. 13 Fri 17 May · Riccione → Cento · Review Milan claims his third stage win in Cento On a flat stage with some crosswind splits, the three Italian breakaway riders were caught 53 km from the finish. The peloton stayed together to Cento, where Jonathan Milan, in the maglia ciclamino, won ahead of Stanisław Aniołkowski and Phil Bauhaus. 14 Sat 18 May · Castiglione delle Stiviere → Desenzano del Garda · Review Ganna beats Pogačar in the Desenzano time trial A week after his defeat in Perugia, Filippo Ganna won the 31.2 km time trial to Desenzano del Garda by 29 seconds over Tadej Pogačar, with Thymen Arensman third. Geraint Thomas, fourth on the day, moved up to second overall, 3:41 behind Pogačar. 15 Sun 19 May · Manerba del Garda → Livigno · Review Pogačar catches Quintana to win the queen stage at Livigno On the longest stage at 222 km, Nairo Quintana passed breakaway rider Georg Steinhauser 13 km from the finish just as Tadej Pogačar attacked from the peloton. Pogačar picked off the escapees, dropped Quintana 2 km from the top of Mottolino and won by 29 seconds, with Steinhauser third. Pogačar now led Geraint Thomas by 6:41. 16 Tue 21 May · Laas → Santa Cristina Valgardena · Review Shortened stage: Pogačar takes win number five on Monte Pana Because of the weather the Umbrail Pass was removed and, after a chaotic start, the stage was cut to 118.7 km starting in Laas. Julian Alaphilippe went solo 33 km from the finish but was caught on the final climb; Tadej Pogačar attacked, passed Giulio Pellizzari 800 m from the line and won by 16 seconds over Pellizzari and Daniel Martínez. Martínez moved back into second overall ahead of Geraint Thomas. 17 Wed 22 May · Selva di Val Gardena → Passo del Brocon · Review Steinhauser solos to victory on the Passo del Brocon Georg Steinhauser went clear with Amanuel Ghebreigzabhier, dropped him on the first ascent of the Passo del Brocon 34 km from the finish and pushed his lead past three minutes on the final climb. Tadej Pogačar attacked 2.5 km from the summit and finished second, 1:24 down – it was Steinhauser's first professional win. 18 Thu 23 May · Fiera di Primiero → Padua · Review Merlier takes his second sprint win in Padua Edoardo Affini bridged alone to the four-man breakaway 55 km from the finish, but the move ended 10 km from Padua. Jonathan Milan lost position in the hectic run-in, and Tim Merlier won his second sprint ahead of Milan and Kaden Groves. 19 Fri 24 May · Mortegliano → Sappada · Review Vendrame solos to victory in Sappada From a 19-rider breakaway containing four stage winners of this Giro, Andrea Vendrame attacked on the descent of the Sella Valcalda. He held off Georg Steinhauser and Pelayo Sánchez on the Cima Sappada and won by 54 seconds over Sánchez; the maglia rosa group finished almost 16 minutes down. 20 Sat 25 May · Alpago → Bassano del Grappa · Review Double Monte Grappa: sixth stage win for Pogačar On the second ascent of Monte Grappa, Tadej Pogačar attacked 5 km from the summit and 36 km from the finish, caught breakaway rider Giulio Pellizzari and left him behind. After the long descent he won in Bassano del Grappa by 2:07 over Valentin Paret-Peintre, Daniel Martínez and Antonio Tiberi, taking a 9:56 lead into the final stage. 21 Sun 26 May · Rome ↻ · Review Merlier wins the sprint in Rome, Pogačar wins the Giro A four-man move was caught 13 km from the finish, and in the sprint near the Colosseum Tim Merlier took his third stage win ahead of Jonathan Milan and Kaden Groves. Tadej Pogačar won the Giro and the maglia azzurra, Milan the maglia ciclamino and Antonio Tiberi the maglia bianca.
